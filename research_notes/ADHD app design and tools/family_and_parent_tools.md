# Family and parent tools: trial evidence, what parents say, and one app for an adult and their child

Research date: 14 November 2026. Scope: tools for parents and carers of children with ADHD (about 4 to 17), and for parents who have ADHD themselves. Out of scope by instruction and by the app's own rules: any child medication tracking or dosing content, and any child self-report screening. Nothing below proposes either.

## 1. Behavioural parent training: active components, and blinded versus unblinded effects

### Takeaway
Parent training reliably improves parenting and child conduct problems, and those effects survive probably-blinded measurement (positive parenting SMD 0.63, negative parenting 0.43, conduct problems 0.31 in Daley 2014). Its short-term effect on core ADHD symptoms is seen by unblinded raters (SMD about 0.35 to 0.40) and is close to zero when raters are probably blinded (0.02); at about five months' follow-up Doffer 2023 reports small-to-moderate effects with no masked/unmasked difference. Component analyses point to antecedent techniques and reinforcement as the active ingredients, and to heavier psychoeducation as associated with worse outcomes.

### Cited Findings

Pooled effects, by who rated the outcome

- Sonuga-Barke EJS et al., 2013, *American Journal of Psychiatry* 170(3):275-289. Meta-analyses of 54 RCTs of dietary and psychological treatments. With raters closest to the therapeutic setting, psychological treatments gave SMDs of 0.40 to 0.64, all significant; the authors concluded that "better evidence for efficacy from blinded assessments is required for behavioral interventions, neurofeedback, cognitive training, and restricted elimination diets before they can be supported as treatments for core ADHD symptoms". [A] — [Southampton repository record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/347884/); [King's record](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/nonpharmacological-interventions-for-adhd-systematic-review-and-m/). DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991 [M].
- For behavioural interventions specifically in that meta-analysis: ADHD symptoms d = 0.40 on parent (most proximal) ratings versus d = 0.02 (95% CI -0.30 to 0.34) on probably blinded ratings. [S] — reported in Daley D et al., 2018, "Practitioner review: current best practice in the use of parent training and other behavioural interventions…", *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry* [M for journal details] — [PDF copy](https://www.zentrales-adhs-netz.de/fileadmin/redakteure/zentrales-adhs-netz/Ueber-das-Netz/Grundlagen/Daley_2018_Practicioner_review_Behavioral_interventions.pdf). The 0.02 figure was not found in the 2013 abstract itself.
- Daley D et al., 2014, *JAACAP* 53(8):835-847, PMID 25062591. Behavioural interventions across outcome domains. Most proximal (usually unblinded) raters: positive parenting SMD 0.68, negative parenting 0.57, parenting self-concept 0.37, child ADHD symptoms 0.35, conduct problems 0.26, social skills 0.47, academic performance 0.28. Probably blinded: positive parenting 0.63, negative parenting 0.43, conduct problems 0.31; no significant blinded effect on ADHD symptoms. The authors add that effects "may feed through into a more positive parenting self-concept but not improved parent mental well-being". [A] — [PubMed 25062591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25062591/). The attribution of 0.35 for ADHD to proximal raters is from a guideline summary [S] — [Dutch guideline appendix](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/gerelateerde_documenten/bijlage/18035/1/90/Kwaliteit%20en%20resultaten%20meta-analyse.html). DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2014.05.013 [M].
- Discrepancy to resolve at source: the abstract is reported as 32 studies analysed; the Dutch guideline summary describes 30 RCTs with 2,077 youths. — same two sources as above.
- Groenman AP et al., 2022, *JAACAP* 61(2):144-158, DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2021.02.024. Individual participant data from 21 RCTs, more than 2,200 children under 18. For raters most proximal to treatment, "small- to medium-sized effects" on ADHD, inattention, hyperactivity/impulsivity, ODD and CD symptoms and impairment. Blinded outcomes were available only for small preschool subsamples and limited measures. [A] — [Nottingham repository record](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/5275298).
- Same paper, moderators: conduct disorder symptoms or diagnosis moderated ADHD, hyperactivity/impulsivity, ODD and CD outcomes; single parenthood moderated ODD outcome; ADHD severity moderated impairment. Higher baseline CD or ADHD symptoms, a CD diagnosis and single parenthood "were related to worsening of symptoms in the untreated but not in the treated group, indicating a protective rather than an ameliorative effect". Authors suggest prioritising children with severe ADHD or CD symptoms and those with single parents. [A]/[S] — [repository record](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/5275298); [JAACAP press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/942861).
- Doffer DPA et al., 2023, *JCPP Advances* 3(3):e12196, DOI 10.1002/jcv2.12196, PMID 37720584 (PMC10501699). 27 studies, 31 interventions, 217 effect sizes; mean follow-up about 5.3 months. Significant small-to-moderate between-group effects at follow-up on ADHD symptoms, behavioural problems, positive parenting, parenting sense of competence and parent-child relationship quality. Within-group gains were sustained in all domains with few changes between post-treatment and follow-up. "No differences between masked and unmasked outcomes on ADHD symptoms" at follow-up. Seven predictors of change in child outcomes, including larger ADHD reductions for girls and larger behaviour-problem reductions for younger children. [A] — [PubMed 37720584](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37720584/); [VU repository record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/sustained-improvements-by-behavioural-parent-training-for-childre/). Pooled SMDs are not in the abstract.

Which components do the work

- Dekkers TJ et al., 2022, *JAACAP*, PMID 34224837. Meta-regression of 29 studies, 138 effect sizes, N = 2,345, coding the dosage of 39 behavioural techniques from intervention manuals. Parent training had "robust small- to medium-sized positive effects on all parental outcomes". Higher dosage of techniques that manipulate antecedents was associated with better parenting sense of competence and parental mental health; higher dosage of reinforcement techniques with larger decreases in negative parenting; higher dosage of psychoeducation was negatively related to parental outcomes. The authors call the component findings exploratory; techniques were not tested in isolation. [A] — [PubMed 34224837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224837/); [UvA repository record](https://dare.uva.nl/id/f667acd7-4774-449f-b158-78ddf4da3c7d). DOI 10.1016/j.jaac.2021.06.015 [M]. Numeric SMDs not retrieved.
- A companion meta-regression on **child** outcomes, 2023, *Child and Adolescent Mental Health*, 32 RCTs of parent, teacher or combined training: higher dosage of psychoeducation for parents was associated with smaller effects on behavioural problems and, for parent training only, smaller effects on ADHD symptoms; teaching parents or teachers to use negative consequences was associated with larger effects on behavioural problems; individual rather than group training with larger effects on ADHD and hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms. [A] — [UvA repository record](https://dare.uva.nl/id/0ed6c8e4-7814-4a9b-98fb-698405d601ae). **Author attribution uncertain**: the search summary named Dekkers, van der Veen-Mulders, Hoekstra and van den Hoofdakker; the researcher's memory is that the first author is Hornstra (2023, CAMH 28(2):258-268) [M]. Confirm before citing.
- Hornstra R et al., 2021, *Journal of Clinical Child & Adolescent Psychology*, DOI 10.1080/15374416.2021.1955368. Three-arm randomised microtrial, parents of 92 children aged 4 to 12 with ADHD: two sessions of antecedent-based (stimulus-control) techniques, two sessions of consequent-based (contingency-management) techniques, or waitlist. Primary outcome daily parent-rated problem behaviour. Conclusion: "Brief training of parents in antecedent- or consequent-based techniques improves problem behaviors of children with ADHD. Antecedent-based techniques appear to be especially important to target inattention." [A] — [abstract mirror](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1429917432864817152); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2021.1955368). Effect sizes not retrieved. All outcomes parent-rated, so unblinded.

Guidelines

- NICE NG87 (published 2018, updated 2019): for children under 5, "a group-based parent-training programme as first-line treatment". For ages 5 to 18 the committee recommended additional support that "could be group-based ADHD-focused support and as few as 1 or 2 sessions for parents and carers", and decided **not** to recommend a full parent-training programme combined with medication for all children in this age group because it "did not offer a good balance of benefits and costs". — [NICE NG87 rationale and impact](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG87/chapter/rationale-and-impact).
- AAP 2019 guideline (Wolraich ML et al., *Pediatrics* 2019): for children aged 4 up to the 6th birthday, clinicians "should prescribe evidence-based parent training in behavior management (PTBM) and/or behavioral classroom interventions as the first line of treatment". — [AAP eQIPP key action statements](https://eqipp.aap.org/courses/adhd/mn/clinical-guide/popups/key-action-statements). PTBM "strongly recommended for parents of preschool to middle school children with behavioral concerns, including ADHD". [S] — [AAFP summary, 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0701/p58.html).

2024 to 2026 updates

- Gosling CJ et al., 2025, *BMJ* 391:e085875, DOI 10.1136/bmj-2025-085875. Umbrella review of 115 meta-analyses (221 participant-intervention-comparator-outcome combinations, 31 treatments, searches to 19 January 2025) with an online shared-decision platform (ebiadhd-database.org). Large apparent effects for some non-pharmacological interventions (acupuncture and CBT in children and adolescents, mindfulness in adults) came with low or very low certainty; all findings limited to short-term outcomes. [A]/[S] — [Southampton repository record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/506618/); [News-Medical report, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20251126/Comprehensive-research-identifies-most-effective-ADHD-treatments.aspx). **Its parent-training result was not retrieved.**
- No 2024 to 2026 meta-analysis specific to behavioural parent training and blinded outcomes was found in the searches run.

### Inferences
- The sentence already in `04-family-space.md` ("these tools help the household, and they help behaviour; they are not a treatment for the attention and impulsivity themselves") matches Daley 2014, Sonuga-Barke 2013 and Doffer 2023 as retrieved here. Doffer's "no masked/unmasked difference at follow-up" is the basis for "modest effects appear at longer follow-up", and it rests on an abstract only.
- Antecedent techniques are the best fit for a phone: they are structure that can be built once and shown (routines, a visual schedule, first/then, a clear single instruction). They are also the component tied to parent sense of competence, parental mental health and child inattention. Reinforcement tools (praise prompts, a parent-run chart) map to the reduction in negative parenting.
- The psychoeducation finding argues for short Library entries and against front-loading reading before a parent can use a tool. It is a dose-association in a meta-regression, not a trial of short versus long text.
- Groenman's "protective rather than ameliorative" reading suggests honest wording: these approaches are associated with things not getting worse, particularly in harder circumstances (single parents, more conduct problems), rather than with a reduction in ADHD.
- One tension to put in front of a person rather than resolve here: the 2023 child-outcome meta-regression associates *negative consequences* with larger effects on behaviour problems, while the app's reward chart is positive-only by rule. The finding is about consequences taught inside a clinician-led programme, not about removing points in an app, and changing the chart rule would touch a hard rule.

### Gaps
- Pooled SMDs for Dekkers 2022, Groenman 2022 and Doffer 2023, and all effect sizes for Hornstra 2021, are in tables that could not be opened.
- No retrieval of component-level evidence for **planned ignoring**, **time-out**, **clear instructions/commands** or **labelled praise** individually in ADHD. Leijten et al., 2019 (JAACAP, parenting-programme components for disruptive behaviour) was queued and not searched because the budget ran out.
- Daily report cards are covered under question 3; their place as a "component" of parent training was not separately evidenced.
- Whether Gosling 2025 rates parent training, and at what certainty, is unknown.

## 2. Digital, app-delivered and self-directed parent training (2015 to 2026)

### Takeaway
Online parent programmes show small pooled effects on child behaviour problems (g about 0.3 to 0.4 against waitlist), but ADHD-specific evidence is thin, and the largest ADHD-relevant trial (WASH, 2025, n = 431) found a blinded effect only when self-help was combined with human support. The UK STEPS app trial (OPTIMA) had finished data collection by March 2025 and no outcome paper was found as of November 2026. Self-directed formats see lower satisfaction and attrition of roughly 30 to 50 percent, higher without practitioner support.

### Cited Findings

App and web trials in ADHD or ADHD-symptom samples

- **OPTIMA / STEPS app (UK).** Two-arm RCT of the Structured E-Parenting Support app versus wait-as-usual for families on a waiting list for ADHD assessment; parents randomised 1:1; target 352; STEPS used for 3 months; follow-up at 3, 6, 9 and 12 months; primary outcome parent-rated oppositional and defiant behaviour (SNAP-IV ODD subscale at 3 months per protocol). — [protocol, Kostyrka-Allchorne et al., *Trials* 2022, PMC9744042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9744042/); [ISRCTN16523503](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN16523503).
- OPTIMA status: King's College London update of 21 March 2025 says "data collection for the trial is complete, and we expect the results shortly"; registry lists the trial as completed. **No published outcome paper was found** in standard or extended searches. — [KCL project page](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/the-optima-trial-evaluating-the-clinical-and-cost-effectiveness-of-the-steps-parenting-app).
- STEPS feasibility and usability: 84% of consenting parents agreed hypothetically to take part in the RCT; parents described the app as easy to navigate; System Usability Scale score 94.8 (SD 4.8) out of 100. These are usability measures, not clinical outcomes. [A] — [JMIR Pediatrics and Parenting 2023;6:e47035, PMC10520770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10520770); [process-evaluation protocol, BMJ Open 2024](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/491032/1/e081563.full.pdf).
- **WASH (Germany).** Three-arm RCT: treatment as usual, plus web-assisted self-help, plus web-assisted self-help with telephone support; target 495, enrolled parents of 431 children aged 6 to 12 with elevated externalising behaviour. Authors' conclusion: "Parent-directed WASH is effective in reducing blinded clinician-rated externalizing symptoms, but only when combined with additional support"; one 12-month clinician-rated effect was significant only in a per-protocol subsample (p = .035). Published 2025, *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*. [A] — [Cologne repository record](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/79445/) (returned by the search alongside this summary; not confirmed to be the record for the efficacy paper); [protocol, Döpfner et al., BMC Psychiatry 2020, DOI 10.1186/s12888-020-2481-0](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12888-020-2481-0). DOI and author list of the 2025 paper not retrieved.
- WASH uptake: in 276 participants, 89.4% took up the intervention. [A] — [Child and Adolescent Psychiatry and Mental Health, March 2024](https://doaj.org/article/5434e89091194ff59e65540ff5f19e2c).
- **Triple P Online.** Franke N, Keown LJ, Sanders MR, 2020, *Journal of Attention Disorders* 24(12):1716-1726, DOI 10.1177/1087054716667598. 53 parents of 3- to 4-year-olds with hyperactive/inattentive behaviour (27 immediate, 26 delayed). Intent-to-treat: significant post-intervention improvements in **mother-rated** hyperactivity/inattention, restlessness/impulsivity, defiance/aggression and social functioning, and in parenting measures; at 6 months some effects maintained (maternal over-reactivity and verbosity, parenting satisfaction and self-efficacy, parental stress and depression). Small sample; retrospectively registered. [A] — [Life Course Centre record](https://lifecoursecentre.org.au/publications/an-rct-of-an-online-parenting-program-for-parents-of-preschool-aged-children-with-adhd-symptoms-2/); [ANZCTR entry](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=363496).
- **Online versus face-to-face.** DuPaul GJ et al., 2018 pilot RCT, 47 preschool children at risk for ADHD: both online and face-to-face parent training improved outcomes relative to waitlist; satisfaction was higher in face-to-face. [S] — [CHADD, "Considering online parent training programs"](https://chadd.org/attention-article/considering-online-parent-training-programs/). Journal details not confirmed.
- **Blended self-directed versus therapist-led.** Randomised non-inferiority pilot, 21 referred school-age children with ADHD (blended n = 11, face-to-face n = 10), *Internet Interventions* 2019, PMID 31890615: blended parent training "was not found to be non-inferior" on behaviour problems; parents in the self-directed arm dropped out earlier and were less satisfied; therapists spent significantly less time. [A] — [PMC6926327](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6926327/). First author Breider [M].
- The same paper notes that only one study in the earlier meta-analyses of self-directed parenting programmes was in children with ADHD, and cites an older meta-analysis finding no significant difference between self-directed and therapist-led interventions on child behaviour. [S] — [PMC6926327](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6926327/).

Meta-analyses of online parenting programmes (not ADHD-specific)

- A 2020 meta-analysis in *Clinical Child and Family Psychology Review* (DOI 10.1007/s10567-020-00326-0): online parenting interventions reduce behaviour problems with Hedges's g = 0.40 against waitlist. [A] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10567-020-00326-0). Authors not confirmed in session (Florean et al. [M]).
- A review of 24 RCTs of digital parent training: g = .27 to .33 versus controls; effects stronger when the intervention was evidence-based, combined an interactive platform with a therapist, and was compared with an inactive control. [A] — [AMiner record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/64a50a36d68f896efaafe37f/digital-parent-training-rct-meta-analysis-and-systematic-review). Authors and year not confirmed.
- A 2025 paper in *European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry* summarises: digital parent management training, alone or with therapist support, "has small to moderate effects on child behavior"; studies disagree on whether adding therapist support to self-directed programmes changes outcomes. [S] — [Springer, s00787-025-02799-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00787-025-02799-2).

Dropout and engagement

- Attrition in technology-assisted parenting interventions ranged from 30% to 50% and tended to be higher without practitioner support. [S] — review cited in a study of the self-directed ParentWorks programme, *Digital Health* 2023, [DOI 10.1177/20552076231161963](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076231161963) (found via a proxy mirror; verify at publisher). That study classed parents as non-starters, partial completers and full completers; which predictors were significant was not retrieved.
- Parental ADHD symptoms predict lower between-session adherence (home practice) but not attendance: Friedman LM et al., 2020, *Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology*, DOI 10.1007/s10802-020-00672-1, N = 148 children aged 7 to 11; the association held after controlling for parental anxiety and depression, child severity and sociodemographics. [A] — [PMC7567125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7567125).

### Inferences
- The single clearest moderator in this literature is human support, which an app with no network cannot supply. The honest position is that Adnotia offers the *tools* a parent-training programme teaches, not the programme; no retrieved trial shows that unsupported self-help tools change blinded child outcomes in ADHD.
- That bears on tier wording in the Library (a decision for a person, not for these notes): the techniques have trial evidence when taught by a clinician; this delivery has none.
- Friedman 2020 plus the attrition figures say the realistic failure is not "the parent never opens it" but "the parent opens it and the practice between uses does not happen". Tools that work on the spot, with nothing to remember to do later, sidestep the adherence step that parental ADHD most affects.
- OPTIMA is the closest published analogue to the Family space (a UK parenting app for families waiting for assessment). Its result, when it appears, should be read before any claim about app-delivered parent support is written.

### Gaps
- OPTIMA primary outcome: unpublished or not findable as of 14 November 2026.
- The self-help New Forest Parenting Programme RCT (Daley & O'Brien, 2013) returned nothing in search; not reported here.
- Predictors of dropout specifically in ADHD online programmes: only fragments (support, satisfaction, parental ADHD). A dedicated search was queued and not run.
- A J Clin Psychiatry review of technology-assisted parent training ([PDF](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/18118_technology-assisted-parent-training.pdf)) and a 2026 JMIR paper (e103353) surfaced in results but their content was not retrieved.

## 3. Evidence for specific child-facing aids

### Takeaway
The sleep intervention, organisational skills training and school daily report cards have RCT or meta-analytic support in ADHD. Visual schedules, visual timers and first/then boards have essentially no ADHD trial evidence that these searches could find; their evidence base is single-case research in autism and one small uncontrolled HCI study in ADHD. Reward charts are supported only indirectly, as the reinforcement component of parent training.

### Cited Findings

Routines, visual schedules, timers, first/then

- No systematic review or trial of visual schedules, first/then boards or visual timers **in ADHD** was returned by a targeted search. — search result summary; see Gaps.
- MOBERO: Sonne T, Müller J, Marshall P, Obel C, Grønbæk K, 2016, CHI '16, DOI 10.1145/2858036.2858157. A smartphone system for morning and bedtime routines. Two-week study with 13 children with ADHD: improved child independence and lower parent frustration, and "a 16.5% drop in core ADHD symptoms and an 8.3% improvement in sleep habits" on questionnaires. Uncontrolled, short, parent-reported. At bedtime it prompts the parent to start, then shows a 15-minute countdown. [A] — [Bristol record](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/changing-family-practices-with-assistive-technology-mobero-improv/); [CHI 2016 PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1485796/1/Sonne.CHI.2016.pdf). A follow-up reported fewer conflicts around routines, with recruitment and sleep tracking made harder by running over a summer holiday [S] — [arXiv survey 2005.09993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09993).
- TangiPlan: a tangible system to help children complete morning routines, aimed at executive functioning; mentioned only, no outcomes retrieved. [S] — [Marshall et al. MOBERO paper](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1517313/1/Marshall_MOBERO%20IDC%20v16.pdf).
- Antecedent techniques as a class (which is where schedules and first/then sit) are supported by Hornstra 2021 and Dekkers 2022 above; that is evidence for the class taught to parents, not for any one aid.
- In autism, "visual supports" meet evidence-based-practice criteria in the NCAEP 2020 review with 104 single-case and 2 group-design studies. [S] — [AFIRM visual supports brief, updated 2024](https://afirm-modules.fpg.unc.edu/Visual-Supports/content/assets/raScOE/Visual%20Supports%20Brief%20Packet%2C%20Sam%20%26%20AFIRM%20Team%2C%20Updated%202024.pdf). Knight V, Sartini E, Spriggs AD, 2015, reviewed 31 papers (1993 to 2013) on visual activity schedules in autism. [S] — [PDF copy](https://cbmt.franciscan.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2021/07/Knight-et-al.-2015_Visual-Schedules.pdf). Journal details [M].

Reward charts and token systems

- Higher dosage of reinforcement techniques in parent training is associated with larger reductions in negative parenting (Dekkers 2022), and two sessions of consequent-based techniques improved daily parent-rated problem behaviour versus waitlist (Hornstra 2021). [A] — [PubMed 34224837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224837/); [abstract mirror](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1429917432864817152).
- No trial of an app-delivered or home reward chart as a stand-alone intervention in ADHD was found.

Behavioural sleep intervention

- Hiscock H, Sciberras E, et al., 2015, *BMJ* 350:h68, DOI 10.1136/bmj.h68, PMID 25646809. RCT, 244 children aged 5 to 12 with ADHD, 21 general paediatric practices in Victoria, Australia. Intervention families reported a greater decrease in ADHD symptoms at 3 months (adjusted mean difference -2.9, 95% CI -5.5 to -0.3, P = 0.03, effect size -0.3) and at 6 months (effect size -0.4, P = 0.004; assignment of this second figure to the 6-month point is inferred from the abstract fragment). Teachers reported improved behaviour at 3 and 6 months. Working memory (backwards digit recall) was higher at 6 months, effect size 0.3, in a subset only (n = 54 at 3 months, n = 37 at 6 months). About a half (3 months) to a third (6 months) of the effect on ADHD symptoms was mediated through improved sleep. Authors: the brief intervention "modestly improves the severity of ADHD symptoms". [A] — [PubMed 25646809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25646809/); [QxMD abstract](https://read.qxmd.com/read/25646809/impact-of-a-behavioural-sleep-intervention-on-symptoms-and-sleep-in-children-with-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-and-parental-mental-health-randomised-controlled-trial).
- Format of that intervention: sleep hygiene and standardised behavioural strategies over two fortnightly consultations and a follow-up phone call. [M] — to confirm at [PubMed 25646809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25646809/).
- Sciberras E et al., 2020, *Psychological Medicine*, DOI 10.1017/S0033291718004063: 12-month follow-up of the same trial. "Small sustained improvements in child wellbeing"; benefits for sleep were smaller for children not taking ADHD medication and for children whose parents had depression. [A] — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/sustained-impact-of-a-sleep-intervention-and-moderators-of-treatment-outcome-for-children-with-adhd-a-randomised-controlled-trial/6A5C03D04EDB0D78EC7633240EB51867). Parent-reported sleep problems at 12 months 28.4% versus 46.5%, p = 0.03 [S] — reported by another source in the same search results, probably [PMC9684935](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9684935); the search tool did not say which page the figure came from.
- The medication moderator above is reported as a finding about who benefited; it is not a reason for the app to record or ask about a child's medication.

Organisational skills training

- Abikoff H et al., 2013, *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*: dual-site RCT, 158 children with ADHD, organisational skills training (n = 64), a performance-based contingency intervention without skills teaching, PATHKO (n = 61), or waitlist (n = 33). Design confirmed; effect sizes not retrieved. [S] — [CHOP summary of OTMP interventions](https://chop.edu/sites/default/files/adhd-exec-8-research-based-interventions-for-otmp-child-focused.pdf).
- Langberg JM et al., *JCCP* (2017 online): HOPS and a second brief school-based intervention (CHIEF) versus waitlist in middle-school students; both beat waitlist on parent-rated homework problems and organisational skills with large effect sizes. [A] — [VCU-hosted copy](https://pass.vcu.edu/media/pass/pdfs/2017-52418-001.pdf-correctedByPAVE(1).pdf). CHOP summarises HOPS as large effects by parent report and medium by teacher report. [S] — [CHOP summary](https://chop.edu/sites/default/files/adhd-exec-8-research-based-interventions-for-otmp-child-focused.pdf).
- Bikic A, Reichow B, McCauley SA, Ibrahim K, Sukhodolsky DG (cited as 2016 or 2017, *Clinical Psychology Review*): meta-analysis of 12 RCTs, 1,054 children (576 treatment, 478 control). Moderate effect on teacher-rated and large on parent-rated organisational skills; small to moderate on teacher- and parent-rated inattention; another source gives g = 0.54 to 0.83 for organisational skills. [S] — [author list on QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/shared-collection/8848); [later paper citing the g range](https://psy.fsu.edu/clc/Publications/nihms-1909033.pdf).

School daily report cards

- Group-design meta-analysis, *Journal of Attention Disorders* (online 2017, issue 2020), PMID 29135352: systematic direct observation Hedges' g = 1.05 (95% CI 0.66 to 1.44) with very high heterogeneity; teacher-rated ADHD symptoms g = 0.36 (95% CI 0.12 to 0.60); 272 participants, mean age 7.9; comorbid externalising symptoms 0.34 with a CI crossing zero. [A] — [PubMed 29135352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29135352/). First author Iznardo [M].
- Pyle K, Fabiano GA, 2017, *Exceptional Children*, DOI 10.1177/0014402917706370: meta-analysis of single-case studies, 14 papers, 40 cases; effect sizes "generally illustrated improvement", varying by estimation method. No pooled figure in the abstract. [A] — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0014402917706370).

### Inferences
- The aids the child surface is built from (timer, schedule, first/then) are the ones with the least direct ADHD evidence. Under `02-evidence-rubric.md` that reads as "a straightforward implementation of a technique used inside a Tier A protocol", which is the Tier C definition; the current Family table groups them with routines and praise at Tier A. Whether the Library should distinguish the technique from the specific aid is a tier question, so it is for a person other than the author.
- The sleep routine has the best child-level trial behind it, with teacher-reported (more independent) improvement. What was trialled was a clinician-delivered two-session intervention, not a routine builder.
- Daily report cards have the strongest objective outcome (direct observation) in this set, and the school runs them. The existing design, which explains them and helps a parent ask, fits the evidence.
- Organisational skills evidence is for taught, school-linked programmes for roughly ages 8 to 14. A checklist tool borrows the content, not the evidence.

### Gaps
- Visual timers: only practitioner guidance was found; no outcome study in ADHD or autism was retrieved.
- Morning and bedtime **checklists** as such: no study found beyond MOBERO.
- Abikoff 2013 effect sizes; whether OST gains held at follow-up.
- Fabiano et al., 2010 RCT of daily report cards: not found.
- A community translational trial of the sleep intervention in ADHD (Hiscock and colleagues, later than 2015) was queued and not searched.

## 4. When the parent has ADHD too

### Takeaway
Parental ADHD is common in these families (41% of mothers and 51% of fathers in one German clinical sample), is associated with household chaos, inconsistent discipline and poorer home practice in parent training, and has repeatedly predicted weaker child response. A 2026 trial (TPAC, n = 120) reports that treating the parent's ADHD before parent training produced faster, small-to-moderate improvements in both parent and child severity, where earlier small studies of treating the parent alone had not shown reliable child benefit.

### Cited Findings

How common

- In a German clinical sample, ADHD was found in 41.3% of mothers and 51.0% of fathers of children with ADHD; the paper also gives 41% to 55% of families with an affected child having at least one affected parent. Single clinical sample. [A] — [PMC4780663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780663). Authors Starck, Grünwald and Schlarb, 2016, *Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment* [M].

Parenting and the household

- In 311 mothers, mothers' ADHD symptoms were positively associated with inconsistent discipline and non-supportive responses to children's negative emotions, and "these associations were mediated by home chaos"; fathers' symptoms predicted more inconsistent discipline and low involvement. Cross-sectional, self-report. [A] — [PMC2864040](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864040). Mokrova et al., 2010 [M].
- A 2019 study of 31 families with a child with ADHD and 53 without found that children's ADHD symptoms relate to inadequate parenting through household chaos; the authors argue this supports interventions that improve household organisation. Small, cross-sectional. [A] — [PMC6532773](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6532773). Authors not retrieved.
- Park JL, Hudec KL, Johnston C, 2017, meta-analysis: higher parental ADHD symptoms go with less positive and more harsh and lax parenting; effects "small but robust". [S] — as summarised in [a 2024 Frontiers in Psychiatry paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1321078/full). Effect sizes not retrieved.

Effect on parent-training outcomes

- Sonuga-Barke EJS, Daley D, Thompson M, 2002, *JAACAP*: 83 three-year-olds with ADHD and their mothers, 8-week parent training. "Children of mothers in the high-ADHD group displayed no improvement after PT", while children of mothers with medium or low ADHD symptoms improved substantially; unrelated to other aspects of maternal mental health. [A] — [Southampton record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/18276).
- Chronis-Tuscano A et al., 2011, *Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology*, DOI 10.1007/s10802-011-9518-2: in mothers of 6- to 10-year-olds in brief behavioural parent training, maternal ADHD symptoms predicted post-treatment child disruptive behaviour controlling for baseline, and the relation "was mediated by change in observed maternal negative parenting". Abbreviated programme; authors call for replication. [A] — [PMC3715311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3715311).
- Friedman LM et al., 2020 (details in question 2): parental ADHD symptoms relate to lower between-session adherence, not to attendance. [A] — [PMC7567125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7567125).

Does supporting the parent's own ADHD help the child

- Background stated in a 2020 paper from the same group: parent training "may be less effective when the parent has ADHD", and treating parental ADHD alone had not reliably improved parenting or child outcomes. [S] — [Chronis-Tuscano et al., *J Clin Psychiatry* 2020, "Treating ADHD in parents and their children"](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/18284_treating-adhd-in-parents-and-their-children.pdf). That study randomised 35 mother-child dyads to 8 weeks of maternal stimulant treatment or behavioural parent training and measured maternal symptoms, parenting and child outcomes; its results were not retrieved.
- Lui JHL, Chronis-Tuscano A, et al., 2025, *J Clin Psychiatry*: pilot sequential (SMART) trial in families where mother and child both have ADHD; authors say results "require replication with a fully powered SMART design" and favour combining treatment of the mother's ADHD with parent training for most such families. [A] — [PDF](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/sequencing-stimulant-medication-behavioral-parent-training-multiplex-ADHD-families-pilot-SMART-trial-24m15463.pdf).
- **TPAC**, Chronis-Tuscano A et al., 2026, *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*, DOI 10.1111/jcpp.70222. Hybrid effectiveness-implementation trial by telehealth, 120 parents with DSM-5 ADHD and their 3- to 8-year-old, stimulant-naive children. Treating the parent's ADHD before parent training gave faster, small-to-moderate improvements in parent and child clinical severity and modest gains in parenting consistency and positivity. Child clinician-rated severity (CGI-S) went from 4.52 to 3.67 over nine months when the parent's ADHD was treated first, versus 4.31 to 3.96 with parent training alone; parents also reported more positive parenting and less inconsistent discipline. Parent training itself improved several parenting behaviours in both arms. [S] — [Psychiatric News alert](https://alert.psychnews.org/treating-parental-adhd-can-help-children-with-adhd); [University of Maryland summary](https://bsos.umd.edu/featured-content/evaluating-treatment-parents-and-children-affected-adhd); [trial protocol, PMC11947710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11947710). Full text, primary analysis and limitations not seen.
- Chronis-Tuscano A et al., 2013: randomised mothers of children with ADHD to an integrated parenting intervention that also targeted maternal depressive symptoms, or standard parent training. Results not retrieved. [S] — [University of Maryland copy](https://bsosdev1.umd.edu/sites/psyc.umd.edu/files/pubs/ChronisTuscano%20et%20al%202013%20IPIA.pdf).

What parents with ADHD say they need (lived experience, not trial evidence)

- Consistency is the named difficulty: "children thrive on consistency, but consistency can be tough for a parent with ADHD", with bedtimes and rules falling apart at follow-through; also lower frustration tolerance and drifting during conversations. [LE] — [ADDA, "Parenting challenges with ADHD"](https://add.org/parenting-challenges-with-adhd/); [Simply Psychology overview](https://www.simplypsychology.org/adhd-parenting.html).
- One parent with ADHD writes that consistency is "my kryptonite" and argues that consistency in connection matters more than rigid routine. [LE] — [personal Substack](https://staceysx4.substack.com/p/consistency-is-my-kryptonite).
- Parents with ADHD asking other adults with ADHD how to follow through on parenting strategies. [LE] — [HealthUnlocked adult ADHD forum thread](https://healthunlocked.com/adult-adhd/posts/148665065/how-do-i-follow-through-looking-for-parenting-strategies).
- Advice repeated across these sources: a few simple, visible, posted routines so nobody relies on memory or verbal reminders; no perfectionism; and treatment or support for the parent's own ADHD as part of supporting the child. [LE] — [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/adhd-parenting.html); [Talkspace](https://www.talkspace.com/mental-health/conditions/articles/parenting-a-child-with-adhd/).

### Inferences
- An app that serves the adult's own ADHD and the child's routines is supported by the direction of this evidence: home organisation is the mediator between parental ADHD and inconsistent discipline, and TPAC suggests the parent's own ADHD being addressed first speeds the family's gains. TPAC's arm involved the parent's own clinical treatment, which the app neither delivers nor advises on; the transferable point is only that the parent's own functioning comes first.
- Design for the parent who will not be consistent. Tools that still work after a missed week, with no streak and nothing to catch up on, fit both Friedman 2020 and the lived-experience reports. This is already the app's rule on guilt mechanics; here it has an evidence-based reason as well as an ethical one.
- "Visible, posted" routines argue for the printed and handed-over forms of a schedule over anything that needs the parent to remember to open the app.
- The Family space Library could say plainly that a parent having ADHD is common and is associated with finding these techniques harder to keep up, without implying anything about a particular parent. Wording for that is a "stop and ask" item.

### Gaps
- The 41% and 51% prevalence figures come from one clinical sample; no meta-analytic estimate was retrieved, and an "eightfold risk" figure seen in a blog was not traced to a source.
- TPAC: effect sizes, primary outcome analysis and limitations are unread. This is the most important single paper to read at source for this question.
- No ADDitude or CHADD first-person pieces were returned; lived-experience material here is thin and mostly clinic blogs. Child Mind Institute's "When parent and child both have ADHD" (2024) surfaced as a reference only.
- No study was found testing adapted parent training for parents with ADHD (shorter sessions, reminders, simplified homework) with child outcomes.

### Inferences
- The top complaints (subscriptions, billing after cancelling, sync failures, a paid device) are things Adnotia does not have. That is worth saying on the welcome page as fact, not as comparison.
- Novelty fading at four to eight weeks is a property of game mechanics. A parent-run chart with no pet, no streak and no app-initiated reward cannot fade in that way, but it also has no pull of its own; its durability depends entirely on the parent, which is where parental ADHD bites.
- The reported fixes line up with the trial evidence on immediacy: few goals, reward close to the behaviour, easy to re-set. A chart that can be awarded in one tap at the moment, and re-themed or restarted without any record of the gap, follows from both.
- A tension for a person to decide: "parent forgets to award" is the commonest reported failure, and the obvious fix (a prompt after a routine ends) would be the app reminding about points on its own initiative, which `02-evidence-rubric.md` rules out in so many words. Any prompt placed inside a parent-started flow would need an ADR arguing why it is not that.
- Several children under one parent holder is praised wherever it exists. Per-child profiles already do this; nothing was found for or against showing siblings side by side, and the Family space already excludes comparing children.

### Gaps
- No retrieved evidence, trial or anecdotal, on a child gaming a reward app (marking tasks done), sibling comparison, or rewards turning into bribes. A search on exactly these was queued and not run. The request names them as known failure modes; these notes cannot source them.
- No Reddit threads were returned by any query; the forum material is from HealthUnlocked.

- Review-site content (NeuroLaunch, Choosing Therapy, Marlvel, JustUseApp) is aggregated or affiliate-style and should be treated as directional. For Joon, Brili and Goally the search tool returned quotations grouped by app, not by page, so the page given beside each quotation is the most likely of the pages returned and needs checking before any quotation is reused.

## 6. Adolescents, 12 to 17: handing over autonomy

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced adolescent approaches are skills-based and done with the teen rather than to them: organisational skills programmes (HOPS) and a parent-teen collaborative model with motivational interviewing (STAND). STAND worked in university trials, showed no overall advantage over usual care in a community trial of 278, and its parent-teen contracting gains were not maintained at six months. Guidelines favour training interventions for this age group over classic parent-run contingency programmes.

### Cited Findings
- AAP 2019: training interventions "focused on organization, time management, and interpersonal skills" work best for middle and high school students; studies "describe some value" in parent and teacher training in behaviour management for high school students; the guideline covers ages 4 to 18 with special attention to preschoolers and teens. [S] — [AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0701/p58.html); [AAP news release](https://www.aap.org/en/news-room/news-releases/aap/2019/aap-updates-guidelines-on-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-with-latest-research/).
- NICE NG87, ages 5 to 18: group-based ADHD-focused support for parents and carers, which may be as few as one or two sessions, rather than a full parent-training programme for all. — [NICE NG87 rationale and impact](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG87/chapter/rationale-and-impact).
- STAND (Supporting Teens' Autonomy Daily), Sibley MH et al. Earlier university-based efficacy trial: significant acute effects on ADHD symptoms, organisation, time management and planning skills, homework behaviour, parent-teen contracting, implementation of home privileges, parenting stress and daily homework recording; at six months some gains held, but parents' use of contracting and privileges and teens' daily homework recording and homework behaviour gains "were not maintained". [A] — [FIU lab copy, 2016 paper](https://self-regulationlab.fiu.edu/publications-and-presentations/2016-18388-001.pdf).
- STAND community trial: N = 278 adolescents in four community clinics, adolescents and therapists both randomised to STAND or usual care (NCT02694939). "Primary outcome analyses suggested no significant differences between groups"; efficacy "was reduced in the community compared to university settings". [A] — [FIU lab copy](https://self-regulationlab.fiu.edu/publications-and-presentations/nihms-1645154.pdf); [trial record](https://trials.co/clinical-trials-details/-/NCT02694939). Which lab-hosted PDF belongs to which STAND paper is inferred from the file names here and in the two neighbouring bullets; confirm before citing.
- STAND three-year follow-up: no long-term group-by-time effects in intent-to-treat analyses; group-by-licensure interactions indicated long-term effects on ADHD symptoms, organisation skills and parent-teen conflict when the clinician was licensed. [A] — [FIU lab copy](https://self-regulationlab.fiu.edu/publications-and-presentations/1-s2.0-s0890856724019488-2.pdf); [ACAMH summary](https://www.acamh.org/blog/adhd-stand-trial/).
- HOPS for middle-school students and the Bikic meta-analysis: see question 3. Parent-rated effects large, teacher-rated medium. [A]/[S] — [VCU copy](https://pass.vcu.edu/media/pass/pdfs/2017-52418-001.pdf-correctedByPAVE(1).pdf); [CHOP summary](https://chop.edu/sites/default/files/adhd-exec-8-research-based-interventions-for-otmp-child-focused.pdf).
- HCI evidence on shared tools: in a study of a smartwatch-and-phone system for children with ADHD and their parents (CHI 2023, "Unpacking the lived experiences of smartwatch mediated self and co-regulation with ADHD children"), the technology gave pervasive regulation support "but the division across devices and parent-child roles interfere with developing independence". [A] — records at [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10540812) and [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/86x7t804); which record is which paper was not confirmed. Participants were children, not specifically adolescents.
- One popular source says research suggests that giving children some executive control over their routines can improve ADHD symptoms and executive functioning. [S, unverified] — [Talkspace](https://www.talkspace.com/mental-health/conditions/articles/parenting-a-child-with-adhd/). The underlying study was not identified.

### Inferences
- For 12 to 17 the evidence favours a tool the teen helps set up and then runs (their own materials, homework and time plan), with the parent agreeing terms rather than issuing them. In app terms that is a routine or plan built together and then handed over in a mode that is less of a kiosk than the one for a six-year-old.
- STAND's six-month fade in contracting says agreements between parent and teen decay. Making an agreement quick to revisit, without a record of having lapsed, is the design response.
- The CHI finding warns against a split where the parent holds the controls and the teen holds a display: it preserved dependence. That is a point in favour of the open question in `04-family-space.md` about adolescents having more autonomy, and it does not settle it.
- Anything a teen records about themselves runs into the open question about a child's own data, and must not become self-report screening. A teen ticking off their own plan is tool use; a teen rating their own symptoms is not on the table.

### Gaps
- NICE NG87's wording on involving children and young people in discussions and decisions was not retrieved (search not run).
- AAP 2019 wording on adolescent assent was not retrieved.
- STAND effect sizes not retrieved.
- No trial of a teen-facing planning app in ADHD was found, and nothing on the age at which handing over works.

## 7. Design patterns for one app serving an adult's own tasks and their children's routines

### Takeaway
Existing products use one of three models: a parent mode behind a code on a shared phone (Brili), a parent account with a separate child experience on either device (Joon), or a dedicated locked child device managed from a parent app (Goally). Research prototypes add two warnings: splitting roles across devices can hold back independence, and families want to look at things together at a deliberate time. No product or study was found that interleaves a parent's own routine with a child's, or that pairs an adult's own ADHD tools with a child's in one app.

### Cited Findings
- **Parent mode behind a code, several children, one holder.** Brili: "All kids can be managed under one parent account accessed through a user-set 4-digit code"; parents build routines in a separate Parent Mode; the account can link to unlimited devices. [LE, vendor] — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brili-routines-visual-time-r/id952895165); [Common Sense Media review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/brili-routines).
- **Parent sets up, child gets a gated experience.** Joon: the parent creates the family and assigns tasks; the child gets an account to see them and can use their own device or share the parent's phone; the game is gated on completing assigned tasks. [LE, vendor] — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1482225056).
- **Dedicated child device plus parent app plus carers.** Goally: a parent-managed tablet that restricts YouTube, browsers and other app stores; a separate parent app to build routines, reminders and checklists; a "care team" app for other caregivers. [LE, vendor] — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goally-adults/id1262461227); [Marlvel profile](https://marlvel.ai/apps/goally).
- **Household model.** Cozi is organised around a shared family calendar and shared lists rather than individual accounts' data; reported weaknesses are sync failures and duplicate list items. [LE] — [Morgen comparison](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/digital-family-calendar). OurHome assigns chores to family members with points. [LE] — [Quicken roundup](https://www.quicken.com/blog/?p=9664).
- **Parent prompted first, then the child's timer.** MOBERO's bedtime module prompts the parent to begin and then runs a 15-minute countdown; it has separate morning and bedtime modules. [A] — [Marshall et al.](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1517313/1/Marshall_MOBERO%20IDC%20v16.pdf); [Sonne et al., CHI 2016](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1485796/1/Sonne.CHI.2016.pdf).
- **Cues for the parent, in the moment.** ParentGuardian (Pina L et al., 2014, PervasiveHealth, DOI 10.4108/icst.pervasivehealth.2014.254958) detected parental stress from a wrist sensor and showed parent-training strategy reminders on a phone and a glanceable display. [A] — [EUDL record](https://eudl.eu/doi/10.4108/icst.pervasivehealth.2014.254958); [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=301559). It depends on sensing and is cited for the idea of a strategy card at the moment of need, not for the sensing.
- **Split roles can impede independence.** CHI 2023 smartwatch study: see question 6. [A] — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10540812).
- **Families want a deliberate time to look together.** "Co-designing situated displays for family co-regulation with ADHD children" (2024; co-design sessions with families, n = 23 participants): families wanted prompts "to establish intentional family time for reviewing data together". [A] — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/86x7t804). Authors include Silva and Cibrian per the search summary; not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Adnotia's existing shape (per-child profiles, a parent gate, a handed-over surface) is the Brili model with a stronger wall. It is the only one of the three models that needs no account, no second device and no network, so it is the only one available under the hard rules.
- A "people" or household model, in which the adult's own routine and each child's sit side by side, is not evidenced by any product or study found, and it cuts across a written boundary: `03-scope.md` and `04-family-space.md` say the two spaces "share the kernel and nothing else". Interleaving a parent's morning with a child's would need an ADR. The evidence in question 4 (home organisation as the mediator; the parent's own functioning first) is an argument for making the adult's tools easy to reach from the Family space, which is a smaller change than merging data.
- If the two are ever shown together, the privacy line is one-directional: a child on the handed-over surface must see nothing of the adult's own record. The reward chart, schedule and first/then are the only things that cross to the child today, and they are parent-authored.
- The "intentional family time" finding suggests a parent-started "look at the chart together" moment as an alternative to any reminder. It is parent-initiated, so it does not conflict with the chart rules as written.
- From the researcher's prior knowledge, not sourced in this session: a web app cannot lock a phone. iOS Guided Access and Android app pinning are operating-system features a parent can switch on to hold a handed-over phone in one app. If the Library mentions them, the wording needs checking against Apple's and Google's current help pages.

## 8. Autism overlap, for a possible later extension

### Takeaway
Visual supports and schedules have a much deeper evidence base in autism (single-case research) than in ADHD, parent training has a large RCT for disruptive behaviour in young autistic children, and a brief behavioural sleep intervention has trial evidence that fades by 12 months. Where recommendations *differ* between autism and ADHD could not be sourced in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Visual supports are an evidence-based practice in the NCAEP 2020 review: 104 single-case and 2 group-design studies; applicable from early childhood to young adulthood. The category is broader than schedules alone. [S] — [AFIRM brief, updated 2024](https://afirm-modules.fpg.unc.edu/Visual-Supports/content/assets/raScOE/Visual%20Supports%20Brief%20Packet%2C%20Sam%20%26%20AFIRM%20Team%2C%20Updated%202024.pdf); [California CAPTAIN training module, 2022](https://captain.ca.gov/documents/CAPTAIN%20Visual%20Supports%20Module%20for%20Regional%20Center-June%201%202022%20Update.pdf), which lists individual, group and first/then boards as the main schedule types.
- Knight, Sartini and Spriggs, 2015: review of 31 papers on visual activity schedules in autism, 1993 to 2013; secondary summaries describe schedules as evidence-based when paired with systematic instruction. [S] — [PDF copy](https://cbmt.franciscan.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2021/07/Knight-et-al.-2015_Visual-Schedules.pdf).
- Parent-run visual schedules at home: Glover, Lloyd, Barton and Mello, 2018, a multiple-baseline study across routines in two child-mother dyads, examining independent schedule use and transitions. Two dyads only. [A] — [Vanderbilt lab record](https://lab.vanderbilt.edu/lloyd-lab/publications/effects-of-parent-implemented-visual-schedule-routines-for-african-american-children-with-asd-in-low-income-home-settings).
- Parent training: Bearss K et al., 2015, *JAMA* 313(15):1524-1533, DOI 10.1001/jama.2015.3150. 180 children aged 3 to 7 with autism and disruptive behaviour, 24 weeks of parent training (n = 89) versus parent education (n = 91), six centres. Parent-rated irritability fell about 48% versus 32% (standardised effect size 0.62; treatment effect -3.9, 95% CI -6.2 to -1.7); Home Situations Questionnaire fell 55% versus 34.2% (effect size 0.45; -0.7, 95% CI -1.1 to -0.3); neither met the prespecified minimal clinically important difference; a blinded clinician rated 68.5% versus 39.6% as improved. [A, from a text extract of the paper] — [University of Minnesota copy](https://reachfamilies.umn.edu/sites/default/files/rdoc/Bearss_2015_0.pdf); [ASAT synopsis](https://asatonline.org/research-treatment/research-synopses/effect-parent-training-vs-parent-education/).
- Sleep: 12-month follow-up of the Sleeping Sound intervention in autistic children, *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*, DOI 10.1007/s10803-022-05809-3: effect sizes fell over time, from g = 0.64 at 3 months to 0.25 at 12 months. [A] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-022-05809-3). First author not confirmed.
- Token economy taught to caregivers remotely: Davis et al., multiple-baseline across two participants, for routine non-preferred activities; no child-behaviour data collected. [A] — [PMC7942799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7942799).

### Inferences
- The child-surface aids (schedule, first/then, timer) would arrive in an autism extension with *more* direct evidence than they have for ADHD, which reverses the usual direction of borrowing. Under the rubric, tiers are assessed within the population, so an autism tier would be assigned separately and could differ.
- The sleep routine is the aid with trial evidence in both groups.
- Bearss 2015 used a blinded clinician rating and an active comparison, a stronger design than most ADHD parent-training trials, and still fell short of its own clinical-importance threshold. The same care in wording would apply.

The combined query returned no relevant sources and the follow-up was not run.

## 9. Regulatory and store-policy points specific to children

### Takeaway
For an app that collects nothing and is aimed at parents, the data-protection regimes mostly have nothing to attach to: COPPA regulates operators that collect children's personal information, and the UK Children's Code applies to services that process personal data and are likely to be accessed by children. Store rules matter only if the app is listed in a store, and there the choices are whether to enter Apple's Kids Category (probably not, for a parent-facing app) and how to declare the target audience on Google Play. US state app-store age laws are a moving target in 2026. None of this is legal advice and most of it comes from secondary sources.

### Cited Findings

Apple

- Since June 2019, Guidelines 1.3 and 5.1.4 restrict Kids Category apps: originally they "may not include third-party advertising or analytics"; later softened so that third-party analytics may be permitted in limited cases, provided the services do not collect or transmit the IDFA or any identifiable information about children. Kids Category apps may not send personally identifiable or device information to third parties. [S] — [Apple developer news, 3 June 2019](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=06032019j); [Michael Tsai's summary of the 2019 changes](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2019/06/04/updates-to-the-app-store-review-guidelines/); [third-party reproduction of the current clause](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/kids-category-third-party-data-and-advertising-restrictions/). The live guideline text was not read.
- Apple's Declared Age Range API "remains available worldwide for users on iOS 26, iPadOS 26, and macOS 26, or later". [S] — [9to5Mac, 23 Dec 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/23/texas-judge-blocks-state-app-store-age-verification-law/).

Google Play

- Developers must declare target age groups in the Play Console; Google can review whether the declaration is accurate; imagery and terms that could be considered targeting children can affect that assessment. If children are not in the target audience the app "should not unintentionally appeal to them". Apps for a mixed audience need a neutral age screen; apps solely targeting children must not transmit the advertising identifier and certain other device identifiers. — [Google Play Families policies help article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en); [Android Developers blog, 2019](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2019/05/building-safer-google-play-for-kids.html?hl=en).
- A Families policy expansion prohibits anonymous chat apps from targeting children, effective 26 August 2026. Not relevant to an app with no network; noted as the most recent dated change found. — [Google Play policy update](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17122218?authuser=4).

UK Children's Code

- The Code applies to information society services that process personal data and are likely to be accessed by under-18s, "even if it's not aimed at them"; "likely" means more probable than not, with no numerical threshold; the assessment looks at appeal to children and how the service is accessed, and is continuing. — [ICO, Age appropriate design code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-data-protection/ico-codes-of-practice/age-appropriate-design-code); [Inside Privacy on the ICO's "likely to be accessed" guidance](https://www.insideprivacy.com/childrens-privacy/uk-ico-provides-guidance-on-when-a-service-is-likely-to-be-accessed-by-children-and-needs-to-comply-with-its-age-appropriate-design-code/); [Taylor Wessing, Feb 2024](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/global-data-hub/2024/february---childrens-data/likely-to-be-accessed-by-children).

United States

- Amended COPPA Rule: published in the Federal Register 22 April 2025, effective 23 June 2025, compliance date 22 April 2026 (with exceptions for some safe-harbour provisions). It expands the factors for "directed to children" to include marketing or promotional materials and plans, and adds a definition of a "mixed audience" site or service. — [Federal Register notice, 90 FR 76](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FTC-2024-0003-0281/content.htm); [Hunton summary](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments); [Davis Polk update](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect).
- In February 2026 the FTC issued a policy statement limiting enforcement where children's data is collected solely for age verification. [S] — [Davis Polk update](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect).
- Texas App Store Accountability Act (SB 2420): enjoined by a federal district court on 23 December 2025 before its 1 January 2026 effective date; the injunction was later lifted on appeal, and in July 2026 the US Supreme Court declined to block the law, so Texas can enforce it. It requires app stores to verify users' ages and pass an age category and parental-consent status to developers, and developers to assign an age rating to each app with a description of what informed it. Similar laws are enacted in Utah, Louisiana and Alabama. [S] — [Route Fifty, July 2026](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2026/07/us-supreme-court-declines-request-block-texas-app-age-verification-law/414615/); [9to5Mac, Dec 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/23/texas-judge-blocks-state-app-store-age-verification-law/); [Troutman privacy blog](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/?p=30307). Sources conflict on timing; Utah's current status was not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Adnotia is a browser app and a single file, not a store listing. Apple's and Google's rules, and the state app-store laws, apply only if it is ever wrapped and listed. Nothing here is a reason to do that.
- If it were listed: it is addressed to parents and the child surface is reached only through a parent, so declaring an adult audience and staying out of Apple's Kids Category looks like the fit, and the absence of any analytics, advertising or identifier makes the data rules moot either way. Whether a handed-over child mode changes a store's view of the audience is exactly the kind of question the stores decide case by case; it is for a qualified person.

With nothing collected there is still no operator in the rule's sense, which is what `03-scope.md` already concludes; the point is only that the welcome page's wording and pictures are part of how "directed to children" is judged.
