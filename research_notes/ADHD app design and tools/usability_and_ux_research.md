## 1. What do HCI, co-design and usability studies with adults with ADHD say they want from apps, and what makes them abandon apps?

### Takeaway
The adult HCI literature is small, recent, qualitative and at concept stage. It says task management is socially and emotionally scaffolded, that no single model fits, that setup and clutter are named frustrations, and that people past diagnosis want help in the moment. There is still no longitudinal study of app abandonment in adults with ADHD; the nearest data are one trial's dropout curve and a few participant quotes.

### Cited Findings
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text, arXiv v2)* 22 interviews with ADHD-identifying adults, then speed-dating of 13 speculative AI concepts with 20 more. About a third had no formal diagnosis. "Task management emerges as a socially and emotionally scaffolded process, in which participants rely on body doubling, peer accountability, adaptive tools, and affective support to initiate and sustain action." "Adults with ADHD do not benefit from a uniform model of task-management support." Participants criticised tools for "requiring too many steps, enforcing rigid structures, or presenting cluttered interfaces"; P4: "Many apps are too complicated to set up, and I feel more anxious using them." P11: "If it's only me reminding myself, it just becomes invisible after a few days." Best received concepts: a "brain weather" state display, an AI body double, pause days, and "flexible planning and gentle streaks" (ideal and baseline goals; streak not reset). Resisted: shared planning seen as surveillance, and an emotional check before commitments seen as friction. "Participants emphasized user control"; "I want it to suggest, not decide." Limits stated by the authors: sample skews to introspective tool users; concepts never built. Chen, Meng and Nie, 2026, accepted to CSCW 2026. Most concepts **need a server**. — [arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)
- **[ADHD-adult study, students only]** *(verified: full text, arXiv)* Co-design with 20 diagnosed undergraduates aged 19–24 (all Chinese, 17 in mainland China) and five experts. Wants: task information pulled together from scattered sources; help estimating time; editable prioritisation; tasks broken into small steps, with one participant asking to limit "the number of subtasks shown at once"; a view of progress; and control kept ("did not want GenAI to fully take over"). Participants wanted reminders that are "interesting, constantly changing"; their own attempts to vary alarm sounds "still failed to engage them over time". Experts supported specific positive feedback and warned that penalty-based feedback "may trigger negative emotions"; one expert warned that showing others' progress risks social comparison. Zhu, Yu and Luo, CHI '26. Generative AI parts **need a server**. — [arXiv 2602.09381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text)* The Irish Adult ADHD App: analytics plus 14 interviews (ages 26–65). Over 18,000 downloads November 2022 to October 2023; about 12,000 active users; about 6,400 returned (about 50%). Average 2.4 engaged sessions per user, 4 min 55 s on first use, 8 min 58 s in total; "engagement peaked in the first two weeks after download". The app is 37 static pages. "The primary limitation was navigating the app": "not knowing where to start and becoming overwhelmed at the number of pages". Adults new to ADHD benefited most; those with more experience "seemed to want an app that would help them in moments of challenge", and often named the under-three-minute mindfulness breaks. Some interviewees suggested unlocking content in stages, "rewards, badges or streaks", and reminders. Only averaged, rounded analytics were available. Seery, Cochrane, Mulcahy, Kilbride, Wrigley and Bramham, 2025, Internet Interventions 39:100807. Analytics **need a server**. — [PMC11847728](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11847728/)
- **[ADHD-adult study, neurodivergent sample]** *(unverified; ACM refused)* Survey of 220 people on body doubling. The n = 220 is confirmed second-hand by the VR paper below; the breakdown (193 neurodivergent, uses, 186 more likely to finish) is not. Eagle, Baltaxe-Admony and Ringland, 2024, TACCESS 17(3). **Needs a server** when done with another person. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3689648)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text, arXiv)* "No studies to date have clearly demonstrated the effectiveness of body doubling, and existing findings remain inconclusive." The same paper then reports its own within-subject VR bricklaying test with 12 adults with ADHD: efficiency was higher with a human or an AI body double than alone (F(2,22) = 6.51, p = .006), with no difference between the two; measured accuracy did not differ. Ara et al., 2025. — [arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12153)
- **[ADHD and autism, adult study, very small]** *(verified: full text, arXiv)* "Although self-tracking is often framed as enabling self-insight, this is rarely our experience as neurodivergent individuals and researchers." Six participants (aged 28–31) made visual representations of masking; three then self-tracked for two weeks. One stopped after a week "because he felt he was not gaining meaningful insights"; one stopped when an internship began; one found "remembering to log in the evenings was challenging" and blamed themselves, not the method. Tracking systems "often presume capacities that many neurodivergent individuals struggle to sustain, including... maintaining stable routines". Rudberg Selin, Unéus and Knudsen, CHI 2026. — [arXiv 2603.22609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22609)
- **[Child or mixed-age]** *(verified: abstract)* After 10 weeks of remote monitoring, 10 adolescents and adults with ADHD and 12 comparison participants were interviewed. Those with ADHD "described the impact that their ADHD symptoms had on participating", the perceived cost of the cognitive tasks, and more technical challenges. Denyer et al., 2023, JMIR Formative Research 7:e44126. **Needs a server.** — [PubMed 37389932](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37389932/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text)* 162 adults with ADHD or high traits wrote about criticism. Sub-themes of what is criticised: organisation, time management, forgetfulness, focus and inattention. Criticism had "negative consequences for self-worth and wellbeing". Shame is not a named theme; it appears once in the discussion through a citation. Beaton, Sirois and Milne, 2022, PLoS ONE 17(2):e0263366. — [PMC8856522](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8856522/)
- **[General-population study]** *(secondary: university press release)* 141 lapsed Fitbit users: "Half... described feeling guilty about their lapsed Fitbit use, and nearly all of those said they would like to return"; an earlier survey of 193 found reactions from guilt to relief. Epstein et al., 2016, UbiComp. — [UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2016/09/08/life-after-fitbit-appealing-to-those-who-feel-guilty-vs-free/)
- **[Opinion/secondary]** *(unverified)* The "novelty fades" account appears on vendor pages with no data. — [Tiimo resource hub](https://www.tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/why-productivity-systems-fail-adhd)

### Inferences
Rules a phone-app designer could apply, each with its basis.
- **Open on something usable, not on a menu of reading.** The first screen offers a short list of tools; a person who knows their ADHD can start one in two taps. Basis: Seery 2025 (evidence, qualitative, one app, 14 people) and COGA "Make short critical paths" (guidance).
- **No setup before first use.** Any configuration is optional and comes after the first completed action. Basis: Chen 2026 participant reports (evidence, qualitative); Kidman 2024 on account creation (general-population evidence).
- **Tell people where to start.** One marked starting point per area; never a flat list of 37 pages. Basis: Seery 2025 (evidence, qualitative).
- **Show few steps at a time.** Reveal the next sub-step when the current one is done. Basis: Zhu 2026, one participant (evidence, weak) and COGA "five or less main choices" (guidance).
- **Content units finish in under five minutes.** First-use sessions averaged about five minutes in Seery 2025 (evidence, one app, averaged analytics).
- **Suggest, never decide.** Any automatic adjustment is offered and can be declined or switched off. Basis: Chen 2026 and Zhu 2026 (evidence, qualitative).
- **Describe a gap neutrally and never attribute it.** Basis: Beaton 2022 (evidence, qualitative, about criticism from people, not apps); the extension to app copy is inference.
- **Do not assume a log gives insight; make logging optional and cheap, and never tie it to an evening clock time.** Basis: Chasing Shadows (evidence, three people); opinion-level strength.
- **Social scaffolding cannot be delivered without a network.** A timer or "start with me" countdown as substitute is untested (opinion). The one positive body-double test is 12 people in VR.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal study of app abandonment in adults with ADHD. "Novelty wears off" rests on single participant quotes (Chen P11; Zhu on alarms) and vendor pages.
- No measurement of onboarding length, steps to capture or navigation depth in this population.
- Spiel et al., CHI '22, was not opened; Tan 2026 and Schofield 2026 both cite it for the argument that ADHD technology aims to discipline people into acting neurotypically.
- Ginapp et al. 2022 ([PMC9403235](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9403235/)) could not be loaded (challenge page).
- The 13-person ChatGPT diary study ([DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3764687.3764713)) was not examined.

## 2. What do systematic reviews of ADHD apps and digital interventions (2018–2026) conclude about engagement, adherence and dropout?

### Takeaway
Reviews agree that apps outnumber evidence, that adult work is mostly web or app CBT and psychoeducation, and that adherence is poorly reported and dropout high. No review links design features to sustained use. Trial data now give a clearer picture of adult ADHD drop-off: most of it happens in the first two weeks.

### Cited Findings
- **[ADHD review, all ages]** *(verified: abstract)* App stores searched to May 2017, rechecked March and September 2019: 355 apps, 109 included. "Very few apps contained information regarding their development and none contained information regarding evidence for its efficacy/ effectiveness." Of 51 papers, one met criteria. Păsărelu, Andersson and Dobrean, 2020, Int J Med Inform 138:104133. — [PubMed 32283479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32283479)
- **[ADHD review, adults]** *(verified: full text)* 133 studies to December 2025. 63 "Treat a Specific Condition" (26 web or app cognitive therapy or psychoeducation, 13 cognitive training, 12 transcranial stimulation, 9 neurofeedback); 36 "Drive Clinical Management"; 19 "Diagnose". It includes a risk-of-bias analysis. "Another key concern... is a low level or lack of reporting on adherence, and high dropout rates"; one cognitive-training study had 38% completion. "A minority of papers reported a form of development which actively implemented this feedback into an iterative design process." Schofield, Wilkie, Nielsen, Brigden, Jones and Isotalus, 2026, Front Digit Health 8:1746732. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1746732/full)
- **[ADHD review, adults]** *(verified: full text, arXiv; work in progress)* 3,538 records, 46 papers (2007–2025; 63% from 2021 on); 20 involve a mobile app. User-centred design was mentioned in 5 of 17 psychotherapy and psychoeducation papers, 0 of 12 cognitive-training papers and 4 of 10 assistive-tool papers; usability was evaluated in 9 of 17, 2 of 12 and 3 of 10. "Most papers take on a therapeutic or intervention perspective instead of a more positive support perspective." Tan, Jost, Gerken and Pascher, AT@WORK workshop, 26 October 2025. — [arXiv 2601.21791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21791)
- **[ADHD review, mostly children]** *(verified: abstract)* 14 studies to May 2024; keyword "children". "The FOCUS ADHD app was well accepted but did not improve treatment compliance." Hosseinnia et al., 2025, Front Public Health. — [PMC11904913](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11904913/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text)* Inflow CBT app, 7-week open study, adults recruited from the company's waitlist. Median 3.86 sessions a week, median session 3.40 minutes; over seven weeks the median user opened the app 27 times, started 2 modules and finished 1 (means 3.29 and 2.10; n = 178). "About 54% could be considered drop outs before the Week 7 assessment: 14% failed to successfully enroll with the Inflow app, 36% dropped out before Week 2, 3% between Week 2 and 4, and only 1% dropped out between Week 4 and 7." Two authors co-founded the company. Knouse, Hu, Sachs and Isaacs, 2022, PLOS Digital Health. Usage data **need a server**. — [PMC9931323](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9931323/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: abstract)* RCT, group psychoeducation supported by an app (n = 30) or brochures (n = 30), eight weekly sessions. Both arms improved; "the smartphone-assisted psychoeducation was significantly more effective in improving inattention and impulsivity and led to higher homework compliance". Observer-rated outcome. The app was an adjunct to a clinician-led group. Selaskowski et al., 2022, Psychiatry Research 317:114802. — [PubMed 36041353](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36041353/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: abstract)* A second trial (40 randomised, 17 per arm completing, three weeks, self-guided) compared a chatbot with the conventional app: symptoms fell in both, with "no group × intervention interaction effects". This is the source of the "similar efficacy" description. Selaskowski et al., 2023, BJPsych Open 9(6):e192. — [PubMed 37827996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37827996/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: abstract)* Self-guided internet intervention RCT, N = 120 with self-reported diagnosis: "29% completed all modules, while 59% completed at least five modules (out of 7)"; symptom effect d = 0.70 against an online psychoeducation control, self-rated. Kenter, Gjestad, Lundervold and Nordgreen, 2023, Internet Interventions 32:100614. — [PubMed 36969389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36969389/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text)* In the related reminder study, 56% (61 of 109) completed at least five of seven modules; mean 4.6 modules. The paper cites Pettersson et al. 2017 for 77% non-completion in an earlier internet CBT trial (that paper not opened). Nordby et al., 2022. — [PMC9149073](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9149073/)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: full text)* 93 Android mental-health apps with 10,000 or more installs, panel data August 2017 to July 2018. Median open rate 4.0% (IQR 4.7%); median 15-day retention 3.9% (IQR 10.3%), 30-day 3.3% (IQR 6.2%). Day 30: trackers 6.1%, mindfulness 4.7%, peer support 8.9% (n = 2), breathing 0.0%. Retention is the share of people who installed and opened the app on day 0 who opened it on a given later day. Baumel, Muench, Edan and Kane, 2019, JMIR 21(9):e14567. — [PMC6785720](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6785720/)
- **[General-population review]** *(verified: full text, PMC)* 18 studies, 525,824 participants. Abandonment is sharp early and then slows; "a median abandonment rate at 100 days of approximately 70%" (8 of 10 studies at 50% or more). By domain at last follow-up: physical activity 54%–75%, alcohol 95%–97%, mental health 89%–92% (two studies), though mental-health apps appeared to be abandoned more gradually. 22 reasons in six categories. "Data loss" and "needing to create an account" each come from one study of user reviews; "annoying notifications" and "lack of accountability" from one other. "Confusing or difficult" was the most consistent reason. Kidman, Curtis, Watson and Maher, 2024, JMIR 26:e56897. — [PMC11694054](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11694054/)
- **[ADHD review, all ages]** *(unverified)* Hernández-Capistrán et al., 2025: 14.8% of apps aided diagnosis. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/tbitxx/v44y2025i16p4037-4064.html)

### Inferences
- **The first session and first fortnight carry the weight.** In Knouse 2022, 50 of the 54 percentage points of dropout came before week 2; in Seery 2025 engagement peaked in the first two weeks. Design so that one session of about five minutes delivers something complete. Basis: evidence (two adult ADHD studies, one with a company interest).
- **"Confusing or difficult" is the best-supported abandonment reason.** Prioritise plain navigation over features. Basis: general-population review (evidence).
- **No account is a real advantage; backup is the matching risk.** Both rest on a single source inside the review, so treat as weak evidence.
- **Do not plan around trial completion rates.** 56%–59% completing five of seven modules comes from recruited, assessed samples; real-world 30-day retention is about 3%.
- A tool used once in a hard week may have done its job. This is opinion; no review tests it.

### Gaps
- No review links design features to sustained use in adults with ADHD.
- Selaskowski 2022's percentages and completer counts are not in the abstract; full text not obtained.
- All retention figures **need a server**. A device-only app cannot measure its own.

## 3. What does cognitive accessibility guidance say that applies?

### Takeaway
COGA is the most applicable guidance and it is advisory consensus, not trial evidence. Read in full, its patterns on interruptions, short paths, memory, reminders and data loss are specific enough to use as rules. No standards body has an ADHD-specific interface guideline.

### Cited Findings
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* "Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities", W3C Working Group Note, 29 April 2021. "Following this guidance is not required for conformance to WCAG." The groups "do not expect this document to become a W3C Recommendation". The abstract does not name ADHD; the glossary defines AD(H)D, and one persona is "Yuki: A Yoga Teacher who has AD(H)D", whose scenarios are dense text, carousels she cannot stop, losing her place in a task, and long videos. — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* The eight objectives: 1 Help Users Understand What Things are and How to Use Them; 2 Help Users Find What They Need; 3 Use Clear and Understandable Content; 4 Help Users Avoid Mistakes and Know How to Correct Them; 5 Help Users Focus; 6 Ensure Processes Do Not Rely on Memory; 7 Provide Help and Support; 8 Support Adaptation and Personalization. — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* Objective 5 patterns. **4.6.1 Limit Interruptions**: provide "an easy way to control interruptions, reminders, and changes in content unless they are started by the user or involve an emergency"; best is "no interruptions at all", a pause, or user-selected types. **4.6.2 Make Short Critical Paths**: "include only the minimally necessary steps... Do not require the user to go through optional steps"; its example of good practice is "open the app, run the most used function". **4.6.3 Avoid Too Much Content**: "Provide users with five or less main choices on each screen"; extra choices under a "more" link. **4.6.4 Provide Information So a User Can Complete and Prepare for a Task**: before a multi-step task give "the time it might take", resources needed and an overview; "a generous estimate"; make clear when a task is in process and when complete. — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* Objective 6. **4.7.5 Do Not Rely on Users Calculations or Memorizing Information**: "each step in a process must contain the information needed to allow a user to proceed. They must not rely on memory from prior steps"; show a step indicator and a summary of earlier steps. **4.7.1 Provide a Login that Does Not Rely on Memory or Other Cognitive Skills**: no memorising character strings; biometrics listed as an option. The text gives a working-memory span of "one to four pieces of information" for people with impairment. — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* Objective 7, **4.8.7 Provide Reminders**: "Reminders must be set only at the user's request and the user must be able to personalize the [reminder] method." "Always give the option to set a reminder at the end of the task so that the user does not get interrupted." "It is essential not to add unwanted reminders." — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* Objective 4, forgiving errors. **4.5.2 Let Users Go Back**: "The user should never lose their work if they press back." **4.5.5 Make it Easy to Undo Form Errors**. **4.5.9 Avoid Data Loss and "Timeouts"**: "Avoid timeouts and let the user save their work as they go." **4.5.10 Provide Feedback**: "For each step in a process let the user know of its status and if it was successfully completed." **4.5.1 Ensure Controls and Content Do Not Move Unexpectedly**. **4.5.4 Design Forms to Prevent Mistakes**: require "as little information as possible". — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* Other relevant patterns: 4.2.4 Make Each Step Clear (steps completed, current, pending); 4.3.1 and 4.3.4 (key tasks and controls visible "without needing to scroll"); 4.2.2 Use a Familiar Hierarchy and Design; 4.4.5 Keep Text Succinct; 4.4.10 Use White Spacing; 4.9.1 Let Users Control When the Content Moves or Changes; 4.9.4 Support a Personalized and Familiar Interface (font, size, spacing, contrast preferences). The Distractions user story adds: "I need to know the context, where I am, what I just did... after I lost cognitive focus." — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* WCAG 2.2 (Recommendation, 12 December 2024) added nine criteria. Relevant: 3.2.6 Consistent Help (A); 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A): previously entered information is "auto-populated, or available for the user to select"; 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum) (AA): no "cognitive function test (such as remembering a password or solving a puzzle)" unless an alternative or assisting mechanism exists, with password managers and copy and paste named as examples; 3.3.9 the Enhanced version (AAA); 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (AA): 24 by 24 CSS pixels. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* Older criteria: 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable (A); 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (A: anything moving that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and sits beside other content can be paused, stopped or hidden); 2.2.3 No Timing (AAA); 2.2.4 Interruptions (AAA: "can be postponed or suppressed by the user, except interruptions involving an emergency"); 2.2.6 Timeouts (AAA); 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA); 3.2.3 Consistent Navigation (AA); 3.2.4 Consistent Identification (AA); 3.2.5 Change on Request (AAA); 3.3.4 Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data) (AA: reversible, checked or confirmed, and it covers deleting user data); 3.3.6 Error Prevention (All) (AAA); 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) 4.5:1 (AA); 1.4.6 Contrast (Enhanced) 7:1 (AAA). — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- **[Opinion/secondary]** *(unverified, not reopened)* UK government and university practitioner pages defer to COGA. — [DWP Digital, 2022](https://dwpdigital.blog.gov.uk/2022/06/30/designing-accessible-services-dont-exclude-the-neurodiverse); [St Andrews, 2025](https://digitalcommunications.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/2025/02/12/designing-for-users-with-adhd/)

### Inferences
All of these rest on guidance, not trials.
- **Five or fewer main choices per screen; the rest behind "more".** COGA 4.6.3.
- **The most used function is reachable as "open the app, run it".** COGA 4.6.2.
- **Optional steps sit outside the path and are never required.** COGA 4.6.2.
- **Nothing interrupts unless the person started it.** No pop-ups mid-task. COGA 4.6.1; WCAG 2.2.4.
- **A reminder exists only if the person set it, and it is offered at the end of a task, never during one.** COGA 4.8.7.
- **Each step shows what is needed from earlier steps; never ask twice.** COGA 4.7.5; WCAG 3.3.7.
- **Back never loses work; entries save as typed; no timeouts.** COGA 4.5.2, 4.5.9.
- **Every save says it saved.** COGA 4.5.10.
- **On return, show where the person was and what they last did.** COGA Distractions user story.
- **Before a multi-step task, say how long it takes, generously.** COGA 4.6.4.
- **A numeric passcode is a cognitive function test.** Allow paste and password-manager fill; offer device biometrics where the platform allows it without a server. WCAG 3.3.8; COGA 4.7.1.
- **Deleting data is reversible or confirmed.** WCAG 3.3.4.
- **Nothing on screen moves by itself.** COGA 4.5.1, 4.9.1; WCAG 2.2.2.

### Gaps
- No ADHD-specific interface guideline from W3C, ISO, NICE or a national ADHD body was found.
- COGA reports no controlled tests of its patterns in ADHD, and describes itself as work in progress.
- Nielsen Norman Group pages are reachable now but none was read closely enough to cite.

## 4. What is known about time perception, working memory, task initiation and reward sensitivity in adult ADHD that has interface implications?

### Takeaway
The cognitive findings hold but need narrower wording. Time-based prospective memory shows a large impairment in adults while event-based does not; planning is the impaired step in complex intentions; delay discounting is medium-sized and does not differ by age group. The timing meta-analysis covers milliseconds to seconds, so it does not directly support claims about misjudging minutes or hours. No visual-timer trial in adults exists.

### Cited Findings
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: abstract)* 25 adults with ADHD and 25 matched controls on a computerised breakfast-preparation task: "Although groups did not differ in event-based PM, results demonstrated a large-sized impairment in individuals with ADHD in time-based PM." Altgassen, Kretschmer and Kliegel, 2014, J Atten Disord 18(7):617–624. — [PubMed 22660916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22660916/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: abstract)* 45 adults with ADHD not on stimulants against 45 matched controls: "A large-scale impairment could be observed in task planning abilities... Only negligible to small effects were found for plan recall, self-initiation and execution." Fuermaier et al., 2013, PLoS ONE 8(3):e58338. — [PubMed 23484020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23484020/)
- **[ADHD review, all ages]** *(verified: abstract)* 55 studies of perceptual timing "in the range of milliseconds to several seconds": deficits in discrimination, estimation, production and reproduction, with attentional and motivational components; "a broad range of timing deficits". No adult subgroup is given in the abstract. Marx, Cortese, Koelch and Hacker, 2022, JAACAP 61(7):866–880. — [PubMed 34923055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34923055/)
- **[ADHD review, adults]** *(verified: full text)* "The number of studies in the field of time perception in ADHD in the past 10 years is very scarce." Mette, 2023, IJERPH 20(4):3098. — [PMC9962130](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9962130/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: abstract)* 20 adults with ADHD, 20 unaffected relatives, 20 controls: "ADHD participants were impaired in temporal reproduction", relatives in between. Pironti, Lai, Morein-Zamir, Müller, Bullmore and Sahakian, 2016, Psychol Med 46(12):2561–2569. — [PubMed 27345441](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27345441/)
- **[ADHD review, adults]** *(verified: abstract; effect sizes unverified)* 38 studies: "moderate-magnitude between-group effect sizes across both WM domains"; deficits "persist into adulthood" and task differences explain why they are not uniformly found. The figures 0.55 and 0.49 are not in the abstract. Alderson, Kasper, Hudec and Patros, 2013, Neuropsychology 27(3):287–302. — [PubMed 23688211](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23688211/)
- **[ADHD review, all ages]** *(verified: abstract)* 21 investigations, 25 case-control comparisons, N = 3,913: steeper monetary delay discounting, d = 0.43. "No significant differences based on sample age [under 18 against over 18], reward outcome, or comorbid status." Jackson and MacKillop, 2016. — [PubMed 27722208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27722208/)
- **[ADHD review, all ages]** *(verified: abstract)* 37 comparisons, 3,763 participants: small-to-medium effects; "offering real rewards in the [simple choice paradigm] almost doubled the odds ratio". Marx, Hacker, Yu, Cortese and Sonuga-Barke, 2021, J Atten Disord 25(2):171–187. — [PubMed 29806533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29806533/)
- **[General-population study, students]** *(verified: abstract)* 54 undergraduates: "only inattention was correlated with general procrastination"; called "preliminary". Niermann and Scheres, 2014. — [PubMed 24992694](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24992694/)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: abstract)* 470 adults, mean age 26.6: more ADHD symptoms, more procrastination and internalising symptoms. Oguchi, Takahashi, Nitta and Kumano, 2021. — [PubMed 34764902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34764902/)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: abstract; N unverified)* ADHD symptom scores in healthy participants were associated with interference from irrelevant distractors (cartoon images). "Higher perceptual load during a task eliminated distraction irrespective of ADHD score." Forster and Lavie, 2016, Psychol Sci 27(2):203–212. — [PubMed 26667659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26667659/)
- **[ADHD-adult study, self-report]** *(verified: full text of the citing paper only)* Chen 2026 cites Desrochers et al. 2019 for calendars, lists and reminders being "perceived as significantly less effective by adults with ADHD than by non-ADHD users, despite similar patterns of adoption". The cited study was not opened. — [arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)
- **[Child study]** PubMed searches for visual timers and ADHD returned two records, both in children (an elementary maths-assessment study and time-related occupational therapy). Nothing in adults. — [PubMed 41440149](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41440149/); [PubMed 32955952](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32955952/)

### Inferences
- **Never make a feature depend on the person acting at a clock time.** Tie prompts to an event: opening the app, finishing a step, something that happens in the day. Basis: Altgassen 2014 (evidence, adults, n = 25 per group, lab task); the interface rule is inference.
- **Supply the plan.** Offer a ready first step or template so starting does not need planning first. Basis: Fuermaier 2013 (evidence, adults, lab task); inference to interfaces.
- **Keep the current step, the last answer and the next action visible together.** Basis: Alderson 2013 (evidence, moderate) and COGA 4.7.5 (guidance).
- **Confirm at the moment of the action, with something real.** A saved state the person can see, not a promise of later benefit. Basis: Jackson and MacKillop 2016, Marx 2021 (evidence about money choices; the interface step is inference).
- **Take decoration off task screens.** Irrelevant images cost attention as symptoms rise. Basis: Forster and Lavie 2016 (evidence, general population, correlational).
- **A visual timer is plausible, not evidenced.** The timing evidence concerns seconds, the adult literature is "very scarce", and no adult trial exists. If offered, show remaining time as a shrinking quantity with the number, and planned beside actual duration; call it untested. Basis: opinion built on COGA 4.6.4 and Zhu 2026 participants' stated wish for time calibration.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of visual time displays, step reduction or immediate feedback as interface features in adults with ADHD.
- Alderson's numeric effect sizes and Jackson and MacKillop's adult-only estimate need the full texts.
- Task initiation is not directly measured in adults; Fuermaier found self-initiation effects negligible to small, which cuts against "initiation deficit" as a lab finding.
- Beheshti et al. 2020 on emotion dysregulation was not retrieved.

## 5. What is the evidence on gamification, streaks and rewards for people with ADHD specifically?

### Takeaway
No study has tested streaks, points or badges on outcomes in adults with ADHD. What exists is stated preference, and it points both ways: some adults with ADHD ask for rewards and streaks, others describe shame around unmet plans, and experts warn against penalties. General-population research shows a displayed broken streak lowers later engagement, most when self-blamed, and gamification did not change adherence in depression-app trials.

### Cited Findings
- **Search result.** PubMed, 14 November 2026: "(streak OR streaks) AND (ADHD OR attention deficit hyperactivity)" returned 0 records; "gamification adults ADHD" returned 1 (a review of board games); a broader points, badges and rewards query returned 5, none testing these mechanics. — [PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=%28streak+OR+streaks%29+AND+%28ADHD+OR+attention+deficit+hyperactivity%29)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text)* In interviews about a psychoeducation app, "Rewards, badges or streaks for engaging with mindfulness practices or reading psychoeducation content were also noted as ways to improve engagement", alongside unlocking content in stages for a "dopamine hit". The authors add that "it may be particularly helpful for adults with ADHD to have gamified elements". Stated preference from some of 14 people; no outcome measured. Seery et al., 2025. — [PMC11847728](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11847728/)
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text, arXiv)* The authors propose "preserving streaks or progress markers even when users skip days could further reduce shame spirals". Their concept "Flexible Planning and Gentle Streaks" (the system "shifts to the baseline plan without resetting progress streaks") "resonated with participants who struggled with self-criticism around incomplete plans"; one said it stopped them "hating themselves for not completing everything". Some cautioned that too much flexibility could demotivate. One participant preferred a "mild haze" state display to "red overdue tasks". Reactions to a described concept, not use. Chen et al., 2026. — [arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)
- **[ADHD-adult study, students; expert opinion]** *(verified: full text, arXiv)* Experts supported "continuous positive feedback and rewards" that are specific, and warned that penalty-based feedback "may trigger negative emotions, such as frustration, and lead to resistance": "it feels like saying, 'See, you didn't do it again.'" Zhu et al., 2026. — [arXiv 2602.09381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: journal abstract as reproduced on a university listing; publisher page refused)* Seven studies: "intact streaks highlighted via behavioral logs increase consumers' subsequent engagement in that behavior, relative to when broken streaks are highlighted. Importantly, this effect is independent of actual past behavior and depends solely on how that behavior is represented within the log." The effect "is amplified when consumers attribute a break in the streak to themselves" and "attenuated when consumers can 'repair' a broken streak". A streak is defined as three or more consecutive times. Silverman and Barasch, 2023, J Consumer Research 49(6):1095–1117. — [University of Colorado listing](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)
- **[General-population review]** *(verified: full text, PMC)* 38 trials of depression apps (n = 8,110), 50 comparisons; 27 of 38 studies had gamification elements (points, badges, levels, stories, goals, feedback, rewards, progress bars, challenges; leaderboards excluded). The number of elements did not predict symptoms (β = −0.03, SE 0.03, P = .38) or adherence (β = −1.93, SE 2.28, P = .40), where adherence means the share of intervention modules completed in a trial. The authors note the result may not carry to conditions without anhedonia. Six, Byrne, Tibbett and Pericot-Valverde, 2021, JMIR Mental Health 8(11):e32199. — [PMC8669581](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8669581/)
- **[ADHD review, all ages]** *(verified: abstract)* Delay-discounting findings show a preference for sooner rewards; they do not test app mechanics. — [PubMed 27722208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27722208/)
- **[Opinion/secondary]** *(unverified)* Vendor pages asserting streaks harm ADHD users. — [Klarity blog](https://www.helloklarity.com/post/breaking-the-chain-why-streak-features-fail-adhd-users-and-how-to-design-better-alternatives)

### Inferences
- **The honest statement.** Leaving streaks out is defensible on general evidence (a shown break lowers engagement, more so when self-blamed) and on adult ADHD reports of self-criticism. It is not shown that streaks harm adults with ADHD, and some ask for them. Say this plainly wherever the choice is explained.
- **If nothing counts consecutive days, nothing can show a break.** Basis: Silverman and Barasch 2023 (evidence, general population): the harm is in the representation.
- **Confirm each action immediately and specifically.** Basis: Zhu 2026 experts (opinion); delay-discounting reviews (evidence, indirect).
- **Never show a penalty, a reset or an overdue marker in alarm colour.** Basis: Zhu 2026 experts (opinion); Chen 2026 participants (evidence, qualitative, weak).
- **What participants asked for can partly be met without a streak:** content revealed in small stages with a clear "done", and totals that only go up. Basis: Seery 2025 (evidence, stated preference). Whether this satisfies the wish is untested.
- Gamification "not helping adherence" is a depression-trial finding; do not cite it as an ADHD result.

### Gaps
- No adult ADHD outcome study of streaks, points, badges, levels or rewards, positive or negative.
- Non-resetting "gentle" streaks have been shown to adults with ADHD only as a described concept.
- Parent-run reward charts for children do not transfer to self-administered adult mechanics.

## 6. What is known about notifications and reminders for ADHD users?

### Takeaway
The one randomised test in adults with ADHD found no overall effect of SMS reminders on completion. Two non-randomised clinic programmes report roughly double the timely stimulant refills. Habituation in ADHD is reported by participants but not measured. Guidance says a reminder must be the person's own.

### Cited Findings
- **[ADHD-adult study]** *(verified: full text)* 109 adults with self-reported diagnosis in a seven-module self-guided intervention. Each week, those who had not finished the module within 48 hours were randomised to an SMS reminder or none ("multiple randomized trial design"). "The overall results did not show an effect of SMS reminders on module completion, number of logins or practice of coping strategies"; a reminder was associated with login within 48 hours in week two only (p = .049), and with faster login and more time in some modules. Limits: everyone got an SMS when a new module opened; week one was excluded after a technical error; almost 40% missed post-assessment; no power analysis. Habituation is raised as a risk, not a finding. Nordby, Gjestad, Kenter, Guribye, Mukhiya, Lundervold and Nordgreen, 2022, Front Digit Health 4:821031. **Needs a server.** — [PMC9149073](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9149073/)
- **[ADHD-adult study, non-randomised]** *(verified: abstract)* Adults 18–55 on stimulants receiving an SMS programme against age- and sex-matched comparators drawn 5 to 1 from health records: 68% against 34% refilled within 37 days (OR 4.04, 95% CI 2.49–6.56). Biederman et al., 2019, J Clin Psychopharmacol 39(4):351–356. **Needs a server**; medication adherence. — [PubMed 31162154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31162154/)
- **[ADHD-adult study, open label]** *(verified: abstract)* 117 adults from primary care and psychiatry; 96% completed 37 days of the programme; 81% against 36% timely refills (OR 7.54, 95% CI 4.46–12.77), matched record comparators. Biederman et al., 2020, Psychiatry Research 291:113158. **Needs a server.** — [PubMed 32559669](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32559669/)
- **[Child study]** *(verified: abstract)* The same programme in 87 children aged 6–12: 85% against 62%. This is the source of a third pair of figures in circulation. The "35% rising to 70%" figure was found in no abstract. Fried et al., 2020. — [PubMed 32077768](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077768/)
- **[ADHD-adult studies, participant reports]** *(verified: full text, arXiv)* "If it's only me reminding myself, it just becomes invisible after a few days" (Chen 2026, P11). Students who varied their alarm sounds found it "still failed to engage them over time" (Zhu 2026). Some found pattern-based nudges intrusive: "it feels like it's watching me" (Chen 2026, S14). Ambient cues were "often too subtle to notice". — [arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258); [arXiv 2602.09381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: abstract)* 1,255 users of a workplace wellbeing app over 89 days, micro-randomised: a tailored push made self-monitoring in the next 24 hours 3.9% more likely (risk ratio 1.039, 95% CI 1.01–1.08). The effect "attenuated over the course of the study" but not significantly (P = .84); weekends 8.7%, weekdays 2.5% (difference P = .18). Three authors hold shares in the app's company. Bidargaddi et al., 2018. **Needs a server.** — [PubMed 30497999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30497999/)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: abstract, arXiv)* 30 volunteers disabled notifications for 24 hours: they "felt less distracted and more productive" and also less responsive, some anxious, and less connected. Pielot and Rello, 2017. — [arXiv 1612.02314](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02314)
- **[General-population review]** *(verified: abstract)* Prompted self-report compliance in research studies: 81.9% (95% CI 79.1–84.4); median protocol seven days, five prompts a day, incentives in 63% of data sets. Not comparable to ordinary app use. Williams et al., 2021, JMIR 23(3):e17023. — [PubMed 33656451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33656451/)
- **[General-population review]** *(verified: full text, PMC)* "Annoying notifications" is a recorded reason for abandoning health apps (one source study). Kidman et al., 2024. — [PMC11694054](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11694054/)
- **[General-population study]** *(unverified, not opened)* Kushlev, Proulx and Dunn, 2016: more self-reported inattention with alerts on. — [Paper PDF](https://interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-CHI16.pdf)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* COGA 4.8.7 and 4.6.1 (section 3): reminders only at the person's request, method chosen by them, offered at the end of a task, easy to turn off. — [W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)

### Inferences
- **An app with no notifications is not missing an evidenced feature.** The only randomised adult ADHD result is null; the positive results are clinic-run, non-randomised and about medication refills. Basis: evidence.
- **If a reminder is ever offered:** off by default; created by the person; offered when they finish something; one tap to remove; never reports what was missed. Basis: COGA 4.8.7, 4.6.1 (guidance); Kidman 2024 (evidence, general population).
- **Prefer event anchors to clock times.** Basis: Altgassen 2014 (evidence, section 4); inference.
- **Expect any fixed cue to fade.** Basis: two participant reports (evidence, anecdotal); Bidargaddi's attenuation was not significant. Do not design around a reminder staying effective.
- **In-app prompts on opening need no server and no permission.** Untested (opinion).

### Gaps
- No study measures habituation, timing or wording of reminders in adults with ADHD.
- Whether locally scheduled notifications work without a server on each platform was not researched.
- The refill studies say nothing about reminders for non-medication tasks.

## 7. Is there credible evidence on visual design preferences (colour, contrast, density, typography, dark mode, animation) for ADHD users?

### Takeaway
Still mostly unevidenced. A PubMed search for dark mode, typeface, font or background colour with adults and ADHD returned nothing on interface design. What exists is one preference survey, one very small reading study known second-hand, qualitative reports of overwhelm from dense or cluttered apps, and guidance.

### Cited Findings
- **[ADHD-adult study, mixed conditions]** *(verified: abstract, on the repository record)* Adults with ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia or dyspraxia rated font style, font size, character and line spacing, title design, background colour, reward icon and instruction layout. "Results indicated consistent preference across all neurodiverse groups... The exception to this was background colour, in which each neurodiverse group preferred a different colour." The colours and the sample size of 204 were not visible on the record. Preference, not performance. Otermans et al., Educational Media International 63(1):6–27, online August 2025. — [Brunel repository](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/32230)
- **[ADHD-adult study, very small]** *(secondary: a literature-review site)* Eye-tracking reading study; the review reports Roboto at default size as fastest (about 10% above mean) with the lowest comprehension accuracy, a speed-comprehension trade-off the earlier notes omitted. Llana, Baron, Yu, Hosseinpour, Suhail, Chin, Khadka and Wallace, CHI EA '26, DOI 10.1145/3772363.3799383. Primary paper not opened. — [Review page](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69ed2c3f083d4508fd230b16)
- **[ADHD-adult studies, participant reports]** *(verified: full text)* Interviewees were "overwhelmed at the number of pages", "preferred pages with colourful graphics and suggested having more graphics" (Seery 2025); participants criticised "cluttered interfaces that heightened anxiety" (Chen 2026). — [PMC11847728](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11847728/); [arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)
- **Search result.** PubMed, 14 November 2026: dark mode, typeface, font or background colour with adults, ADHD and reading, interface or preference returned 6 records, none about interface design. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=dark+mode+ADHD)
- **[General-population study]** *(verified: abstract)* ADHD symptom scores go with distraction by irrelevant images; higher perceptual load removes it. Forster and Lavie, 2016. — [PubMed 26667659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26667659/)
- **[Guidance]** *(verified: full text)* COGA 4.6.3, 4.4.10, 4.3.4, 4.2.3, 4.9.4; the AD(H)D persona's problem is banners "automatically flying by", solved by a system reduced-motion setting. WCAG 2.2.2, 2.3.3, 1.4.3 (4.5:1), 1.4.6 (7:1). — [W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- **[Opinion/secondary]** *(unverified)* Practitioner and vendor claims on fonts and dark mode. — [Accessibility Checker guide](https://www.accessibilitychecker.org/blog/the-best-fonts-for-adhd/)

### Inferences
- **Colour is identity, not treatment.** Meet 4.5:1; never describe a palette as ADHD-specific. Basis: Otermans (evidence, preference only); absence of trials.
- **Graphics that carry content are welcome; decoration beside a task is not.** Basis: Seery 2025 interviewees (evidence, qualitative) against Forster and Lavie (evidence, general population). The two do not conflict if the image is the content.
- **Reduce the count of things, not the warmth.** Five or fewer choices, white space, one marked starting point. Basis: COGA (guidance); Seery 2025 and Chen 2026 (evidence, qualitative).
- **Honour the system reduced-motion setting and start nothing moving.** Basis: COGA persona, WCAG 2.2.2 (guidance).
- **Respect system text size; offer no typeface as an ADHD aid.** Basis: Llana (secondary, tiny, and shows a trade-off).
- **Dark mode, if offered, is a preference.** No evidence either way (opinion).

### Gaps
- No controlled study of colour, contrast, dark mode, density, typeface or animation in adults with ADHD.
- Otermans' colours and sample size, and the Llana paper, need the full texts.
- No study of icon-only against labelled navigation or items per screen in this population.
