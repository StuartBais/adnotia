## 1. Per-app teardown: Tiimo, Structured, Finch, Goblin Tools, Routinery, Llama Life, Amazing Marvin, Sunsama, Things 3, TickTick, Inflow, Focus Bear, Numo, Bearable, Daylio, Time Timer, Focusmate, How We Feel, 2025–2026 standouts, and the family apps Joon, Brili, Goally and Cozi

### Takeaway
The two apps with the strongest current award record are both visual day-timeline planners: Tiimo (Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025, Apple Design Award finalist 2024) and Structured (Apple Design Award Inclusivity finalist 2026, iPad App of the Year finalist 2025), and Apple's praise for both is about calm and scannability rather than feature count. Hard facts on awards, platform stack and Apple's wording are primary-sourced below. After the 14 November 2026 pass, navigation and task flow are known at search-summary level for Tiimo, Structured, Finch, Things and TickTick, pricing has been refreshed for most apps, and Structured's cloud-versus-device AI split is resolved; onboarding detail, typography and accessibility settings are still missing for most apps because vendor sites and store listings remained unreachable.

- **Data model, from the vendor's public API documentation.** Marvin stores tasks in a CouchDB-style document database. A **Task** has `parentId` (a project or category, or "unassigned" for the inbox), `day` ("YYYY-MM-DD" or "unassigned"), `dueDate`, `startDate`, `endDate`, `plannedWeek` and `plannedMonth`, `timeEstimate` ("How long the user thinks the task will take, in ms"), `duration` ("How long the user worked on this task (in ms)", written when the task is done), `times` (a flat array of start and stop timestamps; an odd number of entries means the task is being timed now), `subtasks` (a map of checklist items, each with a title, done flag, rank and its own time estimate), `dependsOn`, `backburner`, `isStarred`, `isFrogged`, `isPinned`, `labelIds`, and `rank` (order in the day) kept apart from `masterRank` (order in the master list). `firstScheduled` records the first day a task was assigned and exists, in the documentation's words, for procrastination tracking. ## 2. How do the best apps model tasks so one item appears in a list, on a timeline, in a breakdown and under a timer; what domains or tabs do "all in one" apps use; how do they avoid overwhelm as scope grows?

### Takeaway
The well-regarded apps treat a task as one record that gains fields as it moves (title, then an icon and estimate, then a time, then a checklist, then a timer state), and expose it through a small number of views rather than separate tools. The clearest primary-sourced lesson on overwhelm is Headspace collapsing four category tabs into a single Today tab backed by an Explore tab.

### Cited Findings
- Things 3 separates structure from time: Area → Project → Heading → To-do → Checklist for structure, and a "When" date (Today, This Evening, Upcoming, Anytime, Someday) plus an optional Deadline for time. A start date hides the task until it is relevant; a deadline keeps it visible. [S] — [Vanja: Things 3 complete guide](https://vanja.io/things-3-complete-guide/); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/things-3/id904237743)
- Structured: an Inbox holds unscheduled tasks; the Timeline holds tasks as blocks of time; subtasks live inside a task; all three are in the free tier. [S] — [Cool Curation review](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)
- Tiimo: a typed or spoken intention becomes tasks with suggested durations, an auto-matched emoji and a place on the day's timeline. [S] — [Tiimo: AI Co-planner design](https://www.tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/ai-co-planner-design); [Built for Mars](https://builtformars.com/company/tiimo)
- Llama Life makes the timer a property of every task rather than a separate tool. [S] — [MakerStack review](https://makerstack.co/reviews/llama-life-review/)
- Routinery makes the routine the object: ordered steps, each with a duration, played in sequence. [S] — [Headway: Routinery](https://makeheadway.com/blog/routinery/)
- TickTick shows the same task in lists, several calendar views, an Eisenhower Matrix and under a Pomodoro timer, with habits alongside. [S] — [CRM.org: TickTick review](https://crm.org/news/ticktick-review)
- Super Productivity (open source) combines subtasks, timeboxing on a schedule timeline next to calendar events, a focus mode (Pomodoro, Flowtime or countdown), break reminders and boards in one local-first model. [P] — [GitHub: super-productivity](https://github.com/johannesjo/super-productivity)
- Sunsama's ritual is a guided pass over one list: carry over, pull in, estimate, schedule, with an over-commitment warning, then an evening shutdown review. [S] — [Morgen: Sunsama pricing](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/sunsama-pricing)
- Structured's developer has said he wants a view that shows only the task being worked on. [S] — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/structured-breakdown)
- Headspace on reducing scope at the surface: earlier versions had separate tabs for meditation, focus, movement and sleep; research led the team to "strip away that complexity" in favour of a Today tab offering one-tap activities for morning, afternoon and night, with an Explore tab as the gateway to everything else. "There's still simplicity at the surface… But there's an incredible depth of content underneath." [P] — [Apple Developer: Behind the Design: Headspace, 5 June 2023](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=fkfnhq8u)
- Amazing Marvin takes the opposite route, opt-in modules ("strategies", 94 to 100+), and reviewers report the first week as overwhelming. [S] — [Saner.ai: Amazing Marvin review](https://blog.saner.ai/amazing-marvin-review/)
- Streaks constrains the count: its creator settled on about six tasks a day. "I found if I did four or five tasks, a sixth… needed to be done too." The app suggests from roughly 500 icons based on what is typed. [P] — [Apple Developer spotlight: Streaks, 5 Feb 2021](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=67dti69d)
- Bears Gratitude has no sign-in: "We wanted people to go straight into the experience and start writing", and was designed "in the same order that users experience it". [P] — [Apple Developer: Behind the Design: Bears Gratitude, 4 Feb 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=i74v3f4r)
- Widgets and Live Activities are repeatedly singled out by Apple as the "glance without opening" surface: Tiimo's widget with a running timer and checklist; Opal's widgets; Lumy's "elegant widgets and Live Activities"; Flighty's Live Activities and Dynamic Island. [P] — [Apple Developer: Tiimo story](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo); [Apple Design Awards 2025](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/); [Apple Design Awards 2023](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2023/)
- Apple's cognitive-accessibility guidance: "Prefer system gestures and behaviors people are already familiar with over creating custom gestures people must learn and retain"; "Views and controls that auto-dismiss on a timer can be problematic for people who need longer to process information… Prefer dismissing views with an explicit action"; avoid auto-playing media without controls. [P] — [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Domains seen across the set: plan or today timeline (Tiimo, Structured); capture inbox or to-do (Structured, Things); routines (Routinery, Brili, Focus Bear); focus timer (Llama Life, Time Timer, Focus Friend, TickTick); mood or check-in (Tiimo via State of Mind, Daylio, How We Feel, Bearable); habits (TickTick, Streaks); learning (Inflow); community or body doubling (Numo, Focusmate). [S/P] — sources as cited per app in section 1; [Apple Developer: Tiimo story](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo)

### Inferences
- A workable single-record model implied by these apps: `item { title, icon or emoji, colour, estimated duration, optional date, optional start time, checklist[], recurrence, state, actual time spent }`. Inbox is "no date"; the list is "date, no time"; the timeline is "date and time"; the breakdown is the checklist; the timer is the running state of the item or of one checklist row; review reads state and actuals. Nothing is re-entered because each stage only adds fields.
- Two shapes recur for the first screen: a "today" surface that already contains the next action (Tiimo, Structured, Headspace's Today, Things' Today) and a library behind it. The apps that lead with configuration (Marvin) or with many choices in onboarding (Finch, per the Pratt critiques) draw the overwhelm complaints.
- Progressive disclosure appears in four forms: fields added only when wanted (duration, checklist); a single now-view while a timer runs; optional modules; and widgets that carry the one current thing outside the app.
- Limiting quantity (Streaks' six, a short routine cap in Focus Bear's free tier) works as a design feature as well as a paywall.

### Gaps
- No primary design write-up was read for Tiimo's, Structured's or TickTick's actual data models; the model above is inferred from reviews.
- Exact tab labels and counts for each app were not verified.
- No source was found for how Tiimo or Structured hand a task from the timeline into a focus timer step by step.

## 4. Which specific visual-design choices give these apps a recognisable identity, and what separates distinctive from generic?

### Takeaway
Recognisable apps commit to one ownable visual idea and carry it everywhere: a colour-block timeline, a single iconic shape, a hand-drawn cast, a restricted palette, or a borrowed real-world vernacular. Generic ones assemble standard components with no such idea. Evidence on typography specifically is missing.

### Cited Findings
- Colour as structure: Tiimo's day is "a timeline of soothing colors" [P]; press coverage describes the blocks as colour-coded and emoji-accented [S; page not identified, candidate linked]. — [Apple Developer, App Store Awards 2025](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-store-awards/); [ZDNet via Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/apple-intelligence/articles/apples-iphone-app-ai-tool-123900823.html)

- Icon or emoji per task, suggested automatically: Tiimo matches an emoji to typed text; Streaks suggests from about 500 icons as you type. [S/P] — [Built for Mars](https://builtformars.com/company/tiimo); [Apple Developer spotlight: Streaks](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=67dti69d)
- Completion as a physical event: Tiimo drops completed tasks as balls from the top of the screen; Llama Life plays a celebratory animation; (Not Boring) Habits makes the checkbox a press-and-hold with "explosive 3D animations, custom sounds, and playful haptics", tuned over "thousands of iterations" so the haptic tells you to keep holding. - Hand-made over vector: Pok Pok Playroom is "not vector art. It's all hand-drawn and hand-animated", limited to "only these three colors: red, yellow and blue", with every sound recorded from real objects and designed to be "heard a number of times without becoming fatiguing"; "We didn't want anything loud and overstimulating." [P] — [Apple Developer: Behind the Design: Pok Pok Playroom, 9 Aug 2021](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=5bcex7xf)
- Art first: Bears Gratitude began from a set of hand-drawn characters and uses a non-standard home screen of prompt cards flipped left and right. "It's definitely a nonstandard UX", but "the first thing users did was flip through the cards." [P] — [Apple Developer: Bears Gratitude](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=i74v3f4r)
- Borrowed vernacular: Flighty has "a look that mirrors time-honored airport design conventions"; Halide's UI "mimics a film camera"; Katha Room's UI is "drawn from the Gond art form — its intricate patterns and vibrant colors". [P] — [Apple Design Awards 2023](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2023/); [Apple Design Awards 2022](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2022/); [Apple Design Awards 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/)
- Restraint as identity: Lumy is cited for "a simple design, carefully curated color palette"; Things shows areas in bold with projects beneath in a lighter weight. [P/S] — [Apple Design Awards 2025](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/); [CRM.org: Things 3 review](https://crm.org/news/things-3-review)
- A named metaphor in the controls: Goblin Tools calls its granularity control "spiciness". [S] — [DataStudios explainer](https://www.datastudios.org/post/goblin-tools-magic-todo-s-task-breakdown-mechanism-and-pricing-explained)
- The case against sameness, from a designer: "There's been a coalescence over the last few years about what product should look like… I believe we need people who want to explore new territory and find new patterns." [P] — [Apple Developer: (Not Boring) Habits](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9ab1g4r3)
- Accessibility floor any identity must clear (Apple): body text default 17 pt and never below 11 pt on iPhone; let text enlarge by at least 200%; contrast 4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt and 3:1 for 18 pt or bold; never signal by colour alone ("distinct shapes or icons, in addition to color"); controls 44×44 pt by default; under Reduce Motion, replace slides with fades and tighten springs. [P] — [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

### Inferences
- Distinctive: one idea a stranger could describe in a sentence (the red disk, the bean that knits, the coloured day strip, the three-colour playroom), applied to the core object and the completion moment, with sound and haptics tuned to match. Generic: default components, a stock accent colour, stock icons, confetti added at the end.
- The identity usually lives in the task object itself (its colour, icon and shape on the timeline), so data entry produces the look for free.
- Calm and distinctive are compatible: Tiimo, Pok Pok and Headspace are all praised for being quiet. The restraint is in palette size, motion and sound, not in the absence of character.
- Colour-coded timelines must pair colour with an icon or label to satisfy "not colour alone"; the emoji-per-block pattern does that.

### Gaps
- No source was retrieved on typefaces for any of these apps (custom or system), nor on specific hex palettes, corner radii or motion curves. Screenshots and Mobbin-style teardowns could not be opened, so no screenshot links are offered beyond the write-ups cited.
- No design-system or rebrand case study was found for Tiimo; the search returned unrelated brands.
- Finch's visual language is described only at the level of "virtual bird" in the sources read; the three Pratt critiques and a ScreensDesign onboarding article exist but were not opened. — [ScreensDesign: Finch onboarding](https://screensdesign.com/articles/finch-app-onboarding-design/)

## 5. Which apps handle medication or symptom logging, and is it kept separate from productivity tools?

### Takeaway
In the sources retrieved, medication and symptom logging lives in separate, dedicated apps (Bearable, and a 2026 crop of ADHD medication trackers), not inside the planners; the closest a planner comes is Tiimo's mood and energy check-in.

### Cited Findings
- Bearable tracks symptoms, mood, sleep and medication with reminders and is positioned for "understanding the impact of different treatments and medication"; a comparison calls it "generic by design, not specific to ADHD or any one prescription". [S] — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bearable); [ADHDose comparison page (vendor)](https://adhdose-site.pages.dev/adhd-medication-tracker-apps.html)
- New ADHD-specific medication trackers model a predicted effect curve rather than only logging: ADHDose says it uses pharmacokinetic modelling "calibrated to your specific medication, dose and timing"; FocusCurve models build-up and wear-off with multi-dose presets; Medly notifies when effects are "building, at their peak, and wearing off". These are vendor claims; no validation studies were cited. [S] — [ADHDose (vendor)](https://adhdose-site.pages.dev/adhd-medication-tracker.html); [App Store: FocusCurve](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id6761119109); [App Store: Medly](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758928146)
- Medisafe is described as plain reminders and logging with no model of the drug's course; one blog claims its free tier now caps at two medications (unconfirmed). [S] — [ADHD Productivity blog](https://adhd-productivity.pages.dev/blog/best-pill-reminder-apps-adhd-medisafe-2026/)
- The pairing pattern, stated by a vendor: many adults use a medication tracker alongside a CBT programme such as Inflow, with Bearable alongside for broader health logging. [S; vendor page, not independent] — [ADHDose comparison page](https://adhdose-site.pages.dev/adhd-medication-tracker-apps.html)
- Tiimo connects mood and energy to the schedule through Apple's State of Mind API, inside the planner. [P] — [Apple Developer: Tiimo story](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo)
- Daylio and How We Feel log mood and emotions only. [S/P] — [Choosing Therapy: Daylio](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/daylio-app-review/); [Apple Design Awards 2024](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)
- Inflow is a learning programme, not a log. [S] — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/id1528183849?l=en-gb)
- Routinery's listing carries an explicit "not a medical device" statement. [S] — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/habit-reminder-routine-timer/id1450486923)

### Inferences
- None of the planner or timer apps reviewed (Tiimo, Structured, Things, TickTick, Sunsama, Amazing Marvin, Llama Life, Routinery, Goblin Tools, Focus Friend) showed medication logging in the material retrieved. This is absence of evidence from summaries, not a verified feature audit.
- The market separates three jobs: planning the day, logging health, and learning skills. An app that holds more than one keeps them apart (Tiimo keeps mood as a check-in, not a clinical record).
- The 2026 curve-modelling trackers present predicted "peak" and "wear-off" times computed by the app. That is a different category from a descriptive diary of what the person reported, and it is where regulatory exposure would sit.

### Gaps
- No independent review was found of any ADHD-specific medication tracker; all descriptions are vendor or store text.
- Whether any all-in-one ADHD app offers an opt-in medication module could not be established.
- How Bearable presents medication versus symptoms in its navigation was not verified.

## 6. Business models: one-off vs subscription vs freemium; 2026 prices; what users accept as fair; generous free cores; open-source apps with paid extras

### Takeaway
Subscriptions dominate, but the best-liked apps pair a usable free core with either a low annual price or a lifetime unlock, and the loudest complaints are about auto-renewal after trials, price confusion and free tiers that were later cut back. Open-source apps on the stores fund themselves through optional premium, a paid store build, sponsorship or name-your-price.

### Cited Findings
- One-off purchase: Things 3 (US$9.99 iPhone, US$19.99 iPad, no in-app purchases); Goblin Tools (US$1.99, website free); Time Timer (free core, about €9.95 full unlock). [S] — [App Pricing Lab: Things 3](https://www.apppricinglab.com/app/apple/904237743); [App Store: Goblin Tools](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6449003064); [App Store (France): Time Timer](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/time-timer/id332520417?l=en-GB)
- Subscription with a lifetime option: Structured (£5.99/month, £17.99/year, £59.99 lifetime in the UK, August 2026); Daylio (US$4.99/month, US$35.99/year, US$59.99 lifetime per a tracker). [S] — [Cool Curation](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk); [Choosing Therapy: Daylio](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/daylio-app-review/); [App Pricing Lab: Daylio](https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1194023242)
- Generous free core, paid extras: Finch (core free, Plus mostly customisation); Focus Friend (free, ad-free by intent, Pro US$1.99/month or US$14.99/year); TickTick (free plan "generous", Premium about US$28–36/year); Focusmate (3 free sessions a week); Gentler Streak (core tracking free, subscription for premium, bootstrapped). [S/P] — [Headway: Finch](https://makeheadway.com/blog/finch-app-review/); [App Store: Focus Friend](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id6742278016); [Lifestack: TickTick pricing](https://lifestack.ai/blog/ticktick-pricing); [Lifestack: Focusmate pricing](https://lifestack.ai/blog/focusmate-pricing); [Apple Developer: Gentler Streak](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3m0ht22s)
- Subscription only, higher price: Sunsama (about US$20–25/month, no free plan); Inflow (about US$200/year or US$48/month); Amazing Marvin (US$12/month or US$96/year); Joon (US$12.99/month, US$80–90/year); Brili (US$49.99/year); Bearable (US$34.99/year). [S] — [Morgen: Sunsama](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/sunsama-pricing); [Neurolaunch: coaching apps](https://neurolaunch.com/adhd-coaching-app/); [Saner.ai: Amazing Marvin](https://blog.saner.ai/amazing-marvin-review/); [Choosing Therapy: Joon](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/joon-app-review/); [App Store: Brili](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brili-routines-visual-time-r/id952895165); [Choosing Therapy: Bearable](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/bearable-app-review/)
- Hardware plus subscription: Goally, about US$189–249 for the tablet plus US$15–20/month. [S] — [Neurolaunch: Goally](https://neurolaunch.com/goally-review/)
- What users object to: auto-renewal after a free trial in an ADHD product ("people with ADHD will forget about it"); a free tier that was narrowed after the fact (Cozi's 30-day calendar window since May 2024); and multiple inconsistent prices for one app (Tiimo, Structured's UK listing). [S] — [Trustpilot: Inflow](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/getinflow.io?page=5); [Unanswered: Cozi pricing](https://unanswered.io/guide/cozi-app-pricing-free-vs-paid); [Cool Curation](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)
- On paying for design: "A lot of people might say, 'Oh I don't want to pay for that.' But maybe it gives you new experiences — or maybe you just want to open your app and feel good." [P] — [Apple Developer: (Not Boring) Habits](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9ab1g4r3)
- Open source with optional premium: KeePassium calls itself "a commercial open source app" (GPLv3), free with no ads; "An optional premium subscription enables a few convenience and time-saving features, and supports the project." It notes a GPL and App Store conflict and so does not accept outside code contributions. [P] — [GitHub: KeePassium](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium)
- Open source, free everywhere, funded by sponsors and an optional sync service: Super Productivity (MIT) is on the App Store, Google Play and F-Droid, says it "always will be" free, is funded through GitHub Sponsors, and offers its own end-to-end encrypted, self-hostable sync alongside Dropbox and WebDAV. [P] — [GitHub: super-productivity](https://github.com/johannesjo/super-productivity)
- Open source with name-your-price: Tasks.org (GPL-3.0; Google Play and F-Droid) offers subscriptions at a price the user chooses, from US$0.99/year, with sync excluded at the lowest tier; GitHub Sponsors is an alternative. [P for licence and stores; S for pricing] — [GitHub: tasks/tasks](https://github.com/tasks/tasks); [Tasks.org: subscriptions](https://www.tasks.org/docs/subscribe.html)

### Inferences
- The pattern users describe as fair: the daily core works for free with no account; payment buys depth, cosmetics, sync or long-range planning; the price is either small and annual (roughly US$15–40) or a one-time unlock (roughly US$2–60); and trials do not silently convert.
- For a free, open-source app with on-device data, the workable store models seen are: free with optional supporter purchase (KeePassium, Tasks.org), or free everywhere with sponsorship (Super Productivity). A paid store build subsidising a free one (Anki) works but attracts resentment.
- A GPL-family licence and App Store distribution need care: KeePassium flags the conflict explicitly and limits contributions because of it.

### Gaps
- No vendor pricing page was opened, so every commercial price above is second-hand and several conflict.
- No survey or study of what ADHD users consider a fair price was found; "what users accept" rests on reviews and complaints.
- OsmAnd, Habitica, Loop Habit Tracker and other open-source examples were not checked.

## 7. How do on-device-only apps handle backup and moving to a new phone, and how do privacy-first health apps present app lock and biometrics?

### Takeaway
Without accounts, apps lean on the operating system's own device backup (iCloud Backup, Android Auto Backup) plus an optional user-held file or user-chosen storage provider; app lock is presented as a simple toggle with a passcode first and biometrics as a convenience, using the system's own prompt.

### Cited Findings
- Android Auto Backup: on by default for apps targeting Android 6+; stores to a private folder in the user's Google Drive, capped at 25 MB per app, not counted against quota; only the latest backup is kept; "end-to-end encrypted on devices running Android 9 or higher using the device's PIN, pattern, or password"; runs roughly nightly when idle on Wi-Fi; restores automatically on install or during device set-up. Over 25 MB, nothing is backed up. [P] — [Android Developers: Auto Backup](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup)
- Android lets an app insist on encryption: `disableIfNoEncryptionCapabilities` (or `requireFlags="clientSideEncryption"` on older versions) stops cloud backup if the device has no screen lock, while direct device-to-device transfer still works because it never touches a server. An app can also opt out with `allowBackup="false"` "if your app deals with sensitive information", though on Android 12+ some manufacturers still allow device-to-device transfer. [P] — [Android Developers: Auto Backup](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup)
- New in Android 16 QPR2: a `<cross-platform-transfer platform="ios">` rule lets an Android app export its data to, and import from, its matching iOS app during a phone switch, matched by bundle ID and team ID. [P] — [Android Developers: Auto Backup](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup)
- iOS: iCloud Backup includes app data except `tmp` and `Library/Caches`; files can be flagged `isExcludedFromBackup`, but Apple says the flag is advisory ("not a mechanism to guarantee those items never appear in a backup or on a restored device"). User-created data should stay in the backup because "it might be difficult, even impossible, for the user to re-create". [P] — [Apple Developer: Optimizing your app's data for iCloud Backup](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/optimizing-your-app-s-data-for-icloud-backup)
- User-chosen storage instead of a vendor server: Super Productivity needs no account, lets people choose where data lives, and syncs through Dropbox, WebDAV or its own end-to-end encrypted, self-hostable service "with a password only the user holds". [P] — [GitHub: super-productivity](https://github.com/johannesjo/super-productivity)
- KeePassium "integrates with the system, does not ask for your storage credentials" and works with iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive and others through the OS file provider. [P] — [GitHub: KeePassium](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium)
- Platform-vendor sync without an account of the app's own: Streaks "securely syncs your history across iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, and Mac via iCloud". [P] — [Apple Developer spotlight: Streaks](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=67dti69d)
- Vendor cloud: Things uses its own Things Cloud; Bearable stores data encrypted on its servers with in-app export and deletion. [P/S] — [Apple Developer: Things story](https://developer.apple.com/articles/things/); [Google Play: Bearable](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bearable)
- Daylio: no account needed on the free tier; one listing places automatic cloud backup and PIN lock under Premium. [S] — [IntuitionLabs: Daylio](https://intuitionlabs.ai/software/telepsychiatry-digital-mental-health/mood-tracking-and-journaling/daylio)
- App lock, as worded by a privacy-first app (KeePassium): "App Lock protects the app itself, by covering any in-app screens. By default, AppLock requires a passcode (of any complexity). For convenience, you can configure the app to use Face ID/Touch ID instead." A separate timeout closes the data "after a failed AppLock attempt". [P] — [GitHub: KeePassium](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium)
- Euki, a privacy-first period tracker (GPL-3.0): "All user data stays on device", "The App does not collect any user data", with "data deletion, offline-friendly functionality, and PIN protection". [P] — [GitHub: Euki-iOS](https://github.com/Euki-Inc/Euki-iOS)
- Android's guidance on the lock prompt: use the system `BiometricPrompt`, a "system-provided dialog [that] is consistent across the apps that use it, creating a more trustworthy user experience"; allow fallback to the device PIN, pattern or password via `BIOMETRIC_STRONG or DEVICE_CREDENTIAL`; for low-risk unlocks, `setConfirmationRequired(false)` lets a face match open the app without an extra tap; a key can be bound to authentication so data is cryptographically locked, not just hidden. [P] — [Android Developers: Biometric authentication](https://developer.android.com/identity/sign-in/biometric-auth)
- On-device AI as a privacy position: Apple's Foundation Models framework offers "an on-device Apple Foundation Model capable of text generation tasks" with structured output and tool calling, on iOS 26+ devices that support Apple Intelligence; Android's Gemini Nano runs "without needing a network connection or sending data to the cloud" through AICore, with ML Kit GenAI APIs for prompts and structured output, subject to device support and token limits. [P] — [Apple Developer: Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels); [Android Developers: Gemini Nano](https://developer.android.com/ai/gemini-nano)

### Inferences
- "Nothing leaves the device" and "survives a new phone" pull against each other. The OS device backup is the lowest-friction answer, but it does place a copy in the person's iCloud or Google account; an app claiming on-device-only storage should say so plainly, and on Android can require that the backup be client-side encrypted.
- Apps that add their own passcode-derived encryption face a second problem: an OS-restored database is unreadable if the key lived only in the secure hardware of the old phone. A user-held encrypted export file avoids this and also covers iOS-to-Android moves on phones older than Android 16 QPR2.
- The 25 MB Android cap is comfortably above what a text-only diary needs, but an app must watch it if it stores images.
- Lock presentation that reads as trustworthy: off by default, offered once during set-up, passcode first with biometrics as an add-on, the system dialog rather than a custom one, a cover screen in the app switcher, and a clear statement of what the lock does and does not protect.
- On-device models are available only on recent hardware, so any AI breakdown must be an enhancement over a manual checklist, never a dependency.

### Gaps
- How We Feel's storage, backup and lock behaviour could not be verified.
- Daylio's and Bearable's actual export formats and restore flows were not read from vendor documentation.
- Apple's guidance on Face ID prompts (LocalAuthentication) and on hiding content in the app switcher was not retrieved.
- No source was found describing how any on-device ADHD planner words its "your data stays here, here is how to move it" screen.

## 8. How do apps serve one person managing their own tasks and a child's routines (profiles, household view, shared routines, handing over the phone, child mode)?

### Takeaway
Three architectures exist: two linked apps (Joon), a dedicated child device (Goally), and one app with a gated parent mode and kid mode (Brili). Only the third works with no account and no server. No app in the set was found to combine an adult's own planner and a child's routines well in one product.

### Cited Findings
- Joon: parent app and child game are separate, linked by a login code, and best on two devices; every completion needs parent verification, which reviewers say can become a burden. [S] — [Choosing Therapy: Joon](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/joon-app-review/); [Neurolaunch: Joon](https://neurolaunch.com/joon-adhd-app-kids/)
- Brili: Parent Mode builds routines; Kid Mode plays them as a game showing what is next and how much time is left; a user-set 4-digit code guards the parent side; all children are managed under one parent account; a review describes parents switching profiles and handing the child control of their own checklist. [S] — [App Store: Brili Routines – Visual Timer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brili-routines-visual-time-r/id952895165); [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/brili-routines)
- Brili later shipped a separate adult-oriented app, and at least one user found its "digital achievements" less motivating than the original's real-world rewards. [S] — [App Store: Brili Routines – Habit Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1516036620)
- Goally: the child gets a single-purpose tablet; the parent manages it from an app; praised as distraction-free. [S] — [Neurolaunch: Goally](https://neurolaunch.com/goally-review/)
- Cozi: one account shared by the whole household, one price covering everyone. [S] — [Unanswered: Cozi pricing](https://unanswered.io/guide/cozi-app-pricing-free-vs-paid)
- S'moresUp: began as chores for children, then opened to partners, grandparents and nannies; asks only for "a nickname and age". [P] — [Apple Developer spotlight: S'moresUp](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=h8wvx2p9)
- Tiimo began as a tool for children and adolescents with ADHD and is now used mostly by adults. [P] — [Apple Developer: Tiimo story](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo)
- What Apple praises on child-facing surfaces: Sago Mini Jinja's Garden's "interactions that require no reading" and "no timer, no to-do lists"; Pok Pok Playroom's quiet sound, three-colour palette and open-ended play ("We didn't want anything loud and overstimulating"). [P] — [Apple Design Awards 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/); [Apple Developer: Pok Pok Playroom](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=5bcex7xf)
- Apple's control-size guidance: 44×44 pt default targets, with spacing treated "as important as size". [P] — [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

### Inferences
- For an offline, no-account app the Brili shape is the only one available: profiles stored locally, a parent-set code, and a kid mode that shows only today's routine, a timer and rewards, with the code required to leave.
- A hand-over model avoids collecting anything from the child and needs only a nickname, in line with S'moresUp's choice.
- The split Brili made (a kids' product and a separate adult product) and Tiimo's drift from children to adults suggest that one interface rarely serves both; a shared engine with two distinct surfaces is the more defensible design.
- Parent verification of every step (Joon) trades child autonomy for parent workload; a hand-over surface where the child ticks steps and the parent reviews later avoids the notification burden.
- Coins, stars and pets are the default motivators in this segment. A product that restricts rewards to a parent-run chart would be a deliberate departure and should expect comparison with them.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed whether Tiimo, Structured, Finch or TickTick offer child profiles, family sharing or a hand-over mode today.
- How any app pins the screen for a child (iOS Guided Access, Android app pinning) was not sourced; Apple and Google support pages were not reachable.
- Skylight, Hearth and other shared-display family products, and Choiceworks-style visual schedule apps, were not researched.
- No independent evaluation was found of how well a single device shared between parent and child works in practice.
