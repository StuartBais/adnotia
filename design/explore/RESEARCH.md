# What the research found

Four research sweeps ran before any direction was drawn, in November 2026: Apple, Google, the best-regarded planning and focus apps, and the best-regarded calm and wellbeing apps. Each returned the principles below and a list of things to avoid. The ten directions in this folder were briefed from them.

These are the agents' summaries of what they read, kept as they returned them. Where a principle quotes a guideline, the quotation has not been checked again by a person.

## Apple's design language as of late 2026: iOS 26 Liquid Glass and its iOS 27 refinements, the current Human Interface Guidelines (typography, layout, navigation, colour, accessibility), Apple's own apps (Reminders, Calendar, Clock, Fitness, Journal, Notes and Music as reference screens), and the Apple Design Award winners and finalists of 2025 and 2026 in Visuals and Graphics, Interaction and Inclusivity, plus a few in other categories that bear on an ADHD planner.

### Principles

- Keep two layers. Content (tasks, the day plan, the current step) lives in the content layer. Navigation and actions float above it as capsules. Apple: 'Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer'; hierarchy comes from 'layout and grouping' rather than 'extra backgrounds or borders'. This answers the 'thin-bordered cards' complaint directly: group with space, shape and fill, not 1 px outlines.
- Put brand colour in the content layer and use the accent sparingly. HIG Branding (September 2026) says to keep the brand colour off most controls, use it for 'primary actions or status indicators', and 'consider moving it into the content layer'. HIG Color says to tint the background of the one primary action, never every control.
- Allow one prominent action per view, two at most (HIG Buttons). When offering a set of choices, mark the preferred one by style, not by size.
- Use concentric geometry. Capsule radius is half the height; an inner radius is the outer radius minus the padding. Near the phone's edge, use a capsule with extra margin. Watch for corners that look 'pinched' or 'flared'. On the web this can be implemented as CSS custom properties.
- Typography: Apple's ideal is the system font with Dynamic Type. Body 17 pt, nothing below 11 pt, no Light or Thin weights, bold left-aligned titles, and title-case section headers (iOS 26 dropped all-caps headers). A custom font is acceptable for headlines only if it supports Dynamic Type and Bold Text. Apple suggests a custom headline face with system body type.
- Use icons in bars, but text where an icon is ambiguous. Apple's example is that a pencil or a checkmark can be misread as Select or Edit, and 'a text label is always the better choice'. Every icon needs an accessibility label.
- Progress should be determinate wherever possible: rings fill clockwise, bars fill from leading to trailing, and the pace is even (HIG). A timer's minimal Live Activity shows the remaining time, not a logo.
- Cognitive accessibility, quoted from the HIG: 'Minimize use of time-boxed interface elements' (no auto-dismissing views); prefer system gestures to custom ones; under Assistive Access, 'a single interaction per screen' and confirm destructive actions twice. Respond to Reduce Motion by swapping movement for fades and tightening springs.
- Every custom colour needs light, dark and increased-contrast versions, and test them against Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast. Apple requires this even for single-appearance apps.
- Simplicity is not minimalism, and delight is not decoration (Apple's design principles, June 2026). Sometimes simplicity means adding context: Apple's example is a play/pause control that also shows the time left. Delight comes from choosing the emotion (for Adnotia, calm and capable) and reinforcing it, 'not by adding confetti'.
- Agency and forgiveness: no forced flows, easy undo, permission requests only when needed and with a reason given (Apple's 2026 principles). This fits Adnotia's local-only, no-accounts stance.
- Apple's own apps break Adnotia's rules in places, so do not copy them wholesale. iOS 27's Apple list includes 'More intuitive journaling streaks' for Journal. Fitness is built on rings and awards. Tab bar badges are red ovals. Take Apple's structure and controls, not its engagement mechanics.
- Inference about the owner's palette (contrast computed with the WCAG formula). Charcoal #222624 on white is 15.3:1, on stone 13.4:1 and on sage 12.3:1. Muted #595C5B on white is 6.8:1, on stone 5.9:1 and on sage 5.4:1. Terracotta #F28C5E with charcoal on it, or as text on charcoal, is 6.3:1, which passes. White text on terracotta, or terracotta text on white, is only 2.4:1 (2.1:1 on stone), which fails. Apple's own orange (#FF8D28) is 2.3:1 on white, which is why Apple puts accent colours behind glyphs rather than using them as text. So: a terracotta-filled primary action carries a charcoal glyph or label. Use terracotta for the 'now' marker, the time-remaining fill and the one primary capture button, and never for body text on light grounds. Sage #DFE9E0 maps naturally to Apple's selected-tab highlight pill, and stone #EEF0EC to a grouped-background ground behind white sections.

### Avoid

- Imitation Liquid Glass and glassmorphism done in CSS or by hand: backdrop blur, fake specular sheen, colour fringing, wobbly refraction. Apple renders the real thing natively and retuned it within a year (iOS 26 to 27 changed the defaults, the edges and the highlights), so copies look fake quickly and usually fail contrast. Inference: if Adnotia stays a wrapped web app, take Liquid Glass's structure (floating capsules, concentric shapes, grouping, one tinted primary action) and render it as solid, opaque fills.
- Clear glass over busy content, and text over text. NN/g's central complaint; Apple itself made the default more tinted in iOS 27.
- An animation on every tap: morphing, jiggling, shimmering controls. NN/g calls it 'motion without meaning' and Six Colors finds it 'distracting' after 'the fifteenth time'. It also works against an ADHD audience.
- Tab bars that collapse to a single icon on scroll, a search button split away from the tab bar (Apple reversed this in its own apps in iOS 27), and back buttons with no label. These are less discoverable, and Apple is already walking some of them back.
- Mesh-gradient backgrounds and 'playlist art' gradients (Denim, a 2025 Delight finalist). Fashionable, and tied to 2025–26.
- AI sparkle buttons and generated prompts scattered through the interface. MacStories found Journal's iOS 27 generated questions sometimes 'creepy' or 'nonsensical'. Not relevant to an offline app anyway.
- Streaks, rings, gems and awards, even 'gentle' ones (Apple Journal streaks, Fitness awards, Opal's 'gems', Gentler Streak's rest-friendly streaks). Banned by Adnotia's rules, and the category is moving away from them.
- Heavy stylised identities: hand-drawn (grug), retro-skeuomorphic hardware (the (Not Boring) Camera, 2026 Visuals finalist), editorial or newspaper looks. They win awards as statements, but they date quickly and pull against a calm, practical tool. The owner has already rejected one such look.

## Google's design language as of late 2026: Material 3 Expressive (tokens, shape morphing, emphasised type, colour roles, spring motion, button groups, floating toolbar), how Google's own apps (Clock, Keep, Tasks, Calendar, Fitbit/Google Health, Photos) adopted it, and Google Play Best of 2025/2026 picks relevant to a focus/task app

### Principles

- Spend emphasis on one hero per screen. Material's own guidance keeps hero moments to one or two per product. For Adnotia that means the 'now' item on Today and the current step on Focus: largest type, brightest container, nearest the thumb. Everything else visibly steps down in size and tone.
- Make the key action findable by size, placement and containment, not decoration. Google's 4x-faster result came from a bigger button near the keyboard in a distinct colour, which argues for capture as an 80dp medium FAB (rounded square, about 20dp corners) in the bottom-right on every screen.
- Keep text labels on actions. Google measured lower usability when email actions lost their labels, and Clock replaced its play glyph with the word 'Start'. Icon-only controls should be the exception, with an accessible name.
- Familiar structure beats novelty. A scrolling list, a bottom bar and a sheet for detail should stay where people expect them; Google's helter-skelter layout tested as modern and unusable. Distinctiveness should come from palette, shape and motion, not from rearranged patterns.
- Use shape as a second channel for state, which also satisfies 'nothing by colour alone'. In M3E a pressed button's corners tighten, a selected toggle swaps round for square, and a connected group's selected segment rounds. Pair the shape with a label, or with aria-pressed on the web.
- Motion with springs, not easing curves. Spatial springs may overshoot slightly and effects springs (colour, opacity) never do. Use the Standard scheme (damping 0.9) for a utility app and save the Expressive bounce (damping 0.6 to 0.8) for one moment, such as completing a step. Screen transitions should not bounce, and reduced-motion must switch off morphing.
- Ticking numbers use tabular figures in a fixed-width box, tested at the largest text size. Google Clock's 2025 redesign jittered every second because its digits changed width.
- Restraint is evidenced, not just tasteful. Google's own study found a strong minority preferred calmer, less intense versions. Critics called Google's apps over-containerised, so a task list can be one container with dividers instead of a card per row.
- Respect component roles. The FAB is for the single most important action, and a FAB menu holds 2 to 6 related items. The floating toolbar is for contextual actions on the current page and pairs with a FAB, not with navigation. The bottom bar (64dp, pill indicator) holds at most five destinations.
- Map the palette onto M3 colour roles by contrast, never by colour psychology. Charcoal #222624 is the 'vibrant' container with white content (15.3:1). Terracotta #F28C5E is a container for the single 'now' element with charcoal content (6.34:1), and it is never text or an icon on white or stone (2.42:1 and 2.11:1, which fail even the 3:1 bar for UI components). Stone #EEF0EC is the page surface and white the card (surfaceContainerLowest). Sage #DFE9E0 is the selected-indicator pill (charcoal on it is 12.3:1). Muted #595C5B is secondary text (6.76:1 on white, 5.89:1 on stone).

### Avoid

- Decorative MaterialShapes (cookie, clover, burst, puffy, 'very sunny') used as avatars, badges or backgrounds. They are the signature of 2025-era Android and will read as 'Android 16 stock' within a couple of years. If used at all, keep one shape for one meaning.
- Wavy progress indicators. Google itself says they suit only 'when a more expressive style is appropriate', and they change the component's height.
- XL buttons (136dp) and giant pill buttons, such as Meet's call buttons and Clock's Stopwatch, which reviewers called 'comical' and out of proportion with the rest of the interface.
- Over-containerisation: every list row in its own card. Google Drive backed out of it once, and Gboard lost density to it.
- Floating pill navigation bars hovering over content, especially translucent or Liquid Glass ones. Google kept navigation docked and gave the floating toolbar to page actions, and Fitbit removed its floating toolbar.
- Bounce on everything, including screen transitions and colour changes. Material's own transition guidance says to avoid bouncy springs there.
- Wallpaper-derived Dynamic Color as the identity. It is Android-only, Google does not ship it on iOS, Android 17 is adding user colour pickers and intensity presets on top of it, and it would override the owner's chosen palette.
- Morphing loading indicators for waits that an offline app does not have. Nothing in Adnotia loads from a network, so this is ornament.
- Companion characters with a currency, as in Focus Friend's bean and socks. The style is popular now, but it also breaks Adnotia's no-guilt and no-points rules.
- Launch 'glows' and AI-style gradients, such as the glow on launch in the Google Home app.

## Best-in-class productivity, planning and focus app interfaces, 2025 to 2026: Things 3, Structured, Tiimo, Sunsama, Akiflow, Amie, Notion Calendar, Linear Mobile, Todoist, TickTick, Llama Life, Opal, Focus Friend, Session, Flow and Endel. The aim is to inform a phone-first redesign of Adnotia that is neither generic nor editorial.

### Principles

- Give the app one signature object that carries the central idea, which is time, and keep everything else quiet. Each leader has one: Structured's capsule whose height is the task's length, Things' Magic Plus and Today star, Llama Life's end-time divider, Session's dial, Tiimo's Now/Next ring. The rejected first build had none; every element was a bordered card of equal weight. (This principle is my inference from the set.)
- Separate things with space, weight and size, not lines. Linear's 2026 principles: 'Structure should be felt not seen' and 'Don’t compete for attention you haven’t earned'. Things draws to-dos with no card at all, and Tiimo separates cards with shadow, not borders.
- Draw 'now' as a different kind of object, not a highlighted row. Structured fills only the current capsule, drains it as time passes, and replaces the time range with '19m remaining'. Tiimo writes 'Now:' with a live '9:33 left'. Everything else stays outline or quiet.
- Encode duration as a length or an angle, and also say it in words. Examples: capsule height (Structured), a ring around the task icon (Tiimo), a wedge (Session, Llama Life), a bar (Sunsama).
- Show capacity as a total and a projected finish, never as a debt. Examples: '6 tasks • 2h 45m' (Todoist), the '6:00' planned chip (Sunsama), 'End time 3:47pm' with a movable divider (Llama Life). Users single out the end time as what keeps them realistic.
- Offer flexible order alongside fixed slots. A Llama Life user: 'What if I don't want to start vacuuming at 10:00? Then I feel like a failure!' An ordered list with a running finish time avoids that, without giving up visible time.
- Make capture a persistent object within thumb reach, outside the tab set, and let it open minimal. Examples: Things' draggable Magic Plus; Structured's + floating beside its tab bar; Todoist Quick Add 'opens clean. Task actions appear after you start typing'; Structured's editor that starts 'with the essentials'.
- Deal with what did not happen through a calm review, one item at a time, instead of red lists. Examples: Structured Replan (swipe for done, later, inbox or delete), Tiimo's daily review, Sunsama's shutdown. Things' red badge and flag, and the red today badge in Notion Calendar, are the patterns to leave out.
- Use one accent for one meaning: Things blue only for action and selection, Flow green, Session red. For Adnotia, terracotta could mean 'now' and 'add'. I computed #F28C5E at about 2.4:1 against white, so it must be a fill under charcoal (about 6.3:1), never white-on-terracotta and never terracotta text. Several admired apps fail here, so do not copy their accents literally: Structured's white + on #F29D97 is about 2.1:1, Llama Life's teal tag text about 1.5:1, Tiimo's #9F85FF bar about 2.8:1.
- Make tasks recognisable through glyphs: a large round tile per task (Structured, Tiimo) speeds scanning. Use inline SVG glyphs rather than emoji, always pair them with text, and never let colour alone carry the meaning.
- Put navigation at the bottom as a floating bar of five items or fewer, with content scrolling beneath it. Use glass only on that layer, with fallbacks for Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency; Linear mirrors iOS's solid outlines. Apple describes controls as 'a distinct functional layer that sits above apps'.
- Use motion as brief, local, physical feedback: Things' Magic Plus deforming as it is dragged, Linear's lift on touch and stretch at the edge, Structured's drag into the timeline. Keep celebrations small and optional, and honour reduced motion.
- Make the key action bigger and more prominent. Google's Material 3 Expressive eye-tracking study found key elements 'up to four times faster', and older users (45+) performing on par with younger ones. This supports a large Now card and a large capture button.
- Build the focus screen in this order: an intention first (Session's 'What's your focus?'), the current step largest with done and upcoming steps visible (Tiimo's subtask timers), the time as a shape plus a number, and adjustments of plus or minus 5 (Llama Life). Let the timer collapse so the plan can be edited (Structured 4.5).
- Set headings in a large, bold system sans with tight tracking for the day or date (Structured's '16. October 2025', Notion Calendar's 'January', Things' 'Today', Sunsama's 'Monday'). It reads as modern, not editorial. Tiimo's serif is the exception among these apps, and the owner has already rejected that look.
- Do not take wellbeing claims at face value. Opal says it is 'guilt-free' while shipping streaks, a score and 'Culprits'. Tiimo is 'gentle' while shipping trophies. Focus Friend's pet becomes 'really really sad'. Judge an interface by what it draws, not by what its marketing says.

### Avoid

- Refraction, specular shine and liquid distortion on content rather than chrome. Apple's own Liquid Glass changed through the beta, and Linear skipped refraction because it hurts legibility.
- 3D-rendered hero objects and mascot orbs, such as Opal's glowing gemstone and Akiflow's gradient 'Aki' orb.
- Purple-gradient 'AI sparkle' tabs and buttons as a main navigation destination (Structured's AI tab, Tiimo's AI co-planner, Akiflow).
- Confetti bursts and chimes on every completion (Llama Life, Tiimo). The novelty wears off, and they are noise for a tired user.
- Virtual pets and decorating economies (Focus Friend). A reviewer reported the room being finished within days, and the pet's sadness is a guilt mechanic.
- Scores, streak flames, trophies and 'culprits' (Opal, Tiimo Streaks, TickTick habits). They are also banned by Adnotia's rules.
- 3D Apple emoji as task icons (Tiimo, Llama Life, TickTick list prefixes). They are also banned for Adnotia.
- Soft 1970s display serifs and 'puffy' wordmarks (Tiimo's Recoleta headline and logo).
- Uppercase, letter-spaced micro-labels used everywhere (Tiimo's group pills, Session's monospace caps).
- Pastel-on-pastel palettes with pale grey metadata that falls below 4.5:1.
- Italic display numerals (Llama Life) and buttons with neon glow (Session's 'START SESSION').

## Visual design of admired wellbeing, habit and calm consumer apps, 2025–2026, and how to tell a premium look from a generic, AI-default one (for the Adnotia phone redesign in charcoal, white and terracotta)

### Principles

- One signature object that is also the information. Every app here that people remember has one drawing that only it would make, and that drawing is the data: Gentler's Activity Path band, How We Feel's shape field, Flighty's board line and progress ring, Tide Guide's sky-coloured curve. For Adnotia (inference), that object is the current step plus remaining time. Draw it the same way on Today, in Focus, in the widget and in the Live Activity, so the app is recognisable with the logo removed.
- Put a plain sentence next to the shape. Gentler sets a daily message directly above its path and translates stats into words 'anyone can understand'. The picture is for a glance; the sentence and the number are for certainty.
- Use a familiar frame and your own content. Gentler's designer warns against leaving 'an obvious mark' with poor UX. Google found that breaking basic patterns (unlabelled actions, helter-skelter lists) made usability worse even when the result looked modern. Keep native tab bars, sheets and Back, and put the distinctiveness into the content layer.
- Let shape and position carry state as well as colour. How We Feel uses jagged edges for high energy and smooth curves for calm, and Gentler uses position in the band. Running, waiting, done and paused should differ by outline, fill or placement, so each still reads in greyscale.
- Put the craft into the most repeated moment, not into milestones. Not Boring designs the checkbox, not a confetti screen. For Adnotia that means capture saving and a step completing. Layer motion, a designed haptic and optional sound, and award nothing.
- Save progress; never reset. Not Boring's 'games solved this decades ago by saving your progress', Gentler's Sick, Injured and On a Break statuses (which also pause notifications) and Finch's Pause mode all treat a gap as a state. Returning should show where you were.
- Consolidate with time. Headspace cut its category tabs down to Today plus Explore; Gentler went from four tabs to three and to a single recommendation; Calm reorganised into four. Fewer, larger elements are how these apps came to look premium.
- Warmth without a serif comes from a characterful sans and warm ink. Headspace's custom Apercu ('playful to clinical', curves that echo the smile) and warm near-black #2D2C2B do the job a serif headline did before. Choose one sans with personality, tabular figures and Dynamic Type support, and set large sizes with tight tracking. Do not justify the choice as an ADHD aid.
- How the colours are distributed matters more than the palette (my calculation from the approved hexes). Terracotta #F28C5E on white measures 2.4:1, on stone #EEF0EC 2.1:1 and on the sage tint 1.9:1. That fails text contrast, and fails the 3:1 non-text minimum too. Against charcoal #222624 it measures 6.3:1, and white on charcoal is 15.3:1. So terracotta belongs on charcoal, or as a fill under charcoal text. That points towards charcoal as the surface for 'now' (the current-step block, Focus), white and stone for the rest of the day, sage tint for done or rest, and terracotta only for the live quantity and marker. Coral buttons on a cream page is the look the owner read as 'Claude'.
- Put the main action at the bottom, large and labelled. Google's expressive Send button above the keyboard was found four times faster, and the 2026 trend pieces agree that the bottom third is where thumbs are. Capture and Start belong there, never in a top bar.
- Use dimension on one meaningful object, not as wallpaper. Airbnb's tilt-responsive translucent card and Not Boring's rotated 3D checkmark show depth used to make one thing feel physical. A whole 3D icon set can now be cloned by AI overnight (Chayka), so keep depth scarce.
- Make personality optional and separate from data. CARROT's voice can be switched off; Headspace keeps Ebb 'not too smiley'. If Adnotia has any character or sound, it is off by default or easy to turn off, it needs nothing from the person, and it never comments on how much they did.

### Avoid

- Hand-made glassmorphism on content: frosted cards over gradients, and text over translucent layers. Apple changes the optics from one beta to the next, and an audit of Show HN launches lists glassmorphism among the tells of a 'vibe-coded' app.
- The 2023–2026 AI-default look: Inter or Roboto, an indigo-to-violet accent (#6366F1, #8B5CF6), a centred hero with one call to action, three icon cards in a row, rounded-2xl with shadow-lg at about 0.1 opacity, a badge above the headline, cards with a coloured left border, numbered 1-2-3 steps, and copy that opens with 'Empower', 'Unlock' or 'Transform'. On a phone (my inference) the equivalents are system font only, a column of identically bordered rounded cards, pill chips, SF Symbols in tinted circles, a 'Good morning' greeting over stat tiles, and a sparkle icon for AI.
- The second-order 'anti-AI' recipe: warm cream ground, serif display face (for example Fraunces), terracotta or ember accent and warm ink. The very dev.to article that teaches how to escape the AI look prescribes exactly that palette and serif ('ember #e8775a', Fraunces). It is becoming a template of its own, and it sits close to Claude's own brand, which is probably why both earlier Adnotia builds read as 'Claude'.
- Skeuomorphic 3D icon sets in the style of Airbnb 2025. Kyle Chayka records AI reproducing them within days, so they stop signalling care almost immediately.
- Aurora, mesh-gradient washes and the AI 'orb' or blob (Headspace's Ebb is a careful version). As a general motif for 'intelligent' features it is already a cliché (inference).
- Mascots with an economy: pets that need energy, currencies, shops and micropets (Finch). Critics already call these cluttered, and for Adnotia they are a guilt mechanic in disguise.
- AI-first features as identity: 'Browse for Me' (Arc Search, whose AI features were disabled on 23 November 2026), 'Plan with AI' (Tiimo), an AI layout assistant (CARROT).
- Streaks, trophies and achievements still marketed in 2026 (Tiimo, Daylio). Excluded for Adnotia anyway, and increasingly out of step with the gentler apps the award juries now reward (Gentler, Structured, Not Boring Habits).
- Bento grids of equal tiles. They appear on nearly every 2026 trend list, and they flatten hierarchy, which works against 'one obvious now'.
- Maximal expressive shape-morphing and oversized wobble on every control. Google's own research found 'a strong minority' preferring calmer versions and a usability cost when patterns break.
- Novel gestures without a visible fallback (long-press-only actions, swipe-only actions). The 2026 guidance treats these as an accessibility failure.
