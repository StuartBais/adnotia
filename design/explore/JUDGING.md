# How the ten were judged

Three judges each looked at all ten boards and scored them out of ten, each through one lens only. They were agents, not people, and they had the owner's palette and the rejected Bookmark Edition as context. Read the scores as a first sort, not a verdict.

- **Modern:** does it look like a premium app shipping in late 2026?
- **Intuitive:** can an adult with ADHD tell within two seconds what is happening now, what is next, how to capture something and how much time is left? Does anything overwhelm, confuse or shame?
- **Distinct:** could it become the brand? Is a screenshot recognisable as this product and no other?

## The modern judge

Top three: lantern, deck, glasshouse

I judged only on visual quality and modernity, after viewing all ten renders in /home/user/adnotia/design/explore/shots/. Lantern is the clear winner. It is the only direction that looks both premium and distinctive while keeping the charcoal and terracotta palette the owner likes, and its large-type, one-bar look carries straight onto the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island. Deck has the most current personality: display type, physical cards and spring motion. It pays for that with a pastel-on-black trend and the loss of terracotta. Glasshouse is the most polished native iOS 26 option but the closest of the leaders to stock iOS, so it needs a stronger signature to escape the "default" criticism. Level is bold but trend-bound, and its giant condensed headlines come close to the headline-led look already rejected. Roundels and Quarters are well crafted but read as a generic wellness app and as a heavy three-ground Today screen respectively. Springform is a literal Material template. Halo and Strata each have one unresolved screen: the cluttered dial and the crude thermometer. Nightline is a generic dark-mode template.

## The intuitive judge

Top three: glasshouse, lantern, nightline

I judged the ten directions only on whether an adult with ADHD can tell, within two seconds, what is happening now, what is next, how to capture something and how much time is left, and on whether anything overwhelms, confuses or shames. Every screenshot was read in full, and the Halo, Glasshouse, Strata and Quarters Today screens were also cropped and enlarged.

1. Glasshouse ranks first because it answers all four questions on every screen and keeps the answers in fixed places: a persistent Now capsule, capture always the same terracotta +, and a step that outranks the clock.
2. Lantern gives the single fastest answer to 'what now', and the drawer always shows the next item. It loses a point because Today and Focus are nearly the same screen and the rest of the day is hidden.
3. Nightline puts the most into words, including 'Next · in 25 min', which no other direction does. Its dark, even-weight rows have to be scanned rather than glanced at.

Roundels has the best capture control, a labelled button that never moves, and Strata's 'length is time' timeline does the most for time-blindness. Each has a hierarchy flaw: in Roundels a picture outranks the step on Today, and in Strata the focus gauge outshouts the step.

The weaker directions fail in common ways:
- **Visuals that need decoding:** Halo's 12-hour dial, Level's water-glass, Springform's state shapes and Deck's meaningless card colours.
- **Capture that moves:** Springform, Deck and Level all move it, often to the top right, out of thumb reach.
- **Now not first:** Quarters puts the anytime list above the Now card.

None of the ten shames the person: no overdue marks, missed counts or breakable streaks. 'Not now', 'Later' and 'Move to later' are used throughout.

Outside this lens: Springform, Nightline, Deck, Level and Roundels do not use the owner's chosen palette.

## The distinct judge

Top three: level, halo, roundels

Lens: distinctiveness and identity. I looked at all ten renders in /home/user/adnotia/design/explore/shots/. Level and Halo are the only two with a single visual idea that carries through every screen and could become the icon and brand mark: the screen as a draining glass, and the day as a dial. Level is the more unmistakable screenshot, but it gives up the owner's palette. Halo keeps the palette, but its focus screen falls back on the common draining-ring timer. Roundels comes third: its disc-plus-pictogram unit is a real system that could last for years, though its pine green reads like a fitness app. Quarters has the most original structural idea (screen colour follows the time of day) but no single brand colour. Lantern is the best on-palette mood but is built from generic parts. Deck and Strata echo existing products (Wallet-style card stacks; Structured's length-as-time timeline). Nightline, Glasshouse and Springform are the least distinctive. Nightline is a generic dark list. Glasshouse and Springform are essentially the iOS 26 and Material 3 Expressive templates, the default-template look the owner already rejected. Caveat: the three most distinctive directions (Level, Roundels, Quarters) all leave the charcoal and terracotta palette the owner likes. If keeping it is required, Halo becomes the clear first choice, and Level's water-level idea would need re-rendering in terracotta before it could be judged fairly.

# Per direction

## Lantern · average 7.7

A dark charcoal canvas lit by one sentence. The app opens on the current step alone, in very large white type, with a single terracotta bar burning down beneath it. The rest of the day waits folded in a light drawer at the bottom edge.

**Modern, 9/10.** Strongest: Looks the most like a premium app shipping in late 2026: a charcoal canvas, very large confident Instrument Sans, one terracotta bar and a stone drawer. It uses the owner's palette better than any other direction, and the Live Activity and Dynamic Island treatment match the app itself, so the look holds up outside the app too. Weakest: The three screens are almost the same composition, so the direction's range is unproven. Today leaves the top third empty, and the light drawer can read as a sheet borrowed from another app. The dark-only look is a commitment the mock doesn't test in daylight.

**Intuitive, 8/10.** Strongest: It answers 'what now' fastest of the ten. The app opens on the current step alone, in very large white type, with one terracotta bar and '18 min left of 25 · ends 10:23' under it. The next item is always visible on the drawer's 'Next 10:30 Call the dentist' line, and capture is the only orange button. It is the least overwhelming screen of the ten. Weakest: Today and Focus look almost the same, so 'am I in focus mode or not?' is unclear. In Focus the tabs fold away. The rest of the day, including every anytime task, sits closed in a drawer, and for this audience a task out of sight is a task forgotten.

**Distinct, 6/10.** Strongest: It is the strongest on-palette mood. A charcoal canvas lit by one large white sentence, with a terracotta bar burning down and a stone drawer at the foot, makes the owner's palette into an atmosphere, and the name and metaphor are brandable. Weakest: The parts are generic: a dark screen, a big sans heading, a progress bar and pill buttons. Without the drawer it looks like many dark-mode focus apps (structurally almost the same as Nightline), so the screenshot is a mood more than one ownable shape.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The drawer's half-open and near-full states, and the 'Later today' and 'Anytime' content, are now only described in a caption, not drawn. Showing them would need a fourth phone or an inset.
- Today and Focus now look like close siblings: a big white step on charcoal over a bar. This follows from the direction itself. They differ in the top context, the step size (40 vs 44), the controls and whether the tabs are visible.
- The timer's meaning is ambiguous. '18 min left of 25' sits under the step, but screen 2 shows 25 min as the whole task's estimate. Whether the time box is per step or per task needs deciding in the product, not in the mock-up.
- The folded drawer is drawn 150 px tall, not the direction's 132 pt, because the Next line, 52 px tabs and the home indicator need the room.
- The mock-up still loads Instrument Sans from Google Fonts (noted in the file; the app would bundle the font). The Dynamic Island drawing leaves out the camera cutout.
- 'Me' is still a text capsule rather than an icon or avatar.

## Level · average 7

The screen is the timer. Behind the running task, a pool-blue plane fills the display and drops like a water level as the minutes pass. The minutes are written on the waterline, and the step sits above it in huge condensed type.

**Modern, 7/10.** Strongest: Bold and unmistakable. Heavy condensed Archivo, hard black outlines and a pool-blue water level that fills the screen make a striking poster-like system with a single clear idea. Weakest: The neo-brutalist trend is peaking now and may age badly. The huge condensed headlines come close to the headline-led look the owner rejected, and pool blue replaces the palette he likes. It reads more as a concept shot than as a calm daily app.

**Intuitive, 5/10.** Strongest: On Focus the step is enormous and the time is a calm level in the background. '18 min left' is written on the waterline beside 'ends 10:23', and 'Step done' is the only filled control. Weakest: On Today the water-glass has to be decoded: dashed brim = estimate, white = used, blue = left. Its labels ('25 min estimate', '7 min so far') float in empty white and look like list rows. Heavy condensed type on every row flattens the hierarchy. Capture moves to the top right on the task and focus screens.

**Distinct, 9/10.** Strongest: The most recognisable screenshot of the ten. The whole screen is the glass, and a pool-blue plane drains with a black time tab riding the waterline. The idea carries through Today, the task and focus, and it maps straight onto an icon (the half-filled glass mark is already in the header), a Live Activity and widgets. One idea, impossible to mistake for another task app. Weakest: It drops the charcoal and terracotta palette the owner chose for pool blue, so the brand starts from a colour nobody asked for. The heavy condensed Archivo could read as a sports or fitness brand and may date faster than the idea itself.

Left unfixed after polish:

- Focus at 18 of 25 minutes shows about 220 pt of empty pool between 'ends 10:23' and the Done/Next list. It is the remaining time and should stay empty, but in a still frame it can look unfinished.
- Collisions as the level falls are not designed. On Today, 'ends 10:23' sits 8 pt above 'Now · step 2 of 3' and would overlap it below roughly 70 %. On Focus, the right-edge tag will cross the Done/Next and Note lines at low levels. The rule 'the line is cut back around glyphs' covers the line, not the ink tag.
- On Today the Anytime ring sits about 40 pt from its name to keep the shared name edge at x 92. This is correct on the grid but a little loose.
- Today has seven tappable things (Me, Back to it, four tabs, capture). That is within the five-main-choices rule only if persistent navigation is not counted, which is the same reading the original relied on.
- Today's tab capsule and capture circle are 60 pt, not the direction's 56 pt. I chose 60 to fit icon and label in a 52 pt pill.
- '+5' is terse on screen and relies on its 'Add 5 minutes' accessibility label.
- The mock-up still loads Archivo from Google Fonts. The comment says the app would bundle it, and other explore files do the same.
- Dynamic Type at large sizes, dark mode and the Lock Screen bar named in the caption are not drawn.

## Deck · average 6.7

The day is a hand of cards. The running task is the top card, full width with its timer bar; the next cards fan out beneath it showing only their edges and times; and 'not now' slides a card to the back of the deck.

**Modern, 8/10.** Strongest: Has the most personality and is the most current. Funnel Display, physical stacked cards and a full-bleed sheet that grows out of a card look like a 2026 consumer app a design-conscious person would screenshot. Nothing about it reads as a template. Weakest: Pastel cards on black is a Dribbble trend that may date quickly. It drops the terracotta palette the owner likes for mint, blue, lime and sand, and the all-teal task sheet is loud.

**Intuitive, 6/10.** Strongest: The top card can be acted on directly with 'Step done' and a non-shaming 'Not now'. The next items peek out beneath it in time order with clock time and length, so now and next are physically stacked. Weakest: The four card colours deliberately mean nothing, which invites someone to try to decode them. The fanned stack hides everything after the second card. Capture jumps from the bottom-right button to the top right on the task and focus screens, out of thumb reach. The full-colour sheets are loud.

**Distinct, 6/10.** Strongest: The hand of cards, with the running task as the top card and the next ones fanned beneath, is a clear and memorable metaphor. The fanned stack is recognisable in a screenshot, and 'Not now' sends the card to the back, which gives it a signature gesture. Weakest: Multicolour pastel cards (teal, periwinkle, lime, sand) on near-black are a current trend look, close to Apple Wallet and many 2024 planner shots. They throw out the owner's palette, and the colour carries no brand meaning, just folder tints.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The deck idea only shows on Today. The sheet and Focus are each one lagoon card, so a static board describes the four-colour system and the Not now slide rather than showing them. A fourth panel showing a card mid-slide, or a deck in two colours, would make the point better.
- The Today tab icon uses the usual stack with edges above, while the real deck fans downward. I tried four versions fanned downward; at 22 px all of them looked like a monitor or a toaster.
- Not now on the sheet and the card is a tinted wash button with an ink label. The brief says buttons are ink with a label in the card's colour. Two solid ink buttons side by side would lose the order of importance, so I left the wash.
- The Focus step stays at 44/48 instead of the spec's 34/40. This is deliberate and recorded in the header comment, but it still departs from the brief.
- If you count every tappable thing, Today has more than five: 2 card buttons, 2 card edges, 2 anytime rows, Me, 4 tabs and capture. It passes only if the five-choice rule means the main action set and navigation (4 tabs plus capture).
- 'Anytime today' is still a standard round-checkbox list, Reminders-style, and is the least distinctive part of the screen.
- The mock-up loads Funnel Display and Funnel Sans from Google Fonts. The app would bundle them. This is acceptable in design/explore but would break the no-network rule if it were copied into the app.

## Roundels · average 6.7

Every task is a roundel: a white disc holding a drawn line object, inside a ring that is its duration. The running roundel sits large at the top of Today, and its ring empties as you work.

**Modern, 6/10.** Strongest: The most complete and carefully crafted system: a set of one-stroke pictograms, ring states drawn as shape, a matching Live Activity, and a pleasant hero roundel on Today. It is clean and friendly throughout. Weakest: Rubik and pine green look like a generic wellness or health app from a few years ago, and the pictogram-in-ring idea is close to existing ADHD planners such as Tiimo. The terracotta palette is gone.

**Intuitive, 7/10.** Strongest: Capture is best here: a labelled '+ Capture' button in exactly the same place on all three screens. On Focus the step is clearly the largest thing and the clock is small beside it. Paused and done states have their own shapes as well as words. Weakest: On Today the biggest element is a pictogram (an envelope), not the step, which sits third in small text. The same ring means remaining time on the running task but total length on upcoming ones, so one shape carries two meanings. A pictogram chosen automatically from the task's words that turns out wrong would confuse the reader.

**Distinct, 7/10.** Strongest: It has a real repeatable brand unit: the roundel, a disc with a one-stroke pictogram inside a duration ring. With a 60-glyph pictogram set and drawn states (dashed when paused, a check when done), it becomes a visual language rather than a skin, and it carries straight to the Live Activity. Weakest: Pine green rings around white discs read like a health or fitness app, close to Apple Fitness. It also leaves the terracotta and charcoal palette, and the pine is close to the first build's sage green that the owner already rejected as generic.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The mock-up still loads Rubik from Google Fonts. The file's own comment says the app would bundle the font, but for this static board I left the link so it renders the same way the other explorations do.
- The Focus screen is deliberately sparse, with about 130 px of empty porcelain above the step block. It is calm, but it is the least distinctive of the three screens and could use a stronger idea from within the direction.
- The 'walk, park' tree pictogram reads close to a lollipop at 28 px. It needs a better drawing within the roughly 60-icon set, which I did not attempt here.
- On the board, the strip's Running halo sits on the presentation canvas (#DFE4E8) rather than on porcelain, so it shows only faintly there. This affects the board, not the app.
- On Today, the second 'Anytime' row scrolls under the dock. That is intended as a scrolling list, but it means only three tasks are visible below the hero at a glance.
- The 11 px tab and capture labels follow iOS convention but are below the project's older 12.5 px screen floor from the web build. Contrast passes: secondary on white is 6.5:1 and white on pine is 6.4:1.

## Glasshouse · average 6

White content runs edge to edge under a floating glass layer. The running task rides in a glass 'Now' capsule docked above the four-tab bar on every screen, so the present moment is always one thumb away.

**Modern, 7/10.** Strongest: The most native and polished. The floating tab bar, the Now capsule docked above it, the Live Activity, a sheet focus view and restrained colour all look like a well-made iOS 26 app such as Things or Apple's own. It feels intuitive and expensive, and it keeps the palette. Weakest: Of the stronger directions, it is the one most at risk of reading as stock iOS. Inter, plain rows and a charcoal pill button come close to the generic look the owner rejected, and the glass is drawn as 96% white, so the signature material barely shows.

**Intuitive, 9/10.** Strongest: Gives all four answers within two seconds and keeps them in the same place on every screen. The Now card names the step in bold and shows '18 min left' and 'ends 10:23'. Later items show clock time and duration blocks. Capture is the one terracotta + in a fixed spot, and it is labelled 'Capture' on the focus screen. On the task screen a Now capsule is docked above the tab bar, so the way back to the running step never moves. On the focus screen the step clearly outranks the bar, and 'Next:' is shown. Weakest: On Today the visible measure of time is a small ring around an envelope icon, so the number '18' carries the meaning more than the quantity does. The Now card is also a quiet stone panel whose step text is only slightly larger than the list rows, so 'now' depends on its position rather than standing out.

**Distinct, 2/10.** Strongest: The persistent glass Now capsule docked above the tab bar gives one consistent way back in, and the palette is kept. Weakest: It is essentially the iOS 26 system template: Liquid Glass bar, SF Pro, plain rows, and a mini-player-style capsule borrowed from Apple Music. A screenshot would pass for Apple Reminders with an orange plus, which is the 'default template' look the owner explicitly rejected.

Left unfixed after polish:

- Google Fonts loading in the mock-up. Every design/explore file does it, the request is only in a design board, and I was told to edit only this file. If the boards should obey the no-network rule too, the fonts need self-hosting or a system-font fallback across all nine explorations together.
- Section headers are title case ('Later Today'), as the direction specifies. The project's own voice rule in docs/07-design-system.md says sentence case everywhere. That conflict is a decision for the owner, not a fix.
- Glass is drawn as the specified 96% opaque white, so over white content it reads as white capsules with an edge and shadow. That is faithful to the spec, but the 'glass' only really shows on a device with the real material.
- The Focus sheet still has a calm empty band between the context and the time. I kept it deliberately rather than filling it with a larger timer, which would make the clock compete with the step.
- The charcoal 'Now' tag on the current step is a small rectangular badge. It is not a pill chip, but it is the one badge-like element in a direction that asks for none. I kept it because it carries the 'not by colour alone' marking.
- The time figures use Nunito 700 as a stand-in for SF Pro Rounded Semibold, because Nunito runs lighter than SF at the same weight. Only a device render will settle the exact weight.

## Quarters · average 6

The day is four bold bands of colour — Morning, Midday, Afternoon and Evening — and the app opens in the band you are in, so the screen's colour says where you are in the day.

**Modern, 6/10.** Strongest: Colour by time of day is a distinctive idea that nobody else on the panel has. The apricot ground with a white slab on the focus screen is warm and modern, and Onest at weight 800 gives it confidence. Weakest: Today stacks three grounds (white, apricot and navy) and looks unbalanced and heavy. Apricot full-bleed with dark-brown blocks can look muddy, and the navy evening strip under the tab bar crowds the bottom.

**Intuitive, 5/10.** Strongest: The plain-sentence summary 'One thing running and a call at 15:30' answers what is happening at a glance. 'Evening · Nothing planned' is calm, and each time-of-day band is named in words, not just shown in colour. Weakest: The first thing on Today is the anytime list, placed above the date and above the Now card, so the reading order begins with what is not now. The whole screen changes colour by time of day. Capture is the least emphasised control, a white outlined circle.

**Distinct, 7/10.** Strongest: The most original structural idea here: the whole screen takes the colour of the part of the day you are in (mist, mint, apricot, dusk). That is unlike any other task app, and a screenshot is instantly identifiable as this product. Weakest: The identity is four shifting colours, none of them the owner's palette, so there is no single brand colour to own. The pastel apricot and mint grounds also sit near the Tiimo or Dribbble planner look that many apps share.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The clock moved from 10:05 to 15:05, which departs from the shared 10:05 scenario every other direction uses. The tasks and steps are identical, but if strict side-by-side comparison matters more than showing the direction, revert the three clocks and the times in Today's rows.
- Mist and mint appear only in the legend tiles, not on a phone, and the 400 ms ease into the next band cannot be shown in a still.
- The current band is about 56% of the viewport, not the 70% the direction specifies, because the pinned white Anytime strip takes 176 px.
- The Anytime strip sits above the Now slab and is the first white shape the eye meets, which competes a little with 'one thing now'. The direction asks for it pinned at the top, so I kept it.
- The task screen leaves about 130 px of empty band between the note and the dock. It depends on content and I left it.
- Numerals are proportional, not the tabular the direction asks for. The file's header comment documents this choice: Onest's tabular 1 opens a gap in '15:30' and '18'.
- The mock-up loads Onest from Google Fonts. The app would bundle the font, but the board itself makes a network request.
- Tab labels are 12 px, which is iOS-normal but small for this audience. The direction did not specify their size.
- The partly elapsed time block has a hard inner edge where ink meets track. It is honest and readable, but less polished than the rounded blocks around it.

## Strata · average 5.7

Today is one column where length is time. Each timed item is a capsule as tall as its duration, and the running one is the only filled shape, its terracotta draining down to a charcoal now-line.

**Modern, 5/10.** Strongest: The timeline where length shows duration is clean on Today and follows good references such as Structured. The terracotta capsule draining to a now-line uses the palette well. Weakest: The focus screen's thermometer gauge is crude: a giant outlined orange tube with dead space beside it. The horizontal 'Anytime' pills are truncated, and the black-outlined capsules feel chunky rather than refined.

**Intuitive, 7/10.** Strongest: The timeline does the most for time-blindness. Length on screen is time, the running item is the only filled shape, and the gaps are named ('Free until 11:00', 'Free after 11:45'). So 'what's next and how long until it' needs no arithmetic. Finished items fold into a neutral 'Done' line with no count. Weakest: On Focus, a tall, saturated draining column is the loudest thing on the screen and competes with the step, while the minutes are small text at its foot. On Today the anytime tasks sit in a truncated sideways scroller ('Order pr…'), and the grey 45-minute 'Walk to the park' block is bigger than the running task.

**Distinct, 5/10.** Strongest: On-palette. The vertical gauge on the focus screen, a tall terracotta tube draining beside the step, is distinctive, and the terracotta capsule on a charcoal now-line makes a clear Today signature. Weakest: Length-as-time on a vertical timeline is the exact idea apps like Structured already own. The rest is rounded grey capsules and chips that sit close to the first build's generic look the owner rejected.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The Anytime tray still cuts its second item to 'Order pr' under a fade. This is a deliberate hint that the tray scrolls sideways, but at a glance it can still look like clipped text.
- The running capsule puts its text inside the terracotta. Under about 15 minutes left at 3 pt there is no longer room for it, so the real build needs a rule for that, such as moving the text to one line or overlaying it. The mock-up only shows the 18-minutes-left state.
- The Focus screen has a lot of empty space above and below the step block on the left. It is calm, but a little sparse.
- The Today tab glyph (stacked capsules with a dot) is on-concept but still reads as a slightly blobby shape at 24 px.
- Behind the task sheet there is only a dimmed blank strip, not a scaled-down Today screen.
- The board still loads Hanken Grotesk from Google Fonts. That is fine for a static mock-up, but the app has to bundle the font, as the file's own comment says.

## Halo · average 5.7

The day is a clock face. Scheduled items sit as charcoal arcs around the rim of a stone dial, the running task is a terracotta band shrinking from the current time, and the current step is written in the middle.

**Modern, 5/10.** Strongest: The task and focus screens are handsome. The bold terracotta ring with the step set large in its centre is a strong, memorable focus image in the owner's palette. Weakest: The Today clock dial looks unresolved. Glyphs float around the rim, the terracotta wedge looks misdrawn, and the check marks and pill shapes are scattered, so the headline screen looks amateur next to the rest.

**Intuitive, 4/10.** Strongest: The centre of the dial states the step and '18 min left' plainly, and the task and focus rings are clear measures of time with the step as the largest type on the focus screen. Weakest: The Today dial cannot be read in two seconds. On a 12-hour face the running task is a small wedge, glyphs float outside the rim, and finished items are capsules at 7 to 8 o'clock. It is ambiguous for anyone who struggles with time, takes half the screen, and leaves the answer to 'what's next' to the list below it.

**Distinct, 8/10.** Strongest: The day as a clock face is an ownable, on-palette idea that runs through all three screens: a 12-hour dial on Today, the task's own ring, then a 25-minute face in focus. The terracotta band from the now-notch is a mark that could become the app icon, the Dynamic Island and the brand symbol without translation. Weakest: Away from the Today dial, it falls back on the most common timer idiom there is, a draining ring like Time Timer's or any pomodoro app's. Screens 2 and 3 alone could be mistaken for a generic focus timer, and circular day planners such as Sectograph already exist.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The 12-hour scale is built into the direction: at 10:05 everything happens in one sixth of the face. The 18-minute band is about a 23 × 24 pt block, the 10-minute dentist arc is a dot, and the dial will always look heavy in one sector with a lot of empty stone.
- The finished arcs still read a little like pills or plasters, because the edge they need to be visible on stone gives them an outlined capsule shape.
- Finished items have no glyph chip and no row in the list. Tapping one would have nothing to scroll to, and the mock-up does not show the 'arc ringed when tapped' state.
- Apart from their rings, the Task and Focus screens are close to stock iOS: stone grouped sections, capsule buttons, a floating tab bar. The halo motif carries all of the identity.
- The note card appears on both the Task and Focus screens, and the Task screen keeps an empty gap above 'Move to later'.
- The mock-up still loads Albert Sans from Google Fonts. That is acceptable for a design board but would have to be bundled in the app.
- The Tasks tab icon's small circles (r 1.5) look a little light next to the other tab icons.

## Nightline · average 5

A dark departures board for your day. Each item is one line: time, name, length. The running line lifts into a wide band with a live mint bar, and every status is said in plain words, such as 'until 10:23' and 'next in 25 min'.

**Modern, 4/10.** Strongest: Competent, crisp dark UI with clear hierarchy and good legibility. The departures-board rows are tidy. Weakest: The most generic of the ten: it looks like a stock dark-mode fintech or to-do template with a mint accent and a square mint FAB. The banded rows are flat and characterless, and it drops the owner's palette entirely.

**Intuitive, 8/10.** Strongest: Every status is said in plain words. The next row reads 'Next · in 25 min', which converts clock time into time-until for someone who struggles to sense time. Focus reads 'Done: … / Now · step 2 of 3 / Then: …'. Every running item carries a bar, a number and an end time, and every row reads the same way: time, name, length. Weakest: Near-black rows of similar weight make the screen something to scan rather than glance at. The Now card is only slightly lighter than the list, so the one mint bar does most of the work of marking now. Capture moves from a floating button on Today to a button in the action row on the other screens. The palette is also not the one the owner likes.

**Distinct, 3/10.** Strongest: The departures-board framing, one line per item with time, name and length and every status in words, is a coherent concept with a nameable story. Weakest: The board is barely expressed visually (no board-like type, no flap or signage character), so it reads as a generic dark-mode list app. Mint on near-black looks like a fintech or developer tool, and it abandons the owner's palette entirely.

Left unfixed after polish:

- The 'Me' button at the top right of Today is a seventh control on the screen. It stays within the five-choice rule only if, like here, the main choices are counted as the tabs plus capture. Where profile and settings live was not mine to decide.
- Capture is not in the same spot on every screen: it sits higher on Today because of the dock and at the bottom edge where there is no dock. Keeping the dock on the pushed task screen would fix that, but it is a structural decision.
- The time column for Anytime items is left blank on purpose. If the owner reads a blank as missing data, the other choice is a light 'Any time' in small grey text.
- Focus keeps about 100 px of empty space under its top bar. I read it as calm rather than a mistake.
