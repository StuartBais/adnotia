# Anchor the rebuild on one shared list

The research supports the direction set in ADR-043 and narrows what the product brief can honestly claim. **The only non-drug approach with blinded trial support in adults with ADHD is a therapist-delivered skills package built mainly from organising, planning and time management, and its benefit shows on clinicians' ratings at about 12 weeks, not on patients' own.** No single tool a phone can deliver alone has been trialled, so every adult tool Adnotia ships is an untested implementation of a tested package, a slice of a programme the app does not run, or something people with ADHD say helps them. Those people rate apps low: in a survey of 1,859 adults with ADHD, 3.53% called a productivity app "extremely helpful", and most reader praise goes to paper, other people and timers. One shared task list remains the best-supported core, as a design hypothesis: no user in anything read asked to "enter a task once", while low-friction capture and a way back after a lapse that carries no accusation are the wants with direct statements behind them. Design has no ADHD-specific evidence for colour, type or dark mode; W3C guidance and a few cognitive findings support short paths, externalised memory, user control and reminders only at the person's request. Streaks have never been tested on outcomes in adults with ADHD, and stated preference points both ways: some interviewees ask for rewards and streaks, a non-resetting "gentle streak" was well received as a concept, and experts warn against penalty feedback. For families, parent-run tools on one phone are the only model that needs no account; no stand-alone child aid has an ADHD trial, the sleep intervention's benefit beyond sleep is parent-reported and was not reproduced, and the evidence on treating a parent's ADHD first is mixed. The research cuts across ADR-043 or standing rules at fifteen points, including two sentences in the existing documents that the checked sources do not support. Trust varies by finding: most trial figures have now been read in full text or abstract, while guidelines for adults, store policy, review-site material and most first-person essays have not been opened.

## What was read, and how each finding is tagged

All five note sets were re-checked against their sources on 14 November 2026. Each check was done by one reader, which is not the independent review `docs/02-evidence-rubric.md` asks for before a tier is assigned. This report uses one set of tags.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[full text]** | The figure was read in the body of the paper (PMC, a repository copy, NICE's or W3C's own page) |
| **[abstract]** | Read in the paper's own abstract or database record only; tables and limitations not seen |
| **[page read]** | A non-journal page was opened and its words read: Apple and Android developer pages, GitHub READMEs, vendor help pages and US App Store listings (text only, no screenshots), ADDitude articles, Hacker News threads |
| **[search summary]** | Known only from a search-result extract or someone else's summary; the page was not opened |
| **[lived]** | First-person statements or reader surveys; never evidence that something works. Paired with a second tag saying whether the page was read |
| **inference** | Reasoning from a finding, by the note-takers or this report |

Not opened, and so no better than a search summary: adult guideline texts (the NICE recommendations on parents were read; the adult ones were not), all store-policy and children's-law sources, review aggregators and competitor blogs, Reddit itself, Finch's help pages, and most first-person essays. Tier placements below are proposals; a tier is assigned by somebody other than the tool's author. Nothing below recommends dose, timing or interaction advice, tracking of a child's medication, or self-report screening of a child.

## Trials back a taught package on clinicians' ratings; no phone tool has been tested alone

### Organising and planning carry the strongest signal, less firmly than first thought

The two anchor trials were read in full and hold. Safren's 2010 trial randomised 86 medicated adults to 12 sessions of CBT or of relaxation with educational support; on a blinded assessor's rating the difference was −4.63 points (95% CI −8.30 to −0.96, d = 0.60), with 67% against 33% responding *[full text]* ([PMC3641654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3641654/)). Solanto's 2010 trial randomised 88 adults to 12 weeks of group meta-cognitive therapy or a matched supportive group. **On the blind interview 42.2% responded against 12% (OR 5.41, 95% CI 1.77 to 16.55)**; the pair often quoted, 53% against 28%, is the self-report criterion. Expectancy did not differ between arms, and home-exercise completion, not attendance, predicted change *[full text]* ([PMC3633586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3633586/)).

The papers describe what was taught. Safren's first and longest module is a calendar, a task list and problem-solving that includes breaking tasks into steps; the second is timing one's attention span and writing distractions down instead of acting on them; the third is cognitive restructuring. Solanto's sessions cover time-awareness, breaking tasks into parts, scheduling and prioritising, and if-then self-instruction phrases such as "If I am having trouble getting started, then the first step is too big" *[full text of the trial papers; the manuals themselves were not opened]*. Priority ratings, alarms and estimate-then-compare exercises are not stated in either paper and remain recall.

Other trials temper the picture. In COMPAS (433 randomised) a structured skills group did no better than individual clinical management on blinded ratings (17.6 against 16.5 from 20.6) *[abstract]* ([PubMed 26536057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26536057/)), and at 1.5 years the blinded difference was still null while self-reported symptoms favoured the group *[full text]* ([PMC6547099](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6547099/)). In a neurofeedback trial of 118 adults, a meta-cognitive therapy group did no better than sham neurofeedback on self-report *[abstract]* ([PubMed 28803030](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28803030/)). Pooled, CBT beats a waiting list on self-ratings (SMD −0.84, moderate quality) and does not beat supportive therapy on self-ratings (−0.16, not significant) *[abstract]* ([PubMed 29566425](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566425/)).

The component argument is weaker than the first pass assumed. The one component network meta-analysis (43 RCTs, 3,817 participants) associates organisational strategies with higher response (incremental OR 2.03, 95% CI 1.27 to 3.24), but **43.2% of its participants were adolescents**, adult-only component estimates are in a supplement nobody read, and the authors write that "our findings should be considered as hypotheses" *[full text]* ([PMC11683884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11683884/)). No dismantling trial was found. Three small trials of time-management programmes point in different directions: a positive pilot on masked ratings (24 against 24), and a pragmatic RCT with no between-group difference *[abstract]* ([PubMed 33472510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33472510/); [PubMed 41527987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41527987/)).

### The benefit is on clinician ratings at 12 weeks, which is not the rating an app user has

Three syntheses read at source say the same thing. The largest, a component network meta-analysis of 113 RCTs and 14,887 adults, reports CBT better than placebo "on clinician-reported scales, but not self-reported scales" at the time point closest to 12 weeks (SMD −0.76, 95% CI −1.26 to −0.26); the same holds for mindfulness (−0.79) and psychoeducation (−0.77). At 52 weeks, from five studies, CBT differed from placebo on self-ratings (−1.09) with a prediction interval running from about −6 to +4 *[full text]* ([repository copy](https://iris.unimo.it/retrieve/be4c8ea5-8571-4778-9ccb-43e6f90f019c/PIIS2215036624003602.pdf)). The 2025 umbrella review gives CBT moderate certainty "when rated by clinicians", SMD 0.53 against passive controls, and nothing at moderate certainty beyond the short term *[full text]* ([PMC12651917](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12651917/)). The Cochrane review agrees, as above. The Library wording a planning tool can carry is that it implements part of a package whose benefit has been shown on clinicians' blinded ratings and less consistently on people's own. The network meta-analysis is not in the rubric's citation list.

A third synthesis is misreported in the rubric itself. `docs/02-evidence-rubric.md` says a 2025 network meta-analysis of 37 RCTs found CBT "the most effective non-pharmacological intervention on core symptoms, short and long term", with mindfulness-based cognitive therapy second. On the paper's own point estimates CBT was third short term (psychoeducation −6.38, mindfulness −5.07, CBT −4.43) and fourth long term; what the authors' conclusion rests on is that CBT alone was also effective on depression and anxiety and had by far the most trials *[full text]* ([PMC11825462](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11825462/)). Estimates of that size are outside every other synthesis and read better as rankings than as effect sizes. Changing the rubric is for a person, with an ADR.

### Digital programmes are better supported than before, and still unblinded

There are now at least nine RCTs of digital skills programmes in adults. The largest (337 adults, a self-guided programme against usual care) reports d = 0.85 on self-rated symptoms, yet only 11.6% against 1.2% met a 30%-reduction criterion; it was funded by the manufacturer *[full text]* ([PMC13079232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13079232/)). A second (307 adults) reports d = 0.54 on quality of life with four of twelve modules started on average, and its authors write that the effects "could therefore be interpreted as a placebo effect" *[full text]* ([PMC13475384](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13475384/)). A third (120 adults, d = 0.70) used a control that covered the same strategies as text, so the contrast is exercises and media, and 28% did not complete the post-test *[full text]* ([PMC10033990](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10033990)). One therapist-guided trial of 104 adults had blind-assessor secondary ratings that "closely mirrored" self-ratings, and in it applied relaxation matched CBT *[full text]* ([PMC10359875](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10359875)). The one comparison with face-to-face delivery favoured face-to-face at a year *[abstract]* ([PubMed 42717581](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42717581/)). These are sequenced multi-week programmes. Their evidence does not transfer to a list, planner or timer inside a different app. The coached course in using ordinary phone apps, the closest trial to Adnotia's proposition, could not be verified ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1b7369c5f7174fb7a75c47bdf1fceb47)).

### Outside the packages the evidence thins fast

Mindfulness has one blinded result: 120 adults, clinician-rated difference −3.44 (d = 0.41) against usual care *[abstract]* ([PubMed 29486807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29486807/)); pooled effects are 0.48 self-rated and 0.32 observer-rated against passive controls *[full text]* ([PMC12440486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440486)). Exercise rests on single sessions (g = 0.23 on symptoms) *[abstract]* ([PubMed 41638541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41638541/)) and one 12-week RCT with 41 analysed and 35% dropout ([PMC12614457](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12614457/)). For sleep, a 2025 review found the only adult ADHD study of CBT for insomnia was an uncontrolled pilot of 19 *[full text]* ([PMC12426716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12426716/)). Goal management training's one RCT (81 adults) separated from usual care only on anxiety *[abstract]* ([PubMed 38046113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38046113)). Direct PubMed searches found no intervention study in adults with ADHD for visual timers, Pomodoro, journaling, habit tracking, implementation intentions or a symptom or medication diary, and one adherence-app RCT (73 adults) found no difference in medication possession *[abstract]* ([PubMed 37341028](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37341028/)). These were single database searches, not systematic reviews.

Two cognitive findings bear on tools. Time-based prospective memory shows "a large-sized impairment" in adults with ADHD while event-based does not (25 against 25) *[abstract]* ([PubMed 22660916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22660916/)). And the timing meta-analysis usually cited for visual timers covers durations "in the range of milliseconds to several seconds", so it does not directly support claims about misjudging minutes or hours *[abstract]* ([PubMed 34923055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34923055/)). A visual timer is plausible and untested.

The exclusion list stands, with one caveat for adult-facing entries. Neurofeedback shows SMD 0.04 on probably blinded ratings, but only three of 38 trials studied adults *[full text]* ([PMC11800020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11800020/)). Computerised training's blinded evidence is one adult trial among 36 *[full text]* ([PMC10208955](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10208955/)); the adult meta-analysis found a small gain on a composite of cognitive tests and none on symptoms *[abstract]* ([PubMed 36892894](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36892894/)). The network meta-analysis's "cognitive remediation" estimate is clinician-rated only, with a very wide interval. Adult entries should cite the adult studies and not quote child-dominated figures as adult ones.

### People with ADHD rate apps low and praise paper, other people and timers

The lived-experience material is now partly read. It is not evidence that anything works, and it skews to ADDitude's readership and to a technical forum. The context comes first. **ADDitude surveyed 1,859 adults with ADHD: 3.53% rated a time-management or productivity app "extremely helpful"**, below a family member or friend (5.6%) and a personal organiser (4.73%) *[lived, page read]* ([ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/punctuality-time-blindness-adhd-apps-tips/)). In an analysis of 3,383 Reddit posts, digital tools appear in 9.0% and 8.4% of posts naming a coping strategy, behind planning support, lifestyle and social support; the coding was by a language model and not human-validated *[full text]* ([Seo et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13495778/)). One interview paper cites a study in which adults with ADHD perceived calendars, lists and reminders as less effective than other users did, despite similar use *[full text of the citing paper only]* ([arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)).

Ranked by how many independent kinds of opened source carry first-person praise, the order is as follows. First, writing things down where they stay visible: 61% of ADDitude readers use hardcopy planners, sticky notes and handwritten lists, with no sample size given, and readers say why: "A digital calendar basically ceases to exist for my brain once closed" *[lived, page read]* ([ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/best-planners-adhd-brains-paper-digital/)). The same article prints readers who are "dedicated to digital" because the phone is always carried and makes a noise. Second, borrowed structure: body doubling, a friend, a deadline set by someone else *[lived, page read]*. The body-doubling survey of 220 people describes practices and tests nothing about effectiveness, and its often-quoted 75% figure could not be verified *[abstract]* ([doi:10.1145/3689648](https://doi.org/10.1145/3689648)). The one controlled test is 12 adults in virtual reality, where efficiency was higher with a double and accuracy was not; the one earlier study with ADHD participants, a master's thesis, was null *[full text of the preprint]* ([arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12153)). Third, timers and alarms, especially a "pre-timer" five minutes before stopping and timers that pace a routine; users also say nothing "helped beyond the initial novelty" and that a reminder does not move a dreaded task *[lived, page read]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34486848)). Then a home for every object (hard for 42% of 1,885 readers), short lists and an "I-did today" list, routines, and distraction blockers. Task breakdown fell in the ranking: its popularity claims all sat on competitor blogs that were not opened, leaving one reader naming Goblin Tools and two people wanting it. Estimating time is a named problem for 31.35% of the survey. Sleep, exercise and medication outrank every tool in both the Reddit analysis and the forum tally.

## One task record removes the double entry, and that is a hypothesis

### The model is documented; the user demand is not

"One place for tasks that feeds planning, breakdown and timers" is not stated by any user in anything read. The one source cited for it, a participant's "one-stop shop", turned out to be about trustworthy information in a psychoeducation app *[full text]* ([PMC11847728](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11847728/)). The nearest user statements are about one place to capture and about not wanting to maintain a tool: "I sometimes spend more time organizing the to-do app than doing the task" *[full text]* ([arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)). Decision 6 should go into the brief as a design hypothesis supported by guidance: WCAG 2.2's criterion 3.3.7 requires previously entered information to be "auto-populated, or available for the user to select" *[full text]* ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).

How good apps do it is now read from vendors' own pages. Tiimo separates "Tasks (Today tab): Scheduled activities" from "To-dos (To-do tab): Unscheduled ideas, or your mental inbox", and one tap on "Start task" moves a to-do to Today "and launch[es] the task in Focus mode" *[page read]* ([Tiimo: tasks and to-dos](https://www.tiimoapp.com/faq/manage-tasks)). Structured's inbox holds items with no date, and day, week and month are views of the same items *[page read]* ([Structured help](https://help.structured.app/en/articles/380546)). Things keeps a start date ("When can I start working on this?") apart from a deadline *[page read]* ([Things support](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803579/)). Both leading planners resolve "when" into three states: at a time, some time today ("Anytime"), and no date. The middle state is what stops a day plan becoming a timetable.

The most precise source is Amazing Marvin's public data documentation: inbox is "no parent" and unscheduled is "no day"; estimate and time worked are separate fields; time worked is stored as start and stop timestamps, so a running timer is derived from the data; sub-tasks are embedded in the task; a recurring task is a template kept apart from its dated instances *[page read]* ([MarvinAPI wiki](https://github.com/amazingmarvin/MarvinAPI/wiki/Marvin-Data-Types)). That is decision 6 as a data shape. One of its fields exists to measure how long a task was put off, and a product with a no-guilt rule can keep estimate beside actual and leave that field out (inference).

### Capture and the return are the two moments with direct support

Low-friction capture is the best-evidenced unmet want: "I need two things: ubiquity, so that I can add ideas, todos, etc. wherever I am; and exaggerated simplicity" *[lived, page read]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46610156)). Readers speak items to an assistant and photograph notes. On a phone, lock-screen, widget and voice entry are operating-system features, which is an argument for the move in decision 1.

The way back after a lapse now has read statements behind it, and what people describe is the absence of an accusation, not a restart ritual. A to-do list catalogues unfinished things "until they become cognitively crushing to think about" *[lived, page read]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43416755)); one design-study participant found a calm state image "more comforting than red overdue tasks" *[full text]* ([arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)). W3C's guidance adds what a person needs on return: "where I am, what I just did" *[full text]* ([W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)).

Planning is the impaired step: 45 adults with ADHD showed "a large-scale impairment" in task planning and negligible to small effects for self-initiation and execution *[abstract]* ([PubMed 23484020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23484020/)). A captured item should be startable with no planning step, and a ready first step does more than an empty breakdown screen (inference). Automatic breakdown as people know it is cloud AI: Goblin Tools' privacy page says "we send the necessary input data to OpenAI's servers" *[page read]* ([Goblin Tools](https://goblin.tools/Privacy)). Structured shows the split an offline app can copy: its assistant uses OpenAI, and a separate feature, "AI-powered subtasks with Apple Intelligence", runs on iPhone 15 and later *[page read]* ([Structured help](https://help.structured.app/en/articles/1782402); [iOS 26 post](https://structured.app/blog/ios26)). Hand-written steps and templates are the baseline. Participants in two studies wanted control kept: "I want it to suggest, not decide."

### Few places, fewer choices

W3C's guidance is specific: "five or less main choices on each screen", and a critical path that is "open the app, run the most used function" *[full text]* ([W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)). Apple says "it's generally easier to navigate among fewer tabs" and asks for onboarding that is "fast, fun, and optional" with nonessential setup postponed *[page read]* ([Apple HIG: tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.json); [onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding.json)). Tiimo has four tabs (Today, To-do, Focus, Me) and Structured four (Inbox, Timeline, Structured AI, Settings) *[page read]*. Headspace collapsed four category tabs into a Today tab with an Explore tab behind it *[page read]* ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=fkfnhq8u)). The Irish psychoeducation app is the cautionary case: 37 static pages, and "the primary limitation was navigating the app" *[full text]*.

The grouping this supports, as inference: tools that act on a task are verbs on the task, reached from the item; the fixed places are a today surface, the list and timeline as views of the same records, routines, and a library that holds everything else with one marked starting point per area. Medication has its own door. Family is a separate surface. New domains arrive in the library, which is how depth grows without more places.

## Design evidence supports control and removal; the look is an identity choice

### What guidance and studies support

| Rule | Basis | How it was read |
|---|---|---|
| Open on something usable; no setup before the first completed action | Interviewees "overwhelmed at the number of pages"; "too complicated to set up" ([PMC11847728](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11847728/); [arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)) | Adult ADHD interviews *[full text]* |
| Five or fewer main choices; optional steps outside the path | W3C patterns 4.6.2, 4.6.3 | Guidance *[full text]*, consensus not trials |
| Each step shows what earlier steps supplied; never ask twice | W3C 4.7.5; WCAG 3.3.7 | Guidance *[full text]* |
| Back never loses work; every save says it saved | W3C 4.5.2, 4.5.9, 4.5.10 | Guidance *[full text]* |
| Nothing moves or interrupts unless the person started it | W3C 4.6.1, 4.9.1; Apple: "Make motion optional" ([HIG: motion](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json)) | Guidance *[full text; page read]* |
| Tie prompts to events, never only to a clock time | Time-based prospective memory impaired ([PubMed 22660916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22660916/)) | Lab study *[abstract]*; the rule is inference |
| Confirm each action at once, specifically | Delay discounting d = 0.43, no difference by age ([PubMed 27722208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27722208/)); experts favour specific positive feedback | *[abstract]*; inference |
| Take decoration off task screens; graphics that carry content are welcome | Distraction by irrelevant images rises with symptom scores ([PubMed 26667659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26667659/)); interviewees "preferred pages with colourful graphics" | General-population *[abstract]*; interviews *[full text]* |
| Describe a gap neutrally | Criticism of organisation and forgetfulness harmed self-worth in 162 adults ([PMC8856522](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8856522/)) | *[full text]*; about people, extended to copy by inference |
| A passcode needs paste, autofill and biometrics | WCAG 3.3.8; W3C 4.7.1 | Guidance *[full text]* |
| Motion, haptics and sound each optional, offered early | Apple: "Make haptics optional" ([HIG: haptics](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics.json)) | Platform guidance *[page read]* |

The first fortnight carries the weight. In a seven-week study of a commercial CBT app, 14% never enrolled and 36% dropped out before week two, against 4% afterwards *[full text]* ([PMC9931323](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9931323/)); the Irish app's engagement "peaked in the first two weeks", with 2.4 sessions per user. Among general mental-health apps, median 30-day retention is 3.3% *[full text]* ([PMC6785720](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6785720/)). "Confusing or difficult" is the most consistent abandonment reason in a general review; account creation and data loss each rest on one source study within it *[full text]* ([PMC11694054](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11694054/)). A device-only app cannot measure its own retention.

### Colour, typeface, dark mode and density have no ADHD evidence

A PubMed search for dark mode, typeface or background colour with adults and ADHD returned nothing on interfaces. One preference survey found each neurodevelopmental group preferring a different background colour *[abstract]* ([Brunel repository](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/32230)). One small reading study, known second-hand, found a sans-serif fastest and lowest on comprehension *[search summary]*. No source names a typeface for any of the 19 apps examined; Tiimo and Structured each offer a dyslexia-friendly font as a setting. Decision 9 asks for a design rebuilt from research, and research supplies behaviour and constraints, not a look. Palette, type and mode are identity, and no screen should describe them as helping attention.

### One ownable idea, and what the admired apps also carry

Apple's citations, read on its own pages, reward calm: Tiimo's "timeline of soothing colors" that "made tackling our to-dos a calming activity" ([App Store Awards](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-store-awards/)); Structured, "visually sharp and easy to scan", which "prioritizes downtime as much as productivity" ([Apple Design Awards 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/)); Gentler Streak, "powered not by insistent reminders but an optimistic and encouraging vibe" ([Apple Design Awards 2024](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)) *[page read]*. This is a curator's judgement. The recognisable apps commit to one idea a stranger could describe (a red disk, a bean that knits, coloured blocks on a day) and apply it to the core object and the completion moment. In the planners the identity lives in the task itself: App Store editors describe Tiimo's "color-coded, emoji-accented blocks" *[page read]* ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2025/12/2025_app_store_award_winners)).

The first version of this report treated these apps as models of restraint. The vendor pages correct that. **Tiimo has streaks and trophies**: "Every session you show up in Tiimo and every task you complete earns you trophies" *[page read]* ([Tiimo: streaks](https://www.tiimoapp.com/product/streaks)). Routinery grows a plant "for each day you keep a streak". Focus Friend's bean will be "really really sad" if the timer is stopped *[page read]* ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6742278016)). One reviewer was asked to rate Tiimo three times in ten minutes and found its emoji "distracting and childish". A product without reward loops departs from the admired apps as well as from the mass market. What Apple's guidance requires of any identity is read and specific: 17 pt default text, 4.5:1 contrast, 44 by 44 pt targets, never colour alone, a custom font that honours Dynamic Type, and "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion" *[page read]* ([HIG: typography](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography.json)).

## People drift when the tool goes invisible; stated preference on rewards is split

### Why people stop

On opened sources the best-evidenced reasons are these. The tool fades: "If it's only me reminding myself, it just becomes invisible after a few days" *[full text]*; a "graveyard of pen and paper notebooks, todo apps, reminder thingies, post-its" *[lived, page read]*. Setup and upkeep are work: "Many apps are too complicated to set up, and I feel more anxious using them." The backlog greets the person on return. Alerts get dismissed unread: "I constantly close the push notifications before I read them" *[lived, page read]* ([ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/best-planners-adhd-brains-paper-digital/)). Some want help with the feeling, not the list. Paper fades too; what differs is that paper left on a desk is still seen. **Cost, a removed feature, app sprawl and sync appear in nothing that was opened**, so price as a reason for leaving is unranked. There is no longitudinal study of app abandonment in adults with ADHD.

### Streaks and rewards: untested on outcomes, wanted by some, harmful when they reset

No study has tested streaks, points or badges on outcomes in adults with ADHD; a PubMed search for streaks with ADHD returned nothing. The earlier statement that nothing exists "in either direction" was too strong, because three adult ADHD studies record what people say. Interviewees suggested "rewards, badges or streaks" and reminders to keep them engaged, and the authors agree gamified elements might help *[full text]* ([PMC11847728](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11847728/)). A concept called "flexible planning and gentle streaks", with an ideal and a baseline goal and a streak that does not reset, "resonated with participants who struggled with self-criticism"; one said it stopped them "hating themselves for not completing everything", and some warned that too much flexibility could demotivate. That is reaction to a described concept, not use *[full text]* ([arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)). Experts in a third study supported specific positive feedback and warned that penalty feedback "feels like saying, 'See, you didn't do it again'" *[full text]* ([arXiv 2602.09381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381)). Others say rewards are inert: they "do not work because I don't look forward to anything" *[lived, page read]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36745858)). For some the tick is enough.

The general-population finding explains the pattern. Across seven studies a highlighted broken streak lowered later engagement, the effect "depends solely on how that behavior is represented", it is amplified by self-blame and attenuated when the streak can be repaired *[abstract, read on a university listing]* ([University of Colorado](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Gamification made no difference to adherence in depression-app trials, a finding its authors caution against carrying to other conditions *[full text]* ([PMC8669581](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8669581/)). The accounts of streak guilt in the first version (the "day 40" story, the "two-week death spiral") sit on pages that were not opened and are dropped.

This is the finding ADR-043 asked for, and it changes the earlier conclusion. The firm part of the rule is the part about loss: nothing that can break, reset, penalise or show a gap in alarm colour. That part has general evidence, expert opinion and participant statements behind it. The absolute part, no streak or count of any kind, cannot be presented as what people with ADHD uniformly want, and a minority ask for exactly what it forbids. The owner has a real choice. One option keeps the rule whole and meets the wish another way: a baseline and an ideal for the day so that a poor day still counts, an "I did" list (which the existing day log already is), and immediate specific confirmation; none of these counts consecutive days. The other option permits a progress marker that never resets. The evidence for that is one design study of 20 people responding to a description. On that evidence the first option is the better-supported one, and the scope document should state the split honestly.

### Reminders: only the ones a person sets

W3C's pattern is exact: "Reminders must be set only at the user's request and the user must be able to personalize the [reminder] method"; "Always give the option to set a reminder at the end of the task"; "It is essential not to add unwanted reminders" *[full text]* ([W3C Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)). The one randomised test in adults with ADHD (109 people) found that SMS reminders "did not show an effect" on module completion, logins or practice *[full text]* ([PMC9149073](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9149073/)). Two clinic text-message programmes report roughly double the timely refills against matched record comparators; they are non-randomised, clinic-run and about prescriptions, and nothing here proposes that the app remind anyone about a dose *[abstract]* ([PubMed 31162154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31162154/)). Participants report cues fading within days, and some found pattern-based nudges intrusive: "it feels like it's watching me". Against that, readers praise alarms they set themselves, above all the warning before a timer ends. The rule that follows: no message the app originates; a reminder exists only if the person made it, is offered when they finish something, is removed in one tap and never reports what was missed; a timer the person started may warn before it ends.

### Price

Decision 3 permits paid extensions. What can be said from pages read is narrow. Vendors that reviewers treat as fair keep the core free: Structured's free tier includes the timeline, inbox, focus timer, Live Activities and "Accessibility built in" *[page read]* ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1499198946)); Things is "a one-time purchase for each platform". Complaints about silent renewal, a lifetime price that tripled, and privacy basics behind a paywall are at search-summary level only. Among open-source apps on the stores, none found charges for privacy or accessibility; they charge for hosting or take tips *[page read]*. Copyleft apps reach the App Store either because the copyright holder publishes its own code (Signal, Ente) or through a written exception: Nextcloud's licence file commits not to pursue a violation that results solely from App Store terms *[page read]* ([COPYING.iOS](https://github.com/nextcloud/ios/blob/master/COPYING.iOS)); KeePassium declines outside code instead ([GitHub](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium)). That supports the contributor agreement in decision 2. It is a reading of README files and not legal advice.

## Family tools help parenting and conduct; the child aids themselves are untested

### What the trials support

The sentence in `docs/04-family-space.md` on parent training holds, with sizes now read. On probably blinded measures behavioural interventions improve positive parenting (SMD 0.63), reduce negative parenting (0.43) and conduct problems (0.31), and do not change ADHD symptoms (0.02, 95% CI −0.30 to 0.34) *[full text]* ([accepted manuscript](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/5841181/file/5841190.pdf)). At about five months the between-group effect on ADHD symptoms is small (SMD 0.21) with no masked-unmasked difference, and the authors caution that those figures sometimes came from different studies and that observers "did not observe change in ADHD symptoms" *[full text]* ([PMC10501699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10501699/)). "Modest" is right; "small" is more exact.

Antecedent techniques are the best fit for a phone. In a microtrial with parents of 92 children, two sessions of antecedent techniques improved daily parent-rated behaviour (d = .56, then .65 at two weeks) and inattention; parents could not be masked *[abstract and full text]* ([PubMed 34424102](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34424102/)). A higher dose of psychoeducation was associated with smaller effects *[full text]* ([PubMed 35417075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35417075/)), which argues for short Library entries. The same paper's association between negative consequences and larger behaviour effects is weak support for anything: a coefficient of .01 with an interval touching zero, uncorrected, on unblinded ratings, for a category that is mostly planned ignoring and logical consequences; the authors cite an experiment in which token removal made children with ADHD persist less. Nothing in it argues against a positive-only chart.

Human support is the moderator an app cannot supply. In the largest trial (431 children), web-assisted self-help reduced blinded clinician-rated behaviour problems "only when combined with additional support"; unsupported self-help was not shown to differ from usual care *[full text]* ([PMC12350813](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12350813/)). In an open-enrolment programme, 54.8% of 2,967 parents never started *[abstract]* ([PubMed 30067388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30067388/)). The UK app trial closest to the Family space still has no outcome paper as of 14 November 2026. Adnotia offers the tools a programme teaches, not the programme.

### No stand-alone child aid has an ADHD trial

PubMed searches found no trial of a visual schedule, first/then board, visual timer or home reward chart used on its own by families of children with ADHD. The nearest are a 38-child RCT of therapist-delivered time-skill training plus devices, in which parents saw improvement and "according to the children, their [daily time management] was not significantly changed" *[abstract]* ([PubMed 28956183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28956183/)), and one classroom session with a visible timer in unselected children. Under the rubric's wording these aids fit Tier C, while the Family table groups them at Tier A.

The sleep claim in `docs/04-family-space.md` overstates its trial. In the 244-child RCT, parents who delivered the intervention reported lower ADHD symptoms (effect size −0.3, then −0.4), but **teacher-rated ADHD symptoms did not differ**; teachers reported less difficult behaviour *[full text]* ([PMC4299655](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4299655/)). The larger community trial (361 children) improved parent-reported sleep and found "no benefits of the intervention ... in other domains" *[abstract]* ([PubMed 31184382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31184382/)). The defensible claim is that a brief clinician-delivered intervention improves parent-reported sleep, repeatedly. The document's sentence that it improves "both sleep and daytime functioning", and the proposed A/B tier, are for a person to revisit.

Organisational skills training shows large effects on ratings by parents and teachers who "were treatment agents" (d = 2.77 and 1.18) *[full text]* ([PMC3549033](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3549033/)). The daily report card meta-analysis rests on seven studies; a 2025 RCT of 213 students adds fewer observed rule violations *[abstract]* ([PubMed 40587316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40587316/)). The school runs report cards, and the existing design fits.

### When the parent has ADHD too

Prevalence figures run from 23% of fathers and 27% of mothers (323 trios) to 41.3% and 51.0% (79 children), all from self-report scales with no clinical interview *[abstract; full text]* ([PubMed 20630538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20630538/); [PMC4780663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780663)). Parental ADHD symptoms predict lower home practice between sessions and not attendance *[abstract]* ([PMC7567125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7567125)). A mother at a parent-training course: "my hand-written graphs were crumpled"; another "forgot she was keeping the charts altogether" *[lived, page read]* ([ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/mothers-with-adhd-raising-kids-with-adhd/)).

Whether treating the parent first helps the child is mixed. A 2026 trial of 120 parents found steeper declines in children's clinical severity (d = 0.37) when the parent's own treatment came before parent training, comparing two active arms, with blinding of the raters not stated *[abstract]* ([PubMed 42611696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42611696/)). The largest earlier trial (144 dyads) found no difference on blinded child externalising symptoms *[abstract]* ([PubMed 26123832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26123832/)), and a pilot favoured the opposite order. Those arms were clinical treatment, which the app neither delivers nor advises on. "The parent's own ADHD affects how parenting support goes" is supported; "address the parent first" is not settled. The firmer ground for decision 8 is NICE's recommendation 1.4.8: "Think about the needs of a parent with ADHD who also has a child with ADHD, including whether they need extra support with organisational strategies" *[full text]* ([NICE NG87](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/Recommendations)).

That supports a small join. A task captured for a child's sake is an item in the parent's own list. A routine is the same kind of object for adult and child. The parent's own tools sit one step from the Family side, and every Family tool works after a missed week with nothing to catch up on. Nothing flows to the child surface except the parent-authored schedule, timer, first/then board and chart. Medication takes no part. No product or study was found that merges an adult's tools with a child's; a phone handed to a child is not in the parent's hand, so a printed schedule is a first-class output (inference). Reward charts fail, in parents' and clinicians' words, when "the novelty of a new behavioral system wears off" and when "a reward ... is collected far off in the future" *[lived, page read]* ([ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/behavior-therapy-examples-common-setbacks-and-solutions/)). A chart awarded in one tap at the moment fits that. A prompt to award would be the app reminding about points, ruled out in the rubric; families in a co-design study asked instead for "intentional family time for reviewing data together" *[search summary]* ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/86x7t804)), which a parent can start.

For 12 to 17, a parent-teen programme showed parent-rated gains in a university trial, "no significant group x time effects" in a community trial of 278, and contracting gains "not maintained" at six months *[full text; abstract]* ([PMC4949080](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4949080/); [PMC7907265](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7907265/)). NICE asks that involvement be discussed "at intervals" as a young person develops. A plan built together and run by the teenager fits; a teenager rating their own symptoms is screening and stays out.

### Models and store rules

Brili's original children's listing returned 404 in the US store and the product on sale is an adult routine app; Joon can run on one shared device with a parent approval step; Tiimo offers up to five profiles under one login *[page read]*. A parent code on one phone remains the only model with no account. The family notes' store-policy section assumed a browser app and was not re-checked: everything on Apple's Kids Category, Google Play's target-audience declaration, the amended COPPA factors and US state age laws is at search-summary level ([Google Play Families policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)). ADR-043 puts the app in the stores, so these apply and belong in the regulatory review, with primary texts read by a qualified person.

## An autism extension inherits a fixed frame, not the ADHD tiers

Two studies were read in full, and neither included people with ADHD, so the AuDHD conflict is an inference across separate populations. In the Kent study (11 autistic and 10 control adults; people with ADHD excluded) changes to colour scheme, font, wording and button order each lowered usability or comfort; one participant left after a wording change ("one change of words can often throw me"); a larger send button was welcomed *[full text]* ([Kent repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/110913/)). Renaming a control cost more than restyling it, which matters for any app that revises its copy. In the Stress Autism Mate study (10 autistic users) a neutral, predictable app was valued as "clear and honest", and its rigidity drove people away: four prompts a day, a 60-minute answer window, repeated tips *[full text]* ([Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1637008/full)). Predictability is not rigidity. A fixed frame with adjustable frequency fits both.

Tiers do not transfer. Visual supports are an evidence-based practice in autism on 65 articles, 62 of them single-case *[full text]* ([PMC8510990](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8510990/)); the earlier count of 104 was wrong. The sleep intervention in autistic children reduced caregiver-reported sleep problems (effect size −0.7 at three months, −0.4 at twelve) *[abstract]* ([PubMed 35285017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35285017/)). The rebuilt Library needs a shape in which a tool carries a tier per population. Energy frameworks, "anchors, not schedules" and everything first-person from AuDHD writers remain unopened leads, and where autism and ADHD recommendations differ was not sourced. This is not enough to scope an extension.

## Conclusion

Checking the sources moved the report in one direction: toward smaller claims and a plainer product. The trial evidence belongs to a taught programme and to a clinician's rating; the people the app is for mostly say apps have not helped them much and that paper has; and the admired apps turn out to carry the streaks and trophies they were assumed to avoid. What survives is specific. A person should be able to put something down in one step from anywhere, start it without planning, be warned by a timer they set, and come back after a fortnight to a screen that does not accuse them. Paper already does the first and the last. The app has to earn its place by doing the middle two.

The guilt rule is where the checking mattered most. Its core, nothing that breaks or resets, came out stronger. Its absoluteness came out as a choice the owner makes knowing some people will want otherwise. Two existing documents say more than their sources do, and both corrections run the same way as everything else here: the evidence supports helping with parenting, sleep and getting organised, on the word of the people living it, and supports little beyond that.
