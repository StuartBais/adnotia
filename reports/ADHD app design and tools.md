# Anchor the rebuild on one shared list

The research supports the direction set in ADR-043 and narrows what the product brief can honestly claim. **The only non-drug approach with repeated, partly blinded trial support in adults with ADHD is a therapist-delivered skills package built mainly from organising, planning and time management; no single tool a phone can deliver alone has been trialled.** Every adult tool Adnotia ships is therefore an untested implementation of a tested package, a slice of a multi-week programme the app does not run, or something people with ADHD say helps them. That makes one shared task list (captured once, then broken down, planned and timed as the same record) the best-supported core: organisation has the strongest claim to being the active ingredient, asking twice for the same information is a named accessibility failure, and juggling several apps is a reported reason for quitting. Design has almost no ADHD-specific evidence; guidance and cognitive findings support short paths, externalised memory and time, user control and the removal of irrelevant decoration, while colour, typeface, dark mode and density are identity choices and need labelling as such. No study has tested streaks, points or badges in adults with ADHD in either direction, general-population findings and lived accounts are enough to keep the no-streak rule as written, and the one randomised test of reminders in adults with ADHD found no effect. For families, the parent-run single-phone model already built is the only architecture that works with no account, and the case for joining it to a parent's own tools is that parental ADHD is common and predicts weaker follow-through. The research cuts across ADR-043 or the rules it leaves standing at thirteen points, most sharply on phone backups versus "your data never leaves the device", on timer alerts versus the earlier no-notification stance, and on several tier labels. **No clinical paper, guideline, forum thread or store page was opened at source in any of the five note sets**; only Apple, Android and GitHub developer pages were read, so every clinical figure below is abstract-level or second-hand and carries a tag saying which.

## Every figure carries a tag, and only platform pages were read at source

The five note sets were written on 14 November 2026 under a network policy that blocked PubMed, PMC, journal sites, Reddit, ADDitude, vendor sites and the app stores, and each ran out of search budget before its last round of checks. This report carries the notes' verification status through with one set of tags, defined below. A figure without the **[read]** tag has not been checked against the full text of its source by anyone, and the report never treats it as verified.

Tier placements in this report are proposals. Under the project's rules a tier is assigned by somebody other than the tool's author, and ADR-043 keeps that rule. Nothing below recommends dose, timing or interaction advice, any tracking of a child's medication, or self-report screening of a child; where a finding brushes against one of those lines the text says so and stops.

## Trials back a taught skills package, and no phone tool has been tested alone

### Organising and planning carry the strongest signal

The trialled treatments are multi-week, therapist-delivered packages. Safren's programme has three core modules (psychoeducation with organising and planning, coping with distractibility, adaptive thinking) and two optional ones *[secondary]* ([CHADD summary](https://chadd.org/attention-article/mastering-your-adult-adhd/)). Solanto's 12-week group targets time management, organisation and planning; in 88 adults it beat a matched supportive-therapy group with **an odds ratio for response of 5.41 (95% CI 1.77 to 16.55) on blinded evaluation** *[abstract]* ([PubMed 20231319](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20231319/)). Safren's 2010 trial in 86 medicated adults found lower independent-assessor ratings after CBT than after relaxation with educational support *[secondary; effect sizes not retrieved]* ([doi:10.1001/jama.2010.1192](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2010.1192)). Pooled, the Cochrane review reports SMD 0.58 on clinician-rated and 0.44 on self-rated symptoms against other specific interventions, both graded low quality *[abstract]* ([PubMed 29566425](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566425/)), and a second meta-analysis gives SMD 0.43 against active controls *[abstract]* ([PubMed 27554190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27554190/)). The largest single trial cuts the other way: in COMPAS a structured skills group did no better than non-specific individual clinical management on the blinded primary outcome *[abstract, via a news report of the paper]* ([News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20151109/Medication-not-psychotherapy-intensity-sways-treatment-outcome-in-adult-ADHD.aspx)), while patients' own ratings favoured the group *[abstract]* ([library record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1576197875)). The fair summary is that structured skills treatment clearly beats a waiting list, modestly beats active controls, and the size of its specific effect is uncertain.

No dismantling trial isolating one skill was found, on two targeted queries and not a systematic search. The only component-level evidence is one component network meta-analysis of 43 RCTs and 3,817 participants, in which **organisational strategies (incremental OR 2.03, 95% CI 1.27 to 3.24)** and third-wave components (1.95) were associated with higher response and problem-solving with lower inattention (incremental SMD 0.42) *[abstract]* ([PMC11683884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11683884/)). That is inference between trials, and whether the pooled trials were adults-only was not confirmed. It lines up with the protocols themselves: organisation and planning are the whole of Solanto's programme, the first and longest part of Safren's, and the part Safren says has to be in place before the rest *[secondary]* ([Psychology Tools interview](https://www.psychologytools.com/articles/meet-the-expert-dr-steven-safren)). Adaptive thinking has no comparable signal in what was retrieved.

Digital delivery now has several adult RCTs. A self-guided CBT-based programme tested in 337 adults reported **d = 0.85 on self-rated symptoms against usual care** *[abstract of the preprint; published figures not checked]* ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.27.25334574.full.pdf)); a self-guided internet programme in 120 adults reported d = 0.70 against an online psychoeducation control *[abstract]* ([PMC10033990](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10033990)); and internet CBT and internet applied relaxation both beat usual care without differing from each other in 104 adults *[abstract]* ([PMC10359875](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10359875)). Every outcome in these trials is self-report by people who knew their allocation, several have commercial interests, and completion is the weak point: one self-guided programme was not completed by 77% of participants *[abstract]* ([PMC9149073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149073)). The closest trial to Adnotia's own proposition is a six-week coached course teaching 57 adults with ADHD or sub-clinical ADHD to organise daily life with ordinary smartphone apps, which lowered inattention scores against a waiting list *[abstract]* ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1b7369c5f7174fb7a75c47bdf1fceb47)). It had a coach, and Adnotia has none. Evidence for a sequenced programme does not transfer to a standalone list, planner or timer inside a different app.

### Self-ratings are the caveat that reaches an app user

The largest synthesis, a component network meta-analysis of 113 RCTs and 14,887 adults *[abstract]* ([repository copy](https://iris.unimo.it/retrieve/be4c8ea5-8571-4778-9ccb-43e6f90f019c/PIIS2215036624003602.pdf)), is reported as finding that at 12 weeks only stimulants and atomoxetine reduced core symptoms on both clinician and self ratings, and that **non-drug treatments looked effective when clinicians rated symptoms and not when patients did** *[secondary]* ([Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20250110/only-stimulants-atomoxetine-reduce-core-adhd-symptoms-in-adults-metaanalysis-shows); [Science Media Centre](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-network-meta-analysis-of-pharmacological-psychological-and-neurostimulatory-interventions-for-adhd/)). The rater attribution of its CBT estimate (SMD −0.76) was not confirmed. A 2025 umbrella review gives CBT in adults at least moderate certainty with a medium effect, and mindfulness a large effect with low certainty, all short-term *[abstract]* ([PubMed 41297970](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41297970/)); a 2025 network meta-analysis of 37 RCTs ranks CBT first while rating most evidence low or very low confidence, with 48.6% of trials at high risk of bias *[abstract]* ([Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1516878/full)). These can all be true together. An app user only ever has their own rating, which on that reading is the rating on which non-drug treatment did not separate from placebo, so the Library wording for planning tools needs both statements, and the large synthesis is not yet in the rubric's citation list.

Outside the packages, only mindfulness, DBT-based skills groups, psychoeducation groups and digital programmes have more than one adult RCT. Mindfulness has one blinded result, d = 0.41 on clinician-rated symptoms against usual care in 120 adults *[abstract and secondary]* ([doi:10.1017/S0033291718000429](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291718000429)). Exercise rests mostly on single-session studies, four studies and 170 adults with small gains *[secondary]* ([summary](https://adhdevidence.org/blog/meta-analysis-finds-small-to-moderate-benefits-of-single-exercise-sessions-for-adult-adhd)). No controlled trial of a behavioural sleep intervention in adults with ADHD was located *[abstract-level review table]* ([PMC8534229](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8534229)). Psychoeducation raised knowledge (d = 0.97) in a pragmatic RCT *[abstract]* ([PEGASUS record](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7499896E20CF58984C603053A386A71B/S0924933800069893a.pdf/psychoeducational-groups-for-adults-with-adhd-and-their-significant-others-pegasus-a-pragmatic-multicenter-and-randomized-controlled-trial.pdf)) and did worse than skills training when used as a control. Body doubling, visual timers, Pomodoro-style work, implementation intentions, journaling and habit tracking have no trial in adults with ADHD in what was retrieved; for several of them the search was thin, so "not found" means not adequately looked for.

Guidelines back the category and not any tool. NICE NG87 is reproduced as asking, at minimum, for a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD, and as treating environmental modifications as an adjustment to make and review; that is the nearest guideline hook for timers and externalised structure, and it carries no evidence grade *[secondary reproduction of the guideline]* ([NG87 copy](https://www.nursinginpractice.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/NG87_ADHD.pdf)). The European consensus statement puts psychoeducation first after diagnosis *[secondary]* ([consensus statement](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/707E2A36539213CF85EACCA576F47427/core-reader)). No guideline was read at source, and none was found to recommend an app. The Library can say the planning tools are drawn from recommended treatment. It cannot say a guideline recommends the tools.

The exclusion list is untouched by anything since 2023. A 2025 meta-analysis of 38 neurofeedback RCTs gives **SMD 0.04 on probably blinded symptom ratings** *[abstract]* ([PubMed 39661381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39661381/)), and computerised cognitive training still shows no effect on total symptoms when raters are blinded *[abstract]* ([PMC10208955](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10208955/)). One tension needs stating in the Library entry before somebody else raises it: the large network meta-analysis reports "cognitive remediation" as better than placebo with a very wide interval *[abstract excerpt; rater not stated]* ([Bern repository record](https://boris-portal.unibe.ch/handle/20.500.12422/194663)).

### The strongest praise is for something an offline app cannot deliver

The lived-experience pass is the weakest evidence in this report. No page was opened, Reddit was barely represented, vendor blogs and affiliate lists dominated the results, and the rankings count kinds of source and not people. With that said, three signals recur across independent kinds of source. Body doubling is the strongest: a peer-reviewed survey of 220 mostly neurodivergent people is summarised as finding that about three quarters use it *[secondary; not checked against the paper, and secondary accounts of its effectiveness figures disagree]* ([doi:10.1145/3689648](https://doi.org/10.1145/3689648)). Staying on paper or a plain text file is the second *[lived]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44864134); [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/best-planners-adhd-brains-paper-digital/), attribution to this page unverified). Making time visible with a timer is the third *[lived]* ([Google Play review of Llama Life](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.llamaapp)). Tiny-step breakdown, a short list or a single visible task, distraction blockers and a fixed home for objects follow *[lived and vendor]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43416755); [r/ADHD via a mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/ADHD/comments/1kynvmz/how_to_not_lose_my_keys)).

Praise and measured effect are different things, and body doubling shows the gap. A 2025 preprint states that no peer-reviewed controlled trial has examined it in adults with ADHD and notes one earlier study with ADHD participants that found no significant effect *[abstract]* ([arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12153)). It also needs another person, which an app with no network cannot supply; the on-device substitutes (a visible timer, a "start with me" countdown) are untested inferences. The fourth tier's best-attested candidate is therefore a Library entry that describes the practice and points outward, not a tool. The paper signal matters more for the build: people who stay on paper are describing what they want software not to do (setup, accounts, features), and that is achievable offline. The honest counter-signal belongs beside it: Hacker News commenters with ADHD say the tool matters less than the person and that sleep and exercise matter more than any app *[lived]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34486848)).

Read as a list of domains, the adult side the evidence carries is small: tasks, with time and focus as states of a task and not separate tools; routines; a body-and-rest group holding sleep, exercise, mindfulness and an energy log; a Library; and medication, apart. Body doubling is a Library entry. Distraction blocking is praised by commenters *[lived]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19989627)) and depends on operating-system permissions the notes did not research for a phone app, so it is undecided. Coaching has no adult trial of the one-to-one kind *[secondary]* ([protocol paper](https://radar.brookes.ac.uk/radar/items/a4a8d49e-0f1c-4741-b550-82533d403b7e/1)) and cannot be delivered by an app. Nothing in the evidence supports a habit tracker, a medication tracker that computes anything, or any item on the exclusion list.

Three placements deserve a person's attention. Body doubling and Pomodoro-style work currently sit at Tier C on mechanism alone, the weaker of the rubric's two limbs, and are not components of any retrieved Tier A protocol; with a fourth tier available, that tier describes them more accurately. Implementation intentions are over-described if the Library calls them a technique used inside a Tier A protocol. And habit tracking has neither evidence nor a protocol basis, and it is the feature streaks attach to, so the research gives no reason to build one.

## One task record that gains fields removes the double entry

### Capture, breakdown, plan and timing are stages of one item

The well-regarded apps treat a task as one record that gains fields as it moves, and show it through a small number of views. Things separates structure (area, project, to-do, checklist) from time (a "when" date that hides a task until it is relevant, and a deadline that keeps it visible) *[secondary]* ([Vanja guide](https://vanja.io/things-3-complete-guide/)). Structured keeps unscheduled tasks in an inbox, scheduled ones as blocks on a timeline, and subtasks inside the task *[secondary]* ([Cool Curation](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)). Llama Life makes the timer a property of every task instead of a separate tool *[secondary]* ([MakerStack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/llama-life-review/)). Super Productivity, which is open source, local-first and needs no account, combines subtasks, timeboxing on a schedule and a focus mode in one model *[read]* ([GitHub](https://github.com/johannesjo/super-productivity)).

The model those apps imply, which is the note-taker's inference from reviews and not a documented design, is a single item holding a title, an icon and colour, an estimated duration, an optional date, an optional start time, a checklist, a state and the time actually spent. The inbox is every item with no date. The list is items with a date and no time. The timeline is items with a date and a time. The breakdown is the checklist. The timer is the running state of the item or of one checklist row. Estimate beside actual is two fields on the same record. **Nothing is entered twice because each stage only adds fields**, which is decision 6 of ADR-043 expressed as a data shape, and it satisfies WCAG 2.2's criterion 3.3.7, which says not to ask again for information already given *[secondary, via third-party summaries of the criterion]* ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).

Four findings shape how an item should move. Capture has to be separate from sorting, because doing both at once is reported to kill the habit *[vendor and advice pages, not user reports]* ([Leicestershire Partnership NHS Trust](https://www.leicspart.nhs.uk/while-you-wait/adhd/strategies-and-support/)). Planning is the impaired step and execution is not: unmedicated adults with ADHD showed "a large-scale impairment" in task planning and only negligible to small effects in executing a plan *[abstract]* ([PMC3590133](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3590133/)), so a person must be able to start a captured item with no planning step in between, and a template or a first step already written does more than an empty breakdown screen (inference). The view while a timer runs should show one thing, which is what commenters ask for and what Structured's developer says he wants to add *[lived; secondary]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43416755); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/structured-breakdown)). And the item list has to survive a lapse: people describe systems that assume they will return tomorrow where they left off *[lived, self-promotional source]* ([chudi.dev](https://chudi.dev/blog/adhd-engineer-productivity-system)), so what a person sees on reopening after a gap should be today, not an overdue pile. What a "gentle restart" consists of is not described by users in anything retrieved; it is a design problem the brief has to solve, and the nearest guide is design advice from a general-population survey of people who had stopped using a fitness tracker: show an aggregated view when there is less data, not the hole *[abstract]* ([Epstein et al. 2016](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/ubicomp2016.pdf)).

One caution on the claim itself. "One place for tasks that feeds planning, breakdown and timers" was not said by a user in anything retrieved. It is a synthesis of the app-sprawl complaint *[vendor]* ([Akiflow](https://akiflow.com/blog/adhd-productivity-apps-tools)), the "one-stop shop" theme in an interview study of 14 users *[abstract, catalogue record]* ([Seery et al.](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-64178dcc0ebe43bd879a5adfadf11792/Description)) and a co-design study in which 20 students with ADHD are summarised as wanting fragmented task information integrated *[secondary]* ([arXiv 2602.09381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381)). The direction is well supported; the sentence should not be printed as something users said.

Automatic breakdown needs its own decision. The version people praise, Goblin Tools' adjustable-granularity breakdown, is described as AI *[secondary]* ([iphone-ticker.de](https://www.iphone-ticker.de/geheimtipp-goblin-ki-fuer-alltagsaufgaben-strukturiert-nach-bedarf-253134/)), and its critics note that knowing the steps and being able to start them are different things *[vendor, competitor review]* ([Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/goblin-tools-review/)). On-device models exist on both platforms for recent hardware *[read]* ([Apple Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels); [Android Gemini Nano](https://developer.android.com/ai/gemini-nano)), but Apple's framework can also route to Private Cloud Compute or a server model *[read]*, so "uses Foundation Models" does not by itself keep text on the phone. Hand-written steps and templates are the baseline; a model is an enhancement for some devices at most, and would need its own ADR and its own tier.

### A today surface in front, a library behind

Two shapes recur for the first screen: a "today" surface that already contains the next action, and a library behind it. The clearest account read at source is Headspace's: earlier versions had separate tabs for meditation, focus, movement and sleep, and research led the team to "strip away that complexity" for a single Today tab with an Explore tab as the gateway to everything else *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Headspace](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=fkfnhq8u)). The opposite route is Amazing Marvin's 94 or more opt-in "strategies", whose first week reviewers call overwhelming *[secondary]* ([Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/amazing-marvin-review/)). Praise for making a system your own and complaints about setup are the same property seen from two sides, and setup burden is one of the three best-attested reasons for quitting.

For grouping, the implication is that tools which act on a task are verbs on the task (break down, plan, start), reached from the item, and not destinations in a menu. The fixed places can then stay few: a today surface holding what is now and next; the list and timeline as two views of the same records; routines; and a library that indexes everything else by area and carries the evidence entries. Medication is entered through its own door and appears on none of those surfaces. Family is a separate surface on the same engine. New domains arrive inside the library and earn a place on the today surface only when the person turns them on, which is how depth is added without widening the first screen. This grouping is inference from six or seven apps, most of them known only from reviews; no usability study has measured navigation depth, onboarding length or steps to capture with adults with ADHD.

Decision 6 says there will be more domains than the current modules. The research does not argue against more domains. It argues against more places: W3C's cognitive accessibility guidance asks for short critical paths and no unnecessary content *[abstract]* ([W3C objective 5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o5-user-focus)), and the maker of one habit app settled on a cap of about six tasks a day as part of the design *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Streaks](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=67dti69d)).

## Design evidence supports control and removal; the look is an identity choice

### Guidance and cognitive findings support a short list of behaviours

Decision 9 asks for a design rebuilt from research into what adults with ADHD find usable. The research can supply behaviours and constraints. It cannot supply a look, because there is no trial showing that any colour, typeface, contrast level, density or colour mode improves anything for adults with ADHD. The adult HCI literature is small, recent and qualitative; reviews of ADHD apps agree that usability is rarely evaluated *[abstract]* ([arXiv 2601.21791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21791)), and none links a design feature to sustained use.

The table gives the rules the evidence does carry, with the kind of basis for each. None has been tested as an interface feature in adults with ADHD.

| Rule | Basis | Kind of basis |
|---|---|---|
| Let a person start what they opened the app for, with no planning step first | Planning is the large deficit ([PMC3590133](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3590133/)); experienced adults want help in the moment ([PMC11847728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11847728)) | Inference from adult ADHD studies *[abstract]* |
| One primary action per screen and a short path to any tool | W3C "Help users focus" ([objective 5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o5-user-focus)) | Consensus guidance, not trials *[abstract]* |
| Never ask twice; carry values forward; keep the current step on screen | WCAG 3.3.7; moderate working-memory deficits in adults ([PubMed 23688211](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23688211/)) | Guidance plus inference *[abstract; effect sizes secondary]* |
| Show time as a quantity that shrinks, and planned time beside actual | Timing deficits across ages ([Southampton repository](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/456003)); adult evidence scarce | Inference; a visual timer is plausible, not evidenced |
| Tie cues to events or to opening the app, not to a clock time | Time-based prospective memory is the impaired kind ([PMC3590133](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3590133/)) | Inference *[abstract]* |
| Give feedback at the moment of the action | Steeper delay discounting, d = 0.43, all ages ([PubMed 27722208](https://www.pubmed.gov/27722208/)) | Inference *[abstract]* |
| Nothing moves, sounds or interrupts unless the person started it | W3C "Limit interruptions" ([pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o5p01-minimal-interruptions)); Apple's guidance against auto-dismissing views and auto-playing media ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)) | Guidance *[abstract; Apple read]* |
| Remove decoration, badges and motion beside a task | ADHD symptom scores correlate with distraction by irrelevant images, r = .32 in a replication ([Sussex repository](https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/59000/); [replication](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-021-01162-5)) | General-population correlation *[abstract]* |
| Save drafts, offer undo, restore the person's place after an interruption | W3C objectives on mistakes and memory ([Note](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)) | Guidance |
| Follow the system text size; 4.5:1 contrast; 44 by 44 pt targets; never colour alone | Apple Human Interface Guidelines ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)) | Platform guidance *[read]* |
| Describe; never frame a gap or an unfinished task as failure | Among 162 adults with ADHD or high ADHD traits, criticism of inattention-related behaviour was linked to lower self-worth ([PMC8856522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8856522/)) | Adult ADHD study *[abstract]* |
| Opt-in tools, without a long settings surface | Adults with ADHD "do not benefit from a uniform model" ([arXiv 2603.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)) | Qualitative adult study *[abstract]* |

The first session carries most of the weight. Among 93 mental-health apps, median 30-day retention was 3.3% *[abstract]* ([PMC6785720](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6785720)), and a scoping review found a median of 70% of users gone within 100 days, with account creation, privacy worries, cost and data loss among 22 named reasons *[abstract]* ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897)). Those are general-population figures; no study measures abandonment over time in adults with ADHD. An app with no account removes two of the named reasons and leaves data loss, which makes backup a feature that bears on staying, not only on safety. One award-winning app reached the same place by taste and has no sign-in: "We wanted people to go straight into the experience" *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Bears Gratitude](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=i74v3f4r)).

Lock and backup belong to the design and not to a settings page. A numeric passcode is a memory test in the sense of WCAG 2.2's criterion 3.3.8, so the passcode field should accept paste and autofill and offer biometrics (inference from the criterion, seen through third-party summaries). The pattern that reads as trustworthy in privacy-first apps is a lock that is off by default, offered once at set-up, passcode first with biometrics as a convenience, and the system's own prompt, which Android describes as "consistent across the apps that use it, creating a more trustworthy user experience" *[read]* ([KeePassium](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium); [Android Developers: biometric authentication](https://developer.android.com/identity/sign-in/biometric-auth)). No source was found for how any on-device ADHD planner words its screen on where data lives and how to move it to a new phone, so that copy has no model to follow.

### Colour, typeface, dark mode and density have no ADHD evidence

One survey of 204 adults across ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia and dyspraxia found most formatting preferences shared, with background colour the exception: each group preferred a different one *[abstract; which colours, and the ADHD-only results, not seen]* ([Brunel repository](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/32230)). One reading study with six ADHD participants found them fastest with a sans-serif at 14 to 16 px *[secondary]* ([review page](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69ed2c3f083d4508fd230b16)), which is too small a sample to quote as a rule. Dark-mode claims rest on anecdote and unpublished company data. The defensible position is the one `07-design-system.md` already takes on hue, extended to everything visual: palette, type and mode are identity and preference, they meet the accessibility floor, and no screen describes any of them as helping attention. On density the supported claim is about irrelevance and not about emptiness; an absorbing task screen protects attention better than a sparse one with ornament at its edge (inference from the distraction correlation).

### One ownable idea separates the recognisable apps from the generic

The best-verified material in the whole research base is Apple's own wording on why it rewards certain apps, read at source. It is a curator's judgement and not evidence of what works for ADHD, and it is consistent. Tiimo, iPhone App of the Year for 2025, is praised for "a timeline of soothing colors" that "made tackling our to-dos a calming activity" *[read]* ([App Store Awards 2025](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-store-awards/)). Structured, a 2026 Apple Design Award finalist, is "visually sharp and easy to scan" and "prioritizes downtime as much as productivity" *[read]* ([Apple Design Awards 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/)). Gentler Streak is "powered not by insistent reminders but an optimistic and encouraging vibe", and its team says "It guided but it didn't push" *[read]* ([Apple Design Awards 2024](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/); [Behind the Design](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3m0ht22s)). **The centre of gravity has moved from list managers to visual day timelines with rest built in, and the praise is for calm and legibility, not for power.**

What makes these apps recognisable is one idea a stranger could describe in a sentence, applied to the core object and to the moment of completion. Time Timer's red disk is both the function and the brand *[secondary]* ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/time-timer/id332520417)). Pok Pok Playroom is hand-drawn, limited to three colours, with sounds recorded from real objects because "we didn't want anything loud and overstimulating" *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Pok Pok](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=5bcex7xf)). Bears Gratitude began from a set of drawn characters: "Instead of trying to set our app apart, how do we make it ours?" *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Bears Gratitude](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=i74v3f4r)). The makers of (Not Boring) Habits tuned a press-and-hold checkbox over "thousands of iterations" and argue that "design is difficult to copy" *[read]* ([Apple Developer: (Not Boring) Habits](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9ab1g4r3)). In the timeline planners the identity lives in the task object itself, its colour, icon and shape on the day, so entering data produces the look; Tiimo suggests an emoji as a task is typed *[secondary]* ([Built for Mars](https://builtformars.com/company/tiimo)) and Streaks suggests from about 500 icons *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Streaks](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=67dti69d)). A generic app, by contrast, assembles default components, a stock accent colour and stock icons, and adds confetti at the end. ADR-043's description of the first build (system fonts, bordered cards, pill chips, one narrow column) is that list.

Calm and distinctive are compatible; the restraint in the praised apps is in palette size, motion and sound, not in an absence of character. Three trade-offs follow for the brief. A character used as a guide and not a scorekeeper is a recurring device (Gentler Streak's mascot "is what your heart would be telling you" *[read]*, [Behind the Design](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3m0ht22s)), but some reviewers find Finch's pet childish *[secondary]* ([Headway](https://makeheadway.com/blog/finch-app-review/)), one study of autistic users found an emotionally neutral tone valued, and character imagery bears on how a store judges an app's audience. Completion as a physical event is where several apps put their signature, and it is also the confetti-sound-vibration pattern that, on one design writer's argument, has to be switchable off before it first fires for a sensory-sensitive person *[secondary; a design article, not user evidence]* ([Editorialge](https://editorialge.com/sensory-friendly-app-design/)). And a coloured timeline has to pair colour with an icon or label to meet "never colour alone", which the emoji-per-block pattern does. On type, no source was retrieved on what typefaces any of these apps use, so the lifted "no font files" rule gets no guidance from this research in either direction.

## People leave at the first missed day; streaks and reminders have little behind them

### Novelty, a missed day and setup are the best-attested reasons for quitting

Everything about why people with ADHD abandon apps comes from forums, essays, store-review extracts and vendor copy; no peer-reviewed study follows abandonment in this population, and no thematic analysis of ADHD app reviews was found. Three reasons recur across the most independent sources. Novelty wears off within weeks *[lived]* ([masukomi, 2024](https://weblog.masukomi.org/2024/05/17/making-great-productivity-tools/)). A missed day produces guilt and the person does not come back: one developer with ADHD names a "two-week death spiral" *[lived]* ([chudi.dev](https://chudi.dev/blog/adhd-engineer-productivity-system)). And setup, complexity and feature count cost more than they return, with the plain text file as the resolution *[lived]* ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44864134)). Subscription cost, notification load, a relied-on feature being changed (Tiimo's October 2024 routine redesign), app sprawl and upkeep that is itself low-interest work follow, less well evidenced. The first two reasons are one event seen at two moments, and an app cannot stop novelty fading. It can decide what a person sees when they come back.

Usage data from a free Irish psychoeducation app gives the one adult ADHD datapoint: over 18,000 downloads in a year, 12,000 active users, 6,400 who returned after first use, and an average user who started three modules and finished two *[abstract]* ([PMC11847728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11847728)). A device-only app cannot measure its own retention at all, which is a consequence of the privacy position worth stating in the brief: Adnotia will never know its drop-off, so its design has to rest on these outside findings.

### No study has tested streaks in adults with ADHD, in either direction

ADR-043 holds the no-guilt-mechanics rule as written until the research reports. It reports this. **There is no published study of streaks, points or badges in adults with ADHD, positive or negative.** The claim that streaks shame ADHD users and the claim that gamification suits "dopamine-seeking" brains are both unevidenced for this population, and the pages asserting harm are mostly from vendors selling streak-free products. What exists is general-population work: across seven studies, highlighting a broken streak reduced later engagement relative to an intact one, more so when people blamed themselves and less when the streak could be "repaired", and the effect came from how the log represented behaviour, not from the behaviour *[abstract]* ([doi:10.1093/jcr/ucac029](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)). A meta-analysis of depression apps found gamification made no significant difference to adherence or effectiveness *[abstract]* ([JMIR Mental Health](https://mental.jmir.org/2021/11/e32199)). Half of lapsed fitness-tracker users described guilt *[abstract]* ([UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2016/09/08/life-after-fitbit-appealing-to-those-who-feel-guilty-vs-free/)).

Lived opinion is split and not one-sided. Some people describe reaching day 40 on guilt alone and never reopening the app *[lived, general audience]* ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/do-streaks-build-habits-or-just-delay-churn)); others say a penalty, or an obligation to a group, got the task done *[lived; attribution unverified]* ([Choosing Therapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/?p=35258)). The split runs between a number about oneself being lost and an obligation to other people, and the second is closer to body doubling than to gamification. The usual "forgiving" example, Finch, does have a streak; its help centre says it counts each day the app is opened and can be repaired *[lived, help page seen as a search extract]* ([Finch help centre](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37780736136205-Understanding-Streaks)). What is praised as forgiving has three properties: nothing is lost on a missed day, any count measures showing up and not performance, and a person can resume without a visible hole. Game mechanics also wear off on their own schedule; reviewers of one children's reward app put the boredom point at four to eight weeks *[lived, review site]* ([NeuroLaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/joon-adhd-app-kids/)).

The finding for the brief is narrow and firm enough to act on. Keep the ban on streaks, consecutive-day counts and any message about missed days. The honest reason is that displayed broken streaks demotivate people in general, self-blame makes it worse, adults with ADHD report frequent criticism for exactly these lapses, and gamification has not been shown to help adherence; it is not that streaks have been shown to harm people with ADHD. The scope document that replaces `03-scope.md` should say it that way, and should say that a minority report that penalties help them. The research gives no ground for adding points or badges. It does support one thing the rule never forbade: immediate, concrete confirmation that an action registered, because the preference for sooner rewards is a small-to-medium effect in two meta-analyses that pool all ages, and real rewards mattered more than hypothetical ones in choice tasks *[abstract]* ([PubMed 27722208](https://www.pubmed.gov/27722208/); [doi:10.1177/1087054718772138](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054718772138)). Those studies test reward timing, not points or badges. The notes also float cumulative totals that cannot go down. That is inference with no study behind it, and it runs against the module contract's rule for the day log ("describe what happened; count nothing") and against ADR-038; the evidence is not strong enough to reopen either. A commercial fact sits beside all this: several of the most loved apps in the category run on reward loops (Finch's pet, Focus Friend's knitting bean, Joon's coins), so a product without one departs from the category and has to find its warmth in tone, colour and craft.

### The one randomised test of reminders in adults with ADHD was null

In a micro-randomised trial inside a self-guided programme for 109 adults with ADHD, SMS reminders "did not show an effect" on module completion, logins or practice *[abstract]* ([Frontiers in Digital Health](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.821031/full)). In a general workplace-wellbeing app a tailored push made engagement in the next 24 hours 3.9% more likely *[abstract]* ([JMIR mHealth](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123/)). Students reported more inattention in a week with phone alerts on than off *[abstract; design details recalled by the note-taker]* ([Kushlev et al.](https://interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-CHI16.pdf)). Clinic-run text-message programmes are associated with higher adherence to prescribed treatment in non-randomised comparisons whose reported figures disagree across summaries *[secondary]* ([CHADD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/text-support-for-adhd/)); they are a clinic's service, need a server, and nothing here proposes that the app remind anyone about a dose. No study measures habituation or alert fatigue in ADHD; "ADHD brains habituate faster" appears only on blogs with no named study. An app that never nudges is not missing an evidenced feature.

A different thing is wanted, and the research separates it cleanly. People ask for a warning before a timer runs out *[lived, one store reviewer]* ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.llamaapp)), paying Tiimo users objected when timed routines with notifications became a checklist *[lived, via an aggregator]* ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1480220328/tiimo-visual-daily-planner/reviews)), and Llama Life is criticised because its sound stops when the person leaves the app and there is no lock-screen countdown *[secondary]* ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.llamaapp)). W3C's guidance draws the same line: interruptions and reminders should be under the person's control "unless they are started by the user", and reminders are listed as a support the person sets *[abstract]* ([W3C pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o5p01-minimal-interruptions)). A timer the person started, signalling its own end when the phone is in a pocket, is a tool finishing its job. A message sent to bring somebody back, or to report a lapse, is the thing with null evidence and a displayed break inside it. Apple repeatedly singles out widgets and Live Activities as the surface for glancing without opening the app *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Tiimo](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo)). The notes did not research what each platform allows a device-only app to schedule locally, and their feasibility table was written for a web app, so that check is outstanding.

### Price is a quit reason, and the complaints are specific

Decision 3 permits paid extensions above the original scope. One r/ADHD commenter calls most paid ADHD apps "a bit of a scam" and uses free notes and calendar apps *[lived, via a mirror]* ([r/ADHD](https://libreddit.0x0c.link/r/ADHD/comments/1ervz6i/what_adhd_apps_do_you_use/li2s1x0)), but the recurring complaints in this category are not about paying as such. They are about auto-renewal after a trial in a product for people who "will forget about it" *[lived]* ([Trustpilot: Inflow](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/getinflow.io?page=5)), a free tier narrowed after the fact *[secondary, competitor comparison pages]* ([Unanswered: Cozi pricing](https://unanswered.io/guide/cozi-app-pricing-free-vs-paid)), and several inconsistent prices for one app *[secondary]* ([Cool Curation](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)). The pattern people describe as fair is a daily core that works free with no account, payment for depth or cosmetics, and either a small annual price or a one-time unlock. No vendor pricing page was opened, every price in the notes is second-hand, and several conflict. Among open-source apps on the stores, KeePassium describes itself as "a commercial open source app" with an optional premium tier, and declines outside code contributions because of the conflict between its GPL licence and the App Store *[read]* ([GitHub: KeePassium](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium)); that is direct support for the contributor agreement in decision 2. Decision 3's three rules do not mention trials or renewal. On this evidence a fourth belongs with them: no trial that converts silently, and a one-time unlock offered wherever a subscription is.

## Family tools help the household, and the parent's follow-through is the weak link

### Parent training improves parenting and conduct, not blinded ADHD ratings

The sentence already in `04-family-space.md` matches the abstracts as retrieved. Behavioural parent training improves positive parenting (SMD 0.63), reduces negative parenting (0.43) and reduces conduct problems (0.31) on probably blinded measures, with no significant blinded effect on ADHD symptoms *[abstract]* ([PubMed 25062591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25062591/)); the unblinded symptom effect of about 0.40 falls to 0.02 with blinded raters *[secondary]* ([Daley et al. 2018](https://www.zentrales-adhs-netz.de/fileadmin/redakteure/zentrales-adhs-netz/Ueber-das-Netz/Grundlagen/Daley_2018_Practicioner_review_Behavioral_interventions.pdf)). At about five months' follow-up a later meta-analysis reports small-to-moderate effects with no difference between masked and unmasked ratings *[abstract]* ([PubMed 37720584](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37720584/)). An individual-participant analysis reads the benefit as "protective rather than ameliorative": things were associated with not getting worse, particularly for single parents and children with more conduct problems *[abstract]* ([Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/5275298)).

Two component findings bear directly on the build. Antecedent techniques, the family that routines, visual schedules, first/then and clear single instructions belong to, are associated with better parental sense of competence and mental health, and reinforcement techniques with less negative parenting *[abstract; exploratory meta-regression]* ([PubMed 34224837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224837/)); a microtrial with parents of 92 children found two sessions of either kind improved daily parent-rated behaviour, with antecedent techniques mattering most for inattention *[abstract; parent-rated, so unblinded]* ([doi:10.1080/15374416.2021.1955368](https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2021.1955368)). And a higher dose of psychoeducation was associated with worse parental outcomes in the same meta-regression, which argues for short Library entries that never stand between a parent and a tool. That is a dose association, not a trial of short text against long.

The moderator that matters most is one the app cannot supply. In the largest relevant trial, web-assisted self-help for parents of 431 children reduced blinded clinician-rated behaviour problems "only when combined with additional support" by telephone *[abstract; the repository record was not confirmed as the efficacy paper]* ([Cologne repository](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/79445/)). Attrition in technology-assisted parenting programmes is reported at 30% to 50% and higher without a practitioner *[secondary]* ([doi:10.1177/20552076231161963](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076231161963)). The closest analogue to the Family space, a UK parenting app trialled with families waiting for assessment, had finished data collection by March 2025 and no outcome paper was found as of 14 November 2026 ([KCL project page](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/the-optima-trial-evaluating-the-clinical-and-cost-effectiveness-of-the-steps-parenting-app)). The honest position for the Library is that Adnotia offers the tools a parent-training programme teaches, not the programme, and that no retrieved trial shows unsupported self-help tools changing blinded child outcomes in ADHD.

### The aids on the child surface have the least direct evidence

The aids differ sharply in what stands behind them. A brief behavioural sleep intervention delivered through paediatric practices, tested in 244 children, produced a modest fall in parent-reported ADHD symptoms at three months (effect size −0.3) and improved teacher-reported behaviour *[abstract]* ([PubMed 25646809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25646809/)). School daily report cards show a large effect on directly observed behaviour (g = 1.05, with very high heterogeneity) and a small one on teacher-rated symptoms (g = 0.36) *[abstract]* ([PubMed 29135352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29135352/)); the school runs them, and the existing design, which explains them and helps a parent ask, fits. Organisational skills programmes for roughly ages 8 to 14 have RCT support *[secondary]* ([CHOP summary](https://chop.edu/sites/default/files/adhd-exec-8-research-based-interventions-for-otmp-child-focused.pdf)), which a checklist tool borrows in content only. By contrast, no trial or review of visual schedules, first/then boards or visual timers in ADHD was returned by a targeted search. Their evidence is single-case research in autism plus one two-week uncontrolled study of a routine app with 13 children *[abstract]* ([CHI 2016](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1485796/1/Sonne.CHI.2016.pdf)). No trial of a home or app reward chart as a stand-alone intervention was found; charts are supported only as the reinforcement component of a taught programme.

Under the rubric's own wording that makes the timer, schedule and first/then board "a straightforward implementation of a technique used inside a Tier A protocol", which is the Tier C definition, while the current Family table groups them at Tier A. Whether the Library should separate the technique from the specific aid is a tier question for a person other than the author. The observation log was not covered by any of the notes.

Parents' accounts of reward charts and reward apps are consistent, and all of it is forum and review-site material: novelty fades in weeks, a reward paid on Friday is too far away, and the commonest failure is the adults not following through *[lived; quotations were not tied to individual pages]* ([HealthUnlocked](https://healthunlocked.com/adhd-parents/posts/148519900/behavior-chart)). The reported fixes are few goals, a reward close to the behaviour and an easy reset, which fit a chart that can be awarded in one tap at the moment and restarted with no record of the gap. Two findings press on the chart's rules and are for a person to decide, not for the brief to settle quietly. "Parent forgets to award" invites a prompt when a routine ends, which would be the app reminding about points on its own initiative, ruled out in `02-evidence-rubric.md`. And one meta-regression associates teaching parents to use negative consequences with larger effects on behaviour problems *[abstract; author attribution uncertain]* ([UvA repository](https://dare.uva.nl/id/0ed6c8e4-7814-4a9b-98fb-698405d601ae)), where the chart is positive-only; that finding concerns consequences taught inside a clinician-led programme, not removing points in an app. A third finding offers a way round the first: families in a co-design study wanted prompts "to establish intentional family time for reviewing data together" *[abstract]* ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/86x7t804)), and a parent-started "look at the chart together" moment is parent-initiated, so it sits inside the rules as written.

### Parental ADHD is the evidence for joining the two sides

In one German clinical sample, 41.3% of mothers and 51.0% of fathers of children with ADHD had ADHD themselves *[abstract; a single sample, no meta-analytic estimate retrieved]* ([PMC4780663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780663)). Mothers' ADHD symptoms were associated with inconsistent discipline, and "these associations were mediated by home chaos" *[abstract; cross-sectional]* ([PMC2864040](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864040)). In an early trial, children of mothers with high ADHD symptoms "displayed no improvement" after parent training *[abstract]* ([Southampton record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/18276)). **Parental ADHD symptoms predict lower between-session practice but not attendance** *[abstract]* ([PMC7567125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7567125)): the realistic failure is the parent who opens the tool and for whom the practice between uses does not happen. A 2026 trial of 120 parents with ADHD is reported as finding faster, small-to-moderate improvements in parent and child when the parent's own ADHD was addressed before parent training *[secondary; full text and limitations unread]* ([Psychiatric News](https://alert.psychnews.org/treating-parental-adhd-can-help-children-with-adhd)). That arm was the parent's own clinical treatment, which the app neither delivers nor advises on. The only point that transfers is that the parent's own functioning comes first.

This is real support for decision 8, and it also limits it. The evidence argues for making an adult's own tools easy to reach from the Family side and for designing every Family tool for a parent who will not be consistent: usable on the spot, nothing to remember later, nothing to catch up on after a missed week. It does not show that merging the two helps, because no product or study was found that interleaves a parent's routine with a child's or pairs an adult's ADHD tools with a child's in one app. The products point the other way: Brili shipped a separate adult-oriented app *[secondary]* ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1516036620)), and Tiimo began as a tool for children and is now used mostly by adults *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Tiimo](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo)). One interface rarely serves both. A shared engine with two distinct surfaces is the defensible reading of "better than two sealed spaces".

In practice the join that the evidence carries is small and specific. A task a parent captures for a child's sake ("pack the PE kit") is an item in the parent's own list and needs no second entry. A routine is the same kind of object whether it is the adult's morning or a child's, built with the same tool. The parent's own tools sit one step from the Family side. Nothing flows the other way: a child on the handed-over surface sees only the parent-authored schedule, timer, first/then board and chart, and nothing of the adult's record. Medication takes no part in any of this, since the adult's medication section is linked to nothing under decision 7 and no child medication is recorded anywhere. A practical constraint follows from the hardware and is this report's inference: a phone handed to a child is not in the parent's hand, so the parent's own tools are unavailable for as long as the child surface is in use. That, and the advice repeated across parent-facing sources that routines be few, visible and posted so nobody relies on memory *[lived]* ([Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/adhd-parenting.html)), make the printed schedule a first-class output of the Family side and a reason the kept report engine matters there.

### One phone and a parent code is the only model that needs no account

Existing products use three architectures: two linked apps with parent verification of each step (Joon), a dedicated locked child device (Goally), and one app with a parent mode behind a code and a child mode (Brili) *[lived and vendor, from search summaries]* ([Brili on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brili-routines-visual-time-r/id952895165); [Choosing Therapy on Joon](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/joon-app-review/)). Only the third works with no account, no second device and no network, and it is what the first build already has. The complaints parents make about the others (subscription price, billing after cancelling, sync failures, hardware cost) are things Adnotia does not have. One children's chore app asks only for "a nickname and age" *[read]* ([Apple Developer: S'moresUp](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=h8wvx2p9)), the same minimum the Family space already keeps. What Apple praises on child-facing surfaces is quiet: "interactions that require no reading", a three-colour palette, nothing "loud and overstimulating" *[read]* ([Apple Design Awards 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/); [Pok Pok](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=5bcex7xf)).

For 12 to 17 the evidence favours tools the teenager helps set up and then runs. A parent-teen collaborative programme worked in university trials, showed no overall advantage over usual care in a community trial of 278, and its contracting gains were not maintained at six months *[abstract; which hosted PDF belongs to which paper was inferred]* ([community trial](https://self-regulationlab.fiu.edu/publications-and-presentations/nihms-1645154.pdf); [earlier trial](https://self-regulationlab.fiu.edu/publications-and-presentations/2016-18388-001.pdf)). A study of a watch-and-phone system for children and parents found that dividing roles across devices "interfere[d] with developing independence" *[abstract]* ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10540812)). The design response is a plan built together and handed over in a mode that is less of a kiosk than a six-year-old's, quick to revisit with no record of having lapsed. A teenager ticking off their own plan is tool use. A teenager rating their own symptoms is self-report screening and stays out. Anything a teenager records about themselves reaches the open question in `04-family-space.md` about a child's own data, which is a stop-and-ask item.

### Store rules for children now apply, which the notes assumed they would not

The family notes reason that Adnotia is a browser app and that store rules apply only if it is ever listed. ADR-043 lists it, so that inference is overtaken and the points it set aside become live. All of it is second-hand: the live Apple guideline, the ICO's text and Google's policy page were not read. Apple's Kids Category restricts advertising, analytics and data sharing *[secondary]* ([Apple developer news, 2019](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=06032019j)); an app addressed to parents, whose child surface is reached only through a parent, looks like a fit for an adult audience outside that category, and whether a handed-over child mode changes a store's view is decided case by case. Google Play requires a declared target audience and says an app not aimed at children "should not unintentionally appeal to them", with imagery counted in that assessment *[search extract; the page was not read in full]* ([Google Play Families policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)). The amended COPPA rule now counts marketing materials among the factors for "directed to children" *[search extract and law-firm summaries]* ([Federal Register notice](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FTC-2024-0003-0281/content.htm)), and store screenshots of the child surface are marketing material. Texas's app-store age law, enforceable since July 2026 on the reporting retrieved, has stores pass an age category to developers and has developers assign an age rating *[secondary; sources conflict on timing]* ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2026/07/us-supreme-court-declines-request-block-texas-app-age-verification-law/414615/)). With nothing collected there is still no operator in COPPA's sense, but each of these belongs on the list for the regulatory review ADR-043 says has to be redone, alongside the store category.

## An autism extension inherits a fixed frame, not the ADHD tiers

The autism material is brief by instruction and should not be used to scope an extension without a dedicated pass. Four things are worth holding now, and the second is cheap to build in from the start and expensive to retrofit.

First, tiers do not transfer, and here the usual direction of borrowing reverses. Visual supports meet evidence-based-practice criteria in autism on 104 single-case and 2 group-design studies *[secondary]* ([AFIRM brief](https://afirm-modules.fpg.unc.edu/Visual-Supports/content/assets/raScOE/Visual%20Supports%20Brief%20Packet%2C%20Sam%20%26%20AFIRM%20Team%2C%20Updated%202024.pdf)), so the schedule, first/then board and timer would arrive in an autism extension with more direct evidence than they have for ADHD. A brief behavioural sleep intervention has trial evidence in autistic children that fades, from g = 0.64 at three months to 0.25 at twelve *[abstract]* ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-022-05809-3)). Parent training for disruptive behaviour in young autistic children has a 180-child RCT against an active comparison, in which a blinded clinician rated more children improved (68.5% against 39.6%) while the parent-rated gains fell short of the trial's own threshold for clinical importance *[abstract, from a text extract]* ([Bearss et al. 2015](https://reachfamilies.umn.edu/sites/default/files/rdoc/Bearss_2015_0.pdf)). The rubric already says tiers are assessed within the population they apply to; an extension needs its own table, and the rebuilt Library needs a shape in which one tool can carry a different tier for each population it serves (inference).

Second, interface change costs autistic users more. In a small study (11 autistic and 10 control participants) cumulative small changes to a calendar interface lowered usability and comfort scores for the autistic group *[abstract]* ([Kent repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/110913)), and in a qualitative study of ten autistic users an emotionally neutral, predictable design was reported as supporting self-regulation *[abstract]* ([Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1637008/full)). Common ADHD-app patterns pull the other way: rotating content and redesigns to counter fading novelty, celebration effects, plans that reshuffle themselves, warm mascots, urgent nudges. The resolution in first-person AuDHD accounts is a split, not a midpoint: a fixed frame (same place, same order, same look) with variety confined to content the person chooses, in their phrase "anchors, not schedules" *[lived; which writer said what is unverified]* ([Late Bloomers podcast](https://audioboom.com/posts/8868868)). A layout that does not move, no motion or sound by default, effects that can be switched off before they first fire, and a plan that never rearranges itself are already aligned with that evidence, and none of the ADHD sources asked for the interface itself to keep changing. Tiimo's founders describe designing for one group and finding the result universal *[read]* ([Apple Developer: Tiimo](https://developer.apple.com/articles/tiimo)).

Third, energy frameworks (spoon theory, energy accounting) are widely used in autistic self-description *[lived]* ([Neurodivergent Insights](https://neurodivergentinsights.com/the-neurodivergent-spoon-drawer-spoon-theory-for-adhders-and-autists/)), and an energy log works offline with no scoring. Any feature that interpreted the log, predicting burnout or flagging risk, would cross the standing rule against detecting risk from data. The same standing rules would bind an extension as bind the Family space: no diagnosis, and no self-report screening of a child.

Fourth, the important question was not answered. Where recommendations differ between autism and ADHD (reward- and praise-based approaches, demand-avoidant profiles, sensory aspects of timers, literal wording on a first/then board) nothing was retrieved, and nothing was found on shutdown or meltdown plans, communication cards or sensory tracking as praised tools.

A short reading list carries most of the weight, and each item on it should be read in full before the brief quotes it, in this order. The network meta-analysis of adult treatments (PMID 39701638), for which rater its non-drug estimates belong to. The component network meta-analysis (PMC11683884), for whether its trials are adults-only. The 2026 trial in parents with ADHD (doi:10.1111/jcpp.70222), for its primary analysis and limits. The body-doubling survey (doi:10.1145/3689648), whose figures reach this report through summaries that disagree. The streak studies (doi:10.1093/jcr/ucac029) and the reminder trial in adults with ADHD, because the guilt rule and the notification decision will cite them. The Safren and Solanto manuals, before any tool is attributed to a named component. The OPTIMA result when it is published. Apple's live review guidelines and Google's Families policy. And NICE NG87 at nice.org.uk, because no guideline wording in this report was read at source.

Several note-level judgements were written for the browser app and need redoing for a phone: the table of what works offline (it treats notifications and distraction blocking as unavailable to a web app), the statement that store rules do not apply, and the remark that an app cannot lock a phone. What each platform lets a device-only app schedule, pin or block was not researched. Details the note-takers supplied from memory (author lists, some DOIs, session-level contents of the two manuals, the format of the child sleep intervention) are not used here as findings and need confirming before any of them reaches a Library entry.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question the brief has to answer. It was framed as which tools to build and how they should look; the evidence says the tools worth building are few and plain, that their trial support belongs to a taught programme the app does not deliver, and that the look is a matter of identity and craft on which research is silent. What research does speak to is the moment that decides whether somebody stays: what they see when they open the app with something to do, and what they see when they come back after not opening it. A single item that can be captured in one step, started without planning and found again without an overdue pile answers both, and it is the one design that the trial evidence, the accessibility guidance and the lived accounts all point at from different directions.

It also shows where honesty will cost something. The category's best-loved products run on reward loops, cloud models and nudges, and the strongest lived-experience signal is for working beside another person; Adnotia can offer none of those. Its distinct position is a combination that no product in the retrieved material holds together: no account, nothing collected, tiers that say what is and is not tested, and a way back in that shows no gap. Before any of this is printed in a Library entry, the reading list above needs working through, starting with the network meta-analysis on self-ratings and the 2026 trial in parents, because the two claims that most shape the brief each rest on a summary of a paper nobody on this project has opened.
