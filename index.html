Adnotia

The days between appointments are the ones you forget.

Adnotia is a notebook for ADHD. You write down how a day went, in about a minute. It keeps those days, and turns a fortnight of them into one page you can hand to whoever you are seeing.

Open Adnotia Free. No account. Nothing to install.

Nothing leaves your phone. There is no server to send it to.

No account, no email, no sign-up. Open it and start.

Free, and open source. You can read every line of it.

What it does

A minute at the end of the day

A short check-in built from the tools you turned on, and nothing from the ones you did not. Miss a day and it says a day is missing, which is all it says.

Tools for when you are stuck

Breaking something down, working out how long it will really take, a few minutes of sitting still, a plan for the morning. Each says what the evidence for it is, and how strong that evidence is.

One page to take with you

Your record, laid out as a single sheet: what you took, what you noticed, how the days went, and how complete the record is. You read it before anybody else does.

What it will not do

  • It will not tell you whether you have ADHD. That is a clinician's job, over time.
  • It will not recommend a dose, or tell you whether to take one today.
  • It will not score you, rank you, or judge how honest you are being.
  • It has no streaks, no badges and no points. Nothing here is trying to keep you.
  • It will not send your record anywhere, in any form, ever.

Each of those is written into the project's scope document rather than into a promise, and the source is public so you can check.

For parents, too

A separate space for a child you care for: a dated log of what you have actually noticed, at home and at school, and the parenting tools with the best evidence behind them — routines, a first-and-then board, and a star chart you run yourself. There is a small screen you can hand over, with a timer and today's plan on it and no way back out without your code.

Open Adnotia

Adnotia is a record you keep, not a medical device, and not a substitute for a clinician. If things are bad right now, the app carries crisis numbers on every screen.
